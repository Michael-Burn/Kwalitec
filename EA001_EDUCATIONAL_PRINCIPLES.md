## EP-01 — Educational Leverage

### Purpose

Maximise learning value per scarce study hour by directing the student to the highest-value next action, then multiplying the value of authoritative materials (CMP, past papers, notes).

### Educational reasoning

Professional candidates rarely fail from lack of material. They fail from suboptimal decisions under time pressure. Guidance that points into the CMP at the right topic, with the right intent, produces more competence than duplicating textbook content inside the product (`GUIDANCE_OVER_CONTENT.md`).

### Student impact

The student spends less time deciding and more time studying what moves exam readiness. Every CMP hour becomes intentional.

### Implementation implications

- Own decisions and evidence; amplify CMP content — do not replace it.  
- Mission and Session design must create leverage (focus questions, recall, checks), not content inventory.  
- Product pressure to “ship more content” is rejected when guidance quality is the bottleneck (EV-001).

### Examples

- Mission: “Open CMP Chapter on GLMs; extract the exponential-family form for one response; return for a 5-minute recall check.”  
- Revision: “Re-work yesterday’s weak link-function example — not re-read the whole chapter.”

### Anti-patterns

- Pasting CMP paragraphs into episodes as if Kwalitec were the textbook.  
- Missions that only say “Study topic 4.2” with no leverage instruction.  
- Building a second parallel course inside Kwalitec to avoid opening the CMP.

## EP-02 — Educational Continuity

### Purpose

Preserve a continuous educational story across days, sessions, plans, and surfaces so the student never experiences erased progress, contradictory status, or a journey that does not follow from yesterday.

### Educational reasoning

Trust collapses when History shows zero sittings while Home claims 80% complete, or when Remaining Topics includes garbage nodes (EV-001 TB-003, TB-005). Continuity is not nostalgia — it is the condition for long-horizon exam preparation (Educational Continuity Standard; Constitution II §1.8).

### Student impact

The student feels remembered. Today’s work bridges from prior learning and hands off to tomorrow. Plan changes do not wipe rightful history.

### Implementation implications

- Tomorrow Preview and Mission narrative must cite real prior/next topics when known.  
- Progress, History, Revision, Journey, and Decision Journal must project One Educational Truth.  
- Study Plan deletion must not destroy Twin/evidence/coverage history.  
- Non-syllabus contaminants must never enter the student pathway.

### Examples

- “Yesterday you finished linear models; today extend to GLMs via the exponential family.”  
- Tomorrow Preview: “Next: 5.1 Bayesian foundations — builds on today’s likelihood thinking.”

### Anti-patterns

- Identical “highest-value next step” boilerplate for every day (EV-001 TB-004).  
- Completed topics with all learning objectives “Not started” (TB-006).  
- Address or publisher metadata as a Future topic (TB-003).  
- Silent topic thrashing for novelty.

## EP-03 — Deliberate Study

### Purpose

Replace passive exposure with intentional study: a clear objective, a defined method, a bounded duration, and a check that learning occurred.

### Educational reasoning

Time spent is not learning (Constitution VIII). Deliberate practice — goal, focus, feedback — outperforms rereading. Kwalitec’s Session philosophy exists to enforce deliberation around CMP work.

### Student impact

The student knows what “done” means for today’s Session and leaves with evidence of attempt, not only a checkbox.

### Implementation implications

- Every Session declares objective, structure, and success criteria before Begin.  
- Stages advance only when the student completes required cognitive work.  
- Empty reading shells are forbidden (TB-007).  
- Duration claims must be honest relative to depth (EV-001 Phase 5).

### Examples

- Before Reading: three focus questions.  
- After Reading: closed-book explain-one-idea in two minutes.  
- Practice: one exam-style short question with feedback.

### Anti-patterns

- “Read the material” with no material pointer and no check.  
- Session stuck after answer with no advance (TB-008).  
- Readiness ±3% theatre instead of educational success criteria.

## EP-04 — Guided Reading

### Purpose

Structure how the student engages CMP (or authorised materials) so reading is directed, selective, and accountable — not open-ended wandering.

### Educational reasoning

Expert tutors rarely say “read the chapter.” They say what to look for, which example to work, and what to ignore today. Guided Reading is Kwalitec’s distinctive teaching move while CMP remains content authority.

### Student impact

Reading time compresses. The student exits with specific notes and residual questions, ready for recall and practice.

### Implementation implications

- Episodes must name CMP locus (chapter/section/LO) when known.  
- Provide focus prompts, annotation tasks, and “stop conditions.”  
- Never paste the CMP chapter into the episode.  
- Refuse to open Guided Reading if topic or material locus cannot be resolved — honest error, not “Today’s topic.”

### Examples

- “In CMP §4.2, find the definition of the linear predictor. Write it in your own words.”  
- “Work Example 3; pause before the solution; attempt the middle algebra yourself.”

### Anti-patterns

- Placeholder “Read material for Today’s topic.”  
- Full CMP dump in the Learning Episode (V1 criterion E1).  
- Guided Reading that never returns the student to Kwalitec for recall.

## EP-05 — Active Recall

### Purpose

Require retrieval of knowledge without notes before the Session closes, so exposure is stress-tested.

### Educational reasoning

Retrieval practice outperforms restudy for durable memory. Active Recall is how Kwalitec converts CMP reading into Educational Evidence candidates (Constitution ranks B–D depending on instrument).

### Student impact

The student discovers gaps early — while correction is cheap — instead of at the exam.

### Implementation implications

- Every first-pass Session includes at least one closed-book or reduced-cue recall moment.  
- Checkpoints and practice items must be topic-specific.  
- Self-report alone must not raise Estimated Mastery (Constitution V).

### Examples

- “Close the CMP. Explain the canonical link for a Bernoulli response in one minute.”  
- Short numeric check with immediate feedback.

### Anti-patterns

- Reflection textarea as the only “activity” with no recall demand.  
- Treating reading completion as proof of retention (Constitution VIII §4).  
- Generic prompts reusable across all topics without substitution.

## EP-06 — Progressive Mastery

### Purpose

Build competence over multiple sessions and evidence events; never confuse first-pass coverage with mastery.

### Educational reasoning

Topics often require N sessions (V2 P1). Mastery is estimated from accumulated evidence, never self-certified (Constitution Articles III–V). Progressive Mastery protects students from false confidence — a leading cause of exam failure.

### Student impact

The student understands that finishing today’s Mission means Study Progress, not “I know this forever.” Confidence grows with warrant.

### Implementation implications

- Language: Completed studying ≠ Mastered.  
- Estimated Knowledge / Mastery remain Twin-owned and evidence-gated.  
- Revision and practice return to topics without erasing first-pass history.  
- Success criteria are countable skills, not readiness-point theatre.

### Examples

- Day 1: Guided Reading + recall on GLM setup.  
- Day 2: Worked deviance example + practice.  
- Later: Spaced revision when Twin signals decay.

### Anti-patterns

- “Topic mastered” after one Session.  
- 80% coverage with empty Revision and zero History (TB-005).  
- LO status “Not started” under Completed topics (TB-006).

## EP-07 — Reflection

### Purpose

Close every Session with student-authored insight that names clarity, confusion, and carry-forward — producing soft evidence and continuity inputs.

### Educational reasoning

Reflection converts experience into metacognition. Tutors ask “what clicked?” because it surfaces misconceptions and sets tomorrow’s brief. System-fabricated reflection is educational fraud (V2 P5).

### Student impact

The student leaves with an honest self-account and a bridge to tomorrow — not a silent dismiss.

### Implementation implications

- Reflection is part of Session lifecycle, not optional product garnish.  
- Prompts must bind to today’s real topic and objective.  
- Deferred capture, if ever allowed, must be explicit and time-bounded.  
- Soft evidence only — never sole path to high Estimated Mastery.

### Examples

- “Which GLM idea is still fuzzy: mean function, link, or variance function?”  
- “What will you re-check tomorrow before Bayesian work?”

### Anti-patterns

- Generic “What is clearer about Today’s topic?”  
- Closing Session as complete with no reflection path.  
- Auto-generated reflection presented as the student’s words.

## EP-08 — Exam Focus

### Purpose

Orient every educational artefact toward examination performance under syllabus weights and sitting date — not toward content completion theatre or engagement metrics.

### Educational reasoning

Kwalitec’s north star is materially higher pass probability for consistent users (Vision 2030). Study that ignores weights, timing, and assessable skills wastes scarce hours.

### Student impact

The student always knows how today’s work serves the sitting — without false pass guarantees (Blueprint product promise).

### Implementation implications

- Mission context cites exam-relevant skill or weight when known.  
- Practice prefers assessable forms (explain, calculate, interpret) over trivia.  
- Revision intensifies as the sitting approaches under authorised modes.  
- Never promise guaranteed passes.

### Examples

- “Examiners often ask you to choose and justify a link function — practise that justification today.”  
- Revision priority: high-weight weak evidence over comfort topics.

### Anti-patterns

- Optimising for streaks, DAU, or time-on-site as success.  
- Coverage celebration that contradicts practice memory.  
- Opaque “~3% readiness movement” as the only expected outcome.

## EP-09 — Tutor Voice

### Purpose

Speak as a calm, specific professional tutor (Study Sensei) — never as a database export, syllabus paste, or platform manual.

### Educational reasoning

EV-001 scored Tutor-like Behaviour 1/10: placeholders and platform disclaimers destroyed primary-study trust. Students follow guidance they believe was written for a human candidate on a real topic today.

### Student impact

The student feels guided, not processed. Trust compounds longitudinally (EV-001 Phase 9 requirement).

### Implementation implications

- Real topic titles and objectives everywhere — zero “Today’s topic” placeholders.  
- Mission narrative: bridge · purpose · success · handoff (see Mission Philosophy).  
- Ban platform meta on primary surfaces (“Mission completion is study progress only…” belongs in Help, not the hero brief).  
- Explainability cites prior topic, prerequisite, or skill gap — never identical boilerplate.  
- Comply with Experience Guidelines: guide before explaining; one purpose per screen.

### Examples

- “Today we move from classical linear models to GLMs so the same machinery can handle non-normal responses.”  
- Decision Journal: “Assigned 4.2 because 4.1 coverage is complete and GLM is the next syllabus unit with exam weight on link functions.”

### Anti-patterns

- Syllabus-title-only missions (TB-002).  
- Mechanically concatenated sentences (episode audit).  
- Engineering jargon, runtime names, milestone IDs on study surfaces.  
- Identical rationale stamps across days (TB-004).

## EP-10 — One Educational Truth

### Purpose

Ensure that for any student moment, Home, Session, History, Revision, Journey, Readiness, and Decision Journal tell the same educational story.

### Educational reasoning

Dual truths are educationally worse than simple honesty (Constitution; Vision engineering principles). Analytics and presentation must project from Twin / Evidence / Educational State authorities — never invent a parallel brain.

### Student impact

The student can trust the scoreboard. Confidence survives week after week.

### Implementation implications

- Topic completion requires completed sittings or an explicit disclosed waiver.  
- Journal outcomes must align with Home assignment.  
- Parent/child curriculum status must roll up coherently.  
- Metrics must not invent mastery or next-action outside authorities.  
- Quality gates fail any artefact that would create a second story.

### Examples

- If Home says Mission 4.2 is current, Journal must not claim 4.2 completed without Home advancing.  
- If coverage is high, Revision must not say “Nothing to revise yet” without an explainable cold-start reason.

### Anti-patterns

- Parallel mastery stores.  
- Runtime A theatre mixed with Runtime C evidence on one path (V1 E8).  
- Placeholder sessions that claim progress without teachable work.
