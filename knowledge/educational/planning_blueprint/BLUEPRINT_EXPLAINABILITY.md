> It never invents educational reasoning or readiness claims.**

## 1. Purpose

Future students should understand the overall shape of their study journey — before any timetable exists.

If Foundation precedes Revision, if practice appears after studied topics, if buffers and recovery exist, if mocks sit near the end, or if Final Preparation freezes new first-pass work, the student must receive educational reasons they can believe.

Silent journey theatre is forbidden.

Kwalitec must explain:

1. **why each phase exists,**
2. **why it appears when it does in the journey structure,**
3. **how it contributes to exam readiness** (honest readiness — not pass prophecy).

---

## 2. Traceability Obligation (Architectural)

Every material phase and structural feature must be traceable to the Planning Decision Package, which already traces to:

| Trace link | Student-facing role |
|------------|---------------------|
| **Student Educational Profile** | “Given where you are now…” |
| **Educational Strategy** | “Under this approach…” |
| **Educational Planning Model** | “Because a lawful plan must…” |
| **Planning Decision Package** | “We decided… so your journey is structured as…” |

Internal IDs (BP-XX, BC-XX, PD-XX, ES-XX) may exist for algorithms and audits. They must not appear as student-facing jargon.

A phase or component with no package warrant is invalid — even if it would schedule conveniently.

---

## 3. Explainability Principles

1. **Structure speaks.** Phases, revision protection, practice placement, buffers, recovery, mocks, and final freeze are material.
2. **One primary educational reason** per surface — not a dump of every internal factor.
3. **Carry package speech forward.** Prefer existing PD explainability patterns; do not invent a parallel reason catalogue.
4. **Facts and estimates stay distinct.** Coverage, hours, leave, and exam anchors are plain; weakness/readiness language is estimated or suggested.
5. **Order is speakable.** Why Foundation comes before Revision is educational causality, not calendar accident.
6. **Trade-offs are spoken aloud** when priority/conflict rules forced structural choices.
7. **Internal machinery stays invisible.** No optimiser names, twin facets, score vectors, or registry IDs in student speech.
8. **Uncertainty is named** when the package used defaults or thin evidence.
9. **Advice does not commandeer Learning Mode** without disclosure.
10. **Infeasibility is first-class speech**, including at journey-structure level.
11. **Readiness language stays honest.** Structure supports exam readiness work; it does not claim the student will pass.

---

## 4. Journey-Level Four-Question Contract

For the blueprint as a whole, and for each material phase, answer:

| # | Question | Guidance |
|---|----------|----------|
| 1 | **What** is this part of your journey? | Concrete phase/mission (e.g. “building syllabus coverage in official order”) |
| 2 | **Why** does it exist educationally? | One primary reason tied to package decisions / MS001 phase law |
| 3 | **Why here in the structure?** | Causality: foundations before later topics; revision reserved before the sitting; recovery after disruption; freeze before the exam |
| 4 | **How does it help exam readiness?** | Honest contribution (coverage, retention, application, craft, freshness) — not pass probability |

Optional fifth when relevant:

| # | Question | When required |
|---|----------|---------------|
| 5 | **What changed?** | After replan, recovery insertion, strategy transition, or feasibility escalation |

## 5. Claim Types in Blueprint Speech

| Claim type | Blueprint examples | Student cue |
|------------|-------------------|-------------|
| Observed Fact | Exam sitting bound; declared weekly hours; leave dates; topics already marked studied | Plain factual language |
| Derived Fact | Coverage progress; remaining first-pass relative to reserved revision posture | Plain derived measure |
| Evidence-backed Estimate | “Estimated weaker on topic X from recent practice, so revision emphasises it” | *Estimated* / *Suggested* |
| Educational Advice | Optional denser practice windows; preferred mock placement intent | *Recommended* / *Optional* where appropriate |

Forbidden speech patterns:

- “You have mastered this phase because the blueprint includes it.”
- “You will pass if you follow this journey structure.”
- “Our scheduler scored phase priority 0.82.”
- “We shuffled phases to keep you engaged.”
- Silent swap of tonight’s mission topic “because the blueprint says so” without Learning Mode authority/disclosure.
- Presenting an infeasible sitting as a complete confident journey map.

## 6. Phase Explanation Patterns

Exact copy may evolve; educational meaning must not. Patterns carry MS004 decision speech into journey structure.

### 6.1 Foundation & Knowledge Building (BP-01)

**Why it exists:**  
“This part of your journey builds syllabus coverage in the official order so later topics rest on earlier foundations.”

**Why here:**  
“It comes first (from your current progress) because learning is cumulative — we do not skip foundations to look faster.”

**Readiness contribution:**  
“Honest coverage is necessary for exam readiness; finishing topics here records study progress — it does not by itself mean mastery.”

**Package trace:** PD-01, PD-02, PD-06.

---

### 6.2 Reinforcement & Question Practice (BP-02)

**Why it exists:**  
“Practice on what you have already studied turns coverage into usable application.”

**Why here:**  
“It sits after (or interleaved with) studied material — practising topics you have not studied yet would invent false progress.”

**Readiness contribution:**  
“Exam readiness needs application evidence, not only pages completed.”

**Package trace:** PD-05.

---

### 6.3 Consolidation Windows (BP-03)

**Why it exists:**  
“Short returns to recent topics protect memory during a long first pass.”

**Why here:**  
“These windows appear during knowledge building so retention does not collapse before revision — without turning the whole journey into revision too early.”

**Readiness contribution:**  
“Retention is part of readiness on long syllabuses.”

**Package trace:** PD-14 / D11.

---

### 6.4 Protected Revision (BP-04)

**Why it exists:**  
“Revision consolidates what you have studied as the exam approaches.”

**Why here:**  
“It is reserved as its own part of the journey so first-pass learning does not consume it.”

**Readiness contribution:**  
“Exam readiness requires consolidation time — not only finishing the syllabus once.”

**Package trace:** PD-03, PD-04, D16.

---

### 6.5 Mock Examinations (BP-05)

**Why it exists:**  
“Timed mocks rehearse exam craft, timing, and stamina.”

**Why here:**  
“They appear when enough coverage makes the exercise meaningful — and with recovery so you can learn from them.”

**Readiness contribution:**  
“Mocks inform preparation; they do not predict your result.”

**Package trace:** PD-15, PD-07.

---

### 6.6 Final Preparation (BP-06)

**Why it exists:**  
“The last stage stabilises focus and protects freshness.”

**Why here:**  
“It comes after revision emphasis so we do not expand the syllabus at the last minute and undo consolidation.”

**Readiness contribution:**  
“Steady, truthful final work supports readiness better than heroic overload.”

**Package trace:** PD-04 late posture, PD-10, PD-11.

---

### 6.7 Recovery / Replan (BP-07)

**Why it exists:**  
“When life interrupts, we rebuild a keepable path — we do not punish missed study with impossible load.”

**Why here:**  
“Recovery inserts when disruption, burnout, or mocks require it — then you re-enter the journey honestly.”

**Readiness contribution:**  
“Sustainable return preserves more readiness than burnout theatre.”

**Package trace:** PD-07, PD-08, PD-16.

---

### 6.8 Intake / feasibility (BP-00)

**Why it exists:**  
“We confirm the exam, your time, and whether this sitting can be prepared honestly before mapping the journey.”

**Why here:**  
“It comes first because a beautiful journey map over an impossible sitting would be misleading.”

**Readiness contribution:**  
“Truthful feasibility is the first act of readiness coaching.”

**Package trace:** D1, PD-12, PD-13.

## 7. Component Explanation Patterns (Short Form)

| Component | Student-facing core |
|-----------|---------------------|
| Learning block | “Study this syllabus stretch next in official order.” |
| Practice block | “Practise material you have already studied.” |
| Consolidation block | “Briefly return to recent topics so they stick.” |
| Revision block | “Consolidate studied material in the reserved revision stage.” |
| Mock block | “Rehearse exam conditions; results guide preparation, not destiny.” |
| Recovery capacity | “Lighter load so you can absorb hard work or life interruption.” |
| Buffer period | “Spare capacity for slip — real study journeys need it.” |
| Milestone | “A clear checkpoint so progress is visible.” |
| Review checkpoint | “A focused check on recent work — estimates labelled where needed.” |
| Transition point | “Your mission changes here — for example from first-pass learning to revision.” |
| Intensity envelope | “Daily study stays within a keepable range.” |

Each line must still satisfy the four-question contract when surfaced as a material feature.

## 8. Progression Explainability

When the student moves, pauses, recovers, or enters revision, explain the **change**:

| Event | Pattern sketch |
|-------|----------------|
| Advance in first-pass | “You completed lawful study on this unit, so the next unit in syllabus order is next.” |
| Interleave practice | “You have studied this material, so practice is educationally useful now.” |
| Pause | “We pause progressive load because {leave / energy / feasibility} — not as punishment.” |
| Recovery insert | “Recovery is part of the journey so you can restart without impossible catch-up.” |
| Revision entry | “From this point, the journey prioritises consolidating what you have studied.” |
| Final freeze | “We stop expanding new first-pass topics so revision and freshness stay intact.” |
| Infeasibility | “This sitting does not fit your hours without changing hours, scope, or sitting — here are lawful options.” |

Conflict and replan narration remains governed by `DECISION_EXPLAINABILITY.md` §8 and `DECISION_CONFLICT_RESOLUTION.md`.

## 9. Blueprint-Level Summary (Student-Facing)

Beyond per-phase patterns, the Planning Blueprint as a whole should support a short student-facing summary:

1. **Where you are** (Profile, plain language — inherited)
2. **Approach we are taking** (Strategy — inherited)
3. **Shape of your journey** (ordered phases in plain language)
4. **What we are protecting** (usually revision, sustainability, truth)
5. **How this supports exam readiness** (coverage → retention/application → consolidation → craft → freshness — honest chain)
6. **What would change the picture** (triggers for replan / re-blueprint)

This summary is educational coaching speech — not a dump of BP/BC/PD catalogues.

It should align with, and not contradict, the package-level summary in `DECISION_EXPLAINABILITY.md` §9.

---

## 10. Audit Checklist

Before a blueprint is considered explainable:

- [ ] Every active phase has a primary educational reason
- [ ] Each reason traces to the Planning Decision Package (and thus Profile + Strategy + Planning Model)
- [ ] “Why here” causality is speakable (order / reservation / recovery / freeze)
- [ ] Readiness contribution is honest (no pass prophecy)
- [ ] Claim types are lawful
- [ ] Feasibility outcome is explicit at journey level
- [ ] Transition into revision is explicit when PD-04 is active
- [ ] No forbidden speech patterns
- [ ] No numeric weight / score language in student speech
- [ ] No new educational reasoning beyond organising package decisions
