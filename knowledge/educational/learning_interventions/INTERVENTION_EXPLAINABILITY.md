## 1. Purpose

Students should never have to guess why Kwalitec is asking them to change how they study — or feel punished for needing the change.

Intervention explainability exists so every material recommendation answers — in plain educational language — why this response, which barrier it addresses, what improvement is hoped for, and what future evidence will show whether it worked.

Without intervention explainability:

- tips feel arbitrary or product-driven;
- diagnosis and response blur into opaque advice;
- compliance is mistaken for learning;
- escalation feels like abandonment;
- thin warrant is dressed as confident prescription.

With intervention explainability:

- the student trusts the tutor posture;
- diagnosis → response → future evidence stays visible;
- local change vs escalation is honest;
- success criteria are educational, not streak-based.

---

## 2. Traceability Obligation (Architectural)

Every material Learning Intervention recommendation must be traceable through:

| Trace link | Student-facing role |
|------------|---------------------|
| **Syllabus scope** | “For [topic / area]…” |
| **Diagnosis (or gather-evidence posture)** | “The barrier we’ve identified is…” / “It’s too early to name the barrier…” |
| **Accumulated evidence (brief)** | “Because across recent study / practice…” |
| **Intervention (response)** | “So the educational next step is…” |
| **Educational objective** | “This aims to strengthen [understanding / retrieval / application / retention / exam technique]…” |
| **Expected improvement** | “We’d hope to see…” |
| **Success evidence** | “We’ll know it’s helping when…” |
| **Boundary honesty** | “This stays within your current plan…” / “This needs a longer-term plan adjustment…” |

Internal IDs (LI-XX, LOB-XX, LO-XX, LPS-XX, EV-XX, EC-X) may exist for algorithms and audits. They must not appear as student-facing jargon.

An intervention with no diagnosis link (or no explicit LI-00 insufficient-warrant account) is invalid — even if the tip sounds motivating.

**Architectural requirement restated:**

```
Evidence → Diagnosis → Intervention → Future evidence
```

Speech must not skip or collapse these layers.

---

## 3. Explainability Principles

1. **Support over judgement.** Interventions are help — never sanctions.
2. **Four answers every time.** Why selected; what diagnosis; what improvement expected; what evidence indicates success.
3. **Keep layers distinct in speech.** Observation ≠ barrier ≠ tip ≠ proof of improvement.
4. **Facts and estimates stay distinct.** Recorded outcomes are facts of observation; interventions are recommendations; success is judged later by evidence.
5. **Prefer one primary response.** Avoid multi-tip dumps; add secondary only for honesty.
6. **Speak confidence honestly.** Provisional recommendations use provisional language; LI-00 speech is dignified.
7. **Label guidance as guidance.** Not Mission law unless Daily Coach / plan authority makes it so.
8. **Boundary honesty.** Say when escalation is needed; do not launder plan rewrites as tips.
9. **No score theatre.** No intervention priority percentages or “impact scores.”
10. **EIP-003 hierarchy.** Advice / estimate / fact claim types remain visible.
11. **Align with obstacle and progression explainability.** Intervention speech must not contradict the diagnosis narration on the same trail.
12. **Close the loop.** Mention that we will look for future evidence — not declare victory at recommendation time.

## 4. Four-Question Framework (Intervention Specialisation)

Aligned to EIP-003’s four-question explainability framework:

| Question | Intervention obligation |
|----------|-------------------------|
| **What do we know?** | Brief evidence facts that supported the diagnosis |
| **What do we estimate?** | The barrier (diagnosis) and the recommended response |
| **Why this response?** | Plain link diagnosis → intervention (cause-matched) |
| **What next?** | Concrete educational next step + what success evidence to watch for (+ escalation path if needed) |

---

## 5. Required Narrative Elements

Every material student-facing intervention explanation should include:

1. **Scope** — which syllabus area the response concerns.
2. **Supportive framing** — changing method when a barrier is named is normal expert tutoring.
3. **Diagnosis link** — the barrier in plain words — or explicit “too early to diagnose; gather evidence.”
4. **Why this intervention** — cause-matched reason in plain words.
5. **Educational objective** — what kind of learning this aims to strengthen.
6. **Expected improvement** — what getting better would feel/look like educationally.
7. **Success evidence** — what we will look for next (without numeric theatre).
8. **Guidance label** — recommendation informing study emphasis; day/plan authorities preserved.
9. **Boundary note** when relevant — local vs needs longer-term plan adjustment.

Optional when useful:

- Provisional confidence language.
- What we are *not* doing (e.g. “not more volume until the misunderstanding is corrected”).
- Secondary supporting move, briefly.

## 6. Speech Patterns by Intervention Family

Patterns are illustrative — not scripts that invent certainty.

### LI-00 — Evidence Gathering

> “For [scope], we’re seeing [symptoms], but the trail isn’t clear enough yet to name the main barrier confidently. Next we’ll [distinguishing check]. We’ll know we can choose a focused response when [what would distinguish causes].”

### Local repair / method / practice (LI-01…LI-11, LI-13, LI-14)

> “For [scope], the barrier looks like [diagnosis in plain words], based on [brief evidence]. So we’ll [intervention in plain words]. This is meant to strengthen [objective]. We’d hope to see [expected improvement]. We’ll know it’s helping when [success evidence].”

### LI-12 — Escalation

> “For [scope], we’ve tried local changes such as [brief], and [barrier] still blocks honest progress inside your current plan. Local coaching isn’t enough — we need to adjust the longer-term plan honestly. That doesn’t mean you’ve failed; it means the plan should match the educational reality.”

---

## 7. The Four Student-Facing Answers (Binding Checklist)

| Student should understand | Unlawful substitute |
|---------------------------|---------------------|
| **Why this intervention was selected** | “Because the app recommends it” / opaque score |
| **What diagnosis it addresses** | Tip with no barrier named (unless LI-00) |
| **What improvement is expected** | Vague “you’ll do better” without educational object |
| **What evidence will indicate success** | “You completed the tip” as success |

---

## 8. Claim-Type Discipline (EIP-003)

| Claim type | Intervention examples |
|------------|----------------------|
| **Fact** | “In recent practice on [scope], unaided questions were weak while notes-open work looked stronger.” |
| **Estimate** | “The leading barrier looks like weak retrieval.” / “A focused retrieval emphasis is the best next response.” |
| **Advice** | “Try reconstructing these ideas without notes in your next session.” |

Forbidden collapses:

- Advice stated as fact (“You have a retrieval deficit” without estimate posture).
- Advice stated as proof of future mastery (“Do this and you will be exam-ready”).
- Compliance stated as educational improvement (“You followed the plan, so understanding improved”).

---

## 9. Alignment with Obstacle Explainability

Obstacle explainability ends with a **next improvement step**.  
Intervention explainability **owns** that step when the diagnosis gate is open.

| If obstacle speech says… | Intervention speech must… |
|---------------------------|----------------------------|
| Named barrier + next step | Match that step to a catalogue LI meaning |
| Too early to diagnose | Use LI-00 speech — no pretended cure |
| Conflicting rivals | Distinguishing evidence plan or dual-hypothesis — not a favourite tip |
| Escalation needed | LI-12 supportive escalation language |

Contradiction between obstacle narration and intervention narration on the same trail is unlawful.

---

## 10. What Must Never Appear in Student-Facing Intervention Speech

- Internal IDs as jargon (LI-03, LOB-02, …)
- Priority scores, impact percentages, or traffic-light maths as educational proof
- Character judgements (“lazy”, “not trying”, “not cut out for this”)
- Silent plan rewrite language dressed as a tip
- Mastery / exam-readiness claims from tip compliance alone
- Daily Coach override language (“Ignore today’s mission”) without lawful day authority
- Shame for needing foundations, revision, or recovery

## 11. Surfaces (Non-Implementation)

Wherever Learning Coach intervention meaning is surfaced (future UI, coaching copy, Profile narration adjuncts):

| Surface | Constraint |
|---------|------------|
| Coach guidance / What-Why-Next | Must carry the four student-facing answers |
| Mission / session prompts | May realise LI emphasis only under Daily Coach / plan authority |
| Escalation notices | Supportive LI-12 speech; pathway named; not abandonment |
| Progress widgets | Must not treat tip completion as mastery evidence |

This document does not design those UIs.
