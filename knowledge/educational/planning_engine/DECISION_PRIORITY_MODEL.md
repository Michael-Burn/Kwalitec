---

## 1. Purpose

An expert IFoA tutor often wants two good things at once: finish the syllabus *and* protect revision; push intensity *and* protect confidence; accelerate *and* recover.

When those goods conflict, the tutor does not average them into an opaque score. The tutor names the conflict and chooses by educational law.

This Model records that choice order for Master Planner.

---

## 2. Absolute Floor (Never Overridable)

Before any objective competition:

1. **Constitutional truth & claim lawfulness** — never optimise by lying (coverage ≠ mastery; no pass guarantees; estimates labelled).
2. **Hard Planning Constraints** — prerequisites, supported-subject integrity, available-time honesty, protected revision minima, no impossible daily load.
3. **Forbidden decisions** — F1–F14 (and successors) remain absolute.

If a candidate decision violates the floor, it is invalid — not “low priority.”

---

## 3. Educational Priority Ladder

When lawful objectives conflict inside the floor, resolve in this order (**highest wins**):

| Rank | Educational priority | Planning Objectives | Tutor reading |
|------|----------------------|---------------------|---------------|
| **P1** | Feasibility & realism | O8 | Refuse fantasy packages |
| **P2** | Sustainability — burnout prevention & consistency | O4, O3 | A keepable journey beats a heroic abandoned one |
| **P3** | Protected revision capacity | O5 | Revision is not leftover scrap |
| **P4** | Honest exam readiness under the above | O1 | Readiness that survives truth |
| **P5** | Retention protection & recovery support | O2, O6 | What was studied must remain usable; interruptions must be restartable |
| **P6** | Earned confidence | O7 | Confidence from warrant and structure — not pep talk |
| **P7** | Secondary quality | O9–O13 | Clarity, craft, preference fit, low decision burden, history continuity |

This ladder specialises MS001 § Conflict Resolution Order for engine use. It does not weaken it.

## 4. Named Objective Tensions

Each tension below is resolved by educational reasoning — not by weights.

---

### 4.1 Exam readiness vs syllabus completion

**Tension:** Finishing every remaining topic as first-pass vs entering the sitting with genuine revision and application.

**Lawful resolution:**

- Prefer **honest readiness (P4)** that still obeys **feasibility (P1)** and **revision protection (P3)**.
- If full syllabus completion cannot coexist with protected revision and sustainable load, **do not erase revision** to “complete” coverage theatre.
- Defer lower-priority first-pass expansion, escalate infeasibility, or counsel sitting realism — speak the trade-off aloud.
- Strategy colouring: Exam Rescue / Late Starter privilege triage; Steady Progression refuses silent revision theft.

**Tutor sentence:** “Better an honest plan that protects revision than a complete-looking syllabus march that leaves you unprepared.”

---

### 4.2 Consistency vs intensity

**Tension:** Steady keepable rhythm vs short bursts of high density to “catch up.”

**Lawful resolution:**

- **Sustainability (P2)** outranks temporary intensity spikes as the *primary* posture.
- Temporary peaks are allowed only with compensating recovery (PD-07) and without violating daily/weekly capacity constraints.
- After missed study, prefer buffer use and scoped deferral (PD-16) over boom–bust punishment pacing.
- Strategy colouring: Steady Progression / Recovery / Confidence Restoration privilege consistency; High Performer may polish intensity *inside* the safe band.

**Tutor sentence:** “We keep a rhythm you can sustain — intensity rises only inside a safe band, never as the whole strategy.”

---

### 4.3 Recovery vs acceleration

**Tension:** Rebuild cadence after interruption vs accelerate remaining work immediately.

**Lawful resolution:**

- When Profile shows recovery need (Recovering / Returning / burnout signals), **recovery support (P5)** and **sustainability (P2)** outrank acceleration.
- Acceleration before cadence returns produces second collapse — educationally negligent.
- Once cadence is restored and feasibility rechecked, readiness ambitions may rise again under the ladder.
- Strategy colouring: ES-08 / ES-07 win over rescue heroics until warrant returns.

**Tutor sentence:** “We restart at a load you can keep; speed comes back after the rhythm does.”

---

### 4.4 Retention vs speed

**Tension:** Spaced return / consolidation vs maximum first-pass topic throughput.

**Lawful resolution:**

- On long syllabuses with rising decay risk, **retention (P5)** constrains speed — light consolidation is not optional decoration.
- Speed that produces late-stage amnesia fails **honest readiness (P4)** even if coverage ticks look advanced.
- Early Foundation Building may keep consolidation light; Knowledge Consolidation / Approach strategies raise retention posture.
- Never abandon official sequence to “space randomly” for engagement.

**Tutor sentence:** “We protect what you have already studied while we continue forward — speed without retention is false progress.”

---

### 4.5 Confidence vs workload

**Tension:** Heavy workload that “shows seriousness” vs workload the student can complete with earned confidence intact.

**Lawful resolution:**

- **Earned confidence (P6)** never overrides **feasibility (P1)** or **sustainability (P2)** — false reassurance and crushing load are both unlawful.
- Prefer completable intensity, clear milestones, and truthful narration over either pep-talk inflation or shame-driven overload.
- After mock shock or fail aftermath, Confidence Restoration may **lower** workload temporarily while keeping educational facts honest.
- Forbidden: inflate readiness estimates to soothe; punish low confidence with double load.

**Tutor sentence:** “We set work you can finish truthfully — confidence grows from kept promises, not from overload or empty reassurance.”

---

### 4.6 Preference fit vs educational law

**Tension:** Student preferences (session shape, timing, topic interest) vs prerequisites, revision protection, and feasibility.

**Lawful resolution:**

- Preferences live at **P7** — they improve adherence *within* law; they never reorder the ladder.
- Honour preferences in remaining lawful room after sequencing, revision, intensity band, and feasibility are set.
- Explain refusals when preference would break constraints.

**Tutor sentence:** “We shape sessions to how you study best — without breaking syllabus order or revision protection.”

---

### 4.7 Practice depth vs remaining first-pass

**Tension:** Practise hard on covered units vs push coverage of remaining units.

**Lawful resolution:**

- Under Practice Intensive / Weak Reinforcement with adequate runway, practice depth may rise **without** abandoning lawful sequencing of remaining first-pass elsewhere.
- Near the sitting, if practice and remaining first-pass compete for protected revision, **P3 revision** and **P1 feasibility** win — freeze unlawful expansion; practise what can still be strengthened.
- Never practise unstudied topics as if coverage existed.

**Tutor sentence:** “We practise what you have studied; we do not pretend practice replaces foundations you have not covered.”

---

## 5. Strategy Does Not Reorder the Ladder

Strategy colours *emphasis within ranks*. It does not promote a lower rank above a higher one.

| Unlawful strategy misuse | Why |
|--------------------------|-----|
| “Rescue” used to justify impossible intensity | Violates P1/P2 |
| “Foundation Building” used to consume revision | Violates P3 |
| “Confidence Restoration” used to hide infeasibility | Violates P1 and claim law |
| “High Performer” used to skip prerequisite repair when foundations are broken | Violates floor constraints |

If Profile warrants a strategy that seems to fight the ladder, **re-check strategy selection** — usually the Profile actually warrants Recovery, Late Starter, or Exam Rescue with honest triage, not a ladder violation.

## 6. Worked Priority Examples

| Situation | Winning priority | Decision posture |
|-----------|------------------|------------------|
| Student wants all remaining topics finished in two weeks; revision would vanish | P1 + P3 | Feasibility crisis; protect revision; triage or sitting counsel |
| Missed two weeks; student asks to “double everything” | P2 + P5 | Recovery allowance; buffers; scoped deferral — not punishment pace |
| Mid first-pass; forgetting earlier chapters | P5 (retention) under P4 | Raise consolidation posture; keep sequence |
| Strong coverage; fragile confidence after harsh mock | P6 within P2 | Moderate load; completable milestones; truthful feedback — no readiness inflation |
| Preference to study advanced topic “for motivation” before foundations | Floor (C7) | Refuse; explain prerequisite law |

---

## 7. What This Model Forbids

| Anti-pattern | Why forbidden |
|--------------|---------------|
| Numeric weight vectors as educational authority | Replaces speakable reasoning with opaque scores |
| Averaging conflicting objectives into a single “utility” without naming trade-offs | Hides the educational choice from the student |
| Letting engagement metrics outrank P1–P6 | Disconnects from educational meaning |
| Silent priority inversion inside scheduling code | Invents educational law downstream |
