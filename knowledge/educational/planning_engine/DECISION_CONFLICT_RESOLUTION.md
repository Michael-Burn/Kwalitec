## 1. Purpose

Even a lawful Planning Decision Package meets reality: limited hours, late starts, missed weeks, unexpected leave, repeated failure.

An expert tutor does not pretend the old package still fits. The tutor:

1. Names the conflict in educational language.
2. Re-checks Profile diagnosis.
3. Considers whether Strategy must change.
4. Re-settles decisions under the Priority Model.
5. Explains the trade-off to the student without shame.

---

## 2. Resolution Posture (Binding)

1. **Truth before optimism.** Hidden infeasibility is forbidden.
2. **No punishment pacing.** Missed study is a replan signal, not a moral debt collected in double load.
3. **Preserve lawful history.** Gaps do not erase Study Progress or invent mastery loss.
4. **Reduce load before inventing heroics.** Sustainability precedes catch-up theatre.
5. **Protect revision minima** unless the package explicitly escalates sitting-level feasibility crisis.
6. **One primary strategy at a time.** Resolve by transition if needed — not by stacking contradictory approaches.
7. **Speak the trade-off.** Students deserve to know what was deferred and why.

---

## 3. Standard Resolution Loop

Whenever a material conflict appears:

```
1. Refresh Student Educational Profile (what is true now?)
2. Re-evaluate Educational Strategy (is the approach still honest?)
3. Re-run Constraint Evaluation (what is still hard-bound?)
4. Apply Decision Priority Model (which educational good wins?)
5. Re-settle affected Educational Decisions (PD-XX)
6. Emit updated Planning Decision Package with explainability
```

Skipping to “just squeeze the calendar” is educationally unlawful.

## 4. Conflict Playbooks

---

### 4.1 Limited available time

**Conflict:** Declared or effective study capacity cannot support remaining first-pass + protected revision + recovery under current ambition.

**Tutor reading:** “The hours you have do not fit the journey as currently scoped.”

**Educational reasoning:**

1. Recompute capacity envelope (PD-12) from truthful hours, leave, and study-day pattern.
2. Apply **P1 feasibility** — do not publish a package that assumes hours the student does not have.
3. Protect **P3 revision** before packing more first-pass.
4. Adaptive responses (choose among lawful options, not all at once as fantasy):
   - Lower study intensity posture within a still-honest band (PD-06).
   - Increase triage under Late Starter / Exam Rescue strategies (PD-10, PD-13).
   - Defer low-priority consolidation density if retention risk is still acceptable (temporary).
   - Escalate sitting counsel as educational advice when even triage cannot produce honest readiness.
5. Prefer strategy transition to ES-10 or ES-09 when shortfall is structural — not silent “same strategy, impossible hours.”

**Must not:** Steal revision by default; schedule impossible daily loads; hide the shortfall.

---

### 4.2 Late syllabus completion

**Conflict:** First-pass remains materially incomplete while the sitting approaches and revision should already dominate.

**Tutor reading:** “We are late on coverage for this sitting — we must triage honestly.”

**Educational reasoning:**

1. Update Profile coverage vs runway; expect At Risk / Exam Preparation distress colouring.
2. Likely strategy transition toward **Exam Rescue (ES-09)** or confirmation of **Late Starter (ES-10)** if the journey began compressed.
3. Advance phase boundary (PD-04): freeze or tightly constrain new first-pass frontiers.
4. Protect remaining revision substance (PD-03); practise and revise what can still be strengthened (PD-05).
5. Feasibility judgement (PD-13) must be explicit — including lawful reduced ambition or sitting realism conversations.
6. Milestone repositioning (PD-09) toward triage checkpoints, not false “on track” theatre.

**Must not:** Pack remaining syllabus into the revision window silently; claim Exam Ready from calendar proximity.

---

### 4.3 Missed study

**Conflict:** Planned study did not happen for days or weeks; remaining work no longer fits the old intensity posture.

**Tutor reading:** “You missed study — we replan without punishment.”

**Educational reasoning:**

1. Record interruption in Profile recovery / consistency dimensions — without erasing lawful coverage already earned.
2. Prefer **Recovery Strategy (ES-08)** when cadence is broken; use Confidence Restoration (ES-07) when the wound is primarily trust/motivation rather than coverage emergency.
3. Apply PD-16 catch-up response under Priority Model:
   - First: use buffers (PD-08).
   - Second: reduce intensity elsewhere / temporarily defer lower-value consolidation.
   - Third: escalate feasibility if buffers are exhausted.
4. Install recovery allowance (PD-07) before any acceleration.
5. Re-judge feasibility (PD-13) after the replan — do not assume the old package still holds.

**Must not:** Double load to “pay back” missed hours (F9); invent mastery loss from the gap; shame narration.

---

### 4.4 Unexpected leave

**Conflict:** New leave, illness, or life event removes capacity that the package assumed.

**Tutor reading:** “Life took study time — we redesign around what remains.”

**Educational reasoning:**

1. Update capacity envelope and leave posture immediately (PD-12).
2. Treat as recovery-class event: protect history; reduce load; rebuild rhythm (PD-07, PD-06).
3. Recalculate revision reservation against the new usable horizon (PD-03) — if revision minima cannot hold, escalate feasibility rather than pretend.
4. Strategy: ES-08 Recovery, possibly ES-09 if the sitting is now materially endangered.
5. Explain what changed (leave dates as Observed Fact; new posture as Educational Advice).

**Must not:** Keep the pre-leave intensity as if hours still exist; convert leave into diagnostic weakness claims.

---

### 4.5 Repeated failure

**Conflict:** Previous unsuccessful attempt(s), or repeated weak mocks on covered material, colour the current sitting.

**Tutor reading:** “Past attempts tell us we need more consolidation and honesty — not faster coverage theatre.”

**Educational reasoning:**

1. Profile must carry previous-attempt history as planning-relevant diagnosis (not as identity stigma).
2. Raise risk mitigation posture (PD-10) and previous-attempt caution (MS001 D20 meaning).
3. Privilege revision/practice depth on evidenced weak areas (PD-05, PD-03, PD-14) over false coverage speed.
4. Strategy colouring often toward Weak Topic Reinforcement, Revision Intensive, Confidence Restoration, or Exam Rescue — selected from Profile, not from shame.
5. Feasibility caution rises: repeat sitters need more consolidation room, not tighter fantasy packs.
6. Confidence protection (PD-11) must stay truth-preserving — no pass guarantees to “motivate.”

**Must not:** Treat failure as licence for punishment intensity; wipe or ignore lawful prior learning; mint readiness claims from effort alone.

---

### 4.6 Burnout or adherence collapse

**Conflict:** Student cannot sustain the decided intensity; consistency collapses even without a single leave event.

**Tutor reading:** “The plan is asking more than you can keep — we reduce load before we lose the journey.”

**Educational reasoning:**

1. Sustainability (P2) wins over remaining coverage ambition.
2. Lower study intensity band posture (PD-06); add recovery allowance (PD-07); possibly transition to ES-08 / ES-07.
3. Re-check feasibility (PD-13). If even reduced load cannot finish honestly, escalate sitting-level truth.
4. Prefer fewer keepable sessions over theatrical volume.

**Must not:** Interpret collapse as laziness requiring more load; hide that the prior intensity decision failed.

---

### 4.7 Evidence shock (harsh mock / sudden weak estimates)

**Conflict:** New practice or mock evidence contradicts a previously calmer readiness posture.

**Tutor reading:** “New evidence changed the picture — we adjust emphasis without panic theatre.”

**Educational reasoning:**

1. Update Profile evidence posture; keep claim types lawful (estimates labelled).
2. Possibly transition toward Weak Topic Reinforcement or Confidence Restoration depending on whether the need is skill repair or trust repair (or both, sequenced).
3. Raise targeted practice intensity (PD-05) on *covered* weak units; protect recovery after mocks (PD-07).
4. Do not inflate or deflate into destiny language — mocks calibrate, they do not prophesy.

**Must not:** Discard the whole package for one mock without Profile re-read; convert one weak set into global mastery denial beyond warrant.

---

### 4.8 Strategy / decision contradiction

**Conflict:** Adaptive decisions in the package fight the active strategy’s refusals (e.g. Foundation Building package emitting mock-heavy revision theatre).

**Tutor reading:** “Our approach and our decisions disagree — we fix that before scheduling.”

**Educational reasoning:**

1. Treat as engine integrity failure — package is incomplete/invalid until aligned.
2. Either re-settle decisions to honour strategy privileges/refusals, or re-select strategy if Profile no longer warrants it.
3. Never “average” contradictory postures in the calendar.

## 5. Conflict Outcome Classes

Every resolved conflict should land in one of these educational outcomes:

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| **Adjusted package** | Same strategy; decisions updated within law |
| **Strategy transition + new package** | Approach changed under MS003; decisions rebuilt |
| **Feasibility crisis package** | Explicit infeasibility with lawful options (reduce scope, change hours, change sitting) |
| **Stabilisation package** | Temporary recovery/confidence posture before ambition returns |

“Do nothing and hope” is not an outcome class.

## 6. Shame & Theatre Rules

| Forbidden conflict response | Why |
|----------------------------|-----|
| Moralising missed study | Destroys trust; invents character diagnosis |
| Hidden infeasibility | Educational fraud |
| Pass/fail probability theatre | Invents certainty |
| Erasing Study Progress after leave | Violates continuity |
| Engagement gimmicks to “fix” conflict | Avoids educational truth |
