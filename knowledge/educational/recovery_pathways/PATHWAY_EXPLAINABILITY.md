## 1. Purpose

Students should never have to guess why Kwalitec chose *this kind* of recovery — or feel that pathway changes are opaque mode switches.

Pathway explainability specialises MS001 recovery explainability so every material pathway decision answers — in plain educational language — why this journey type, how it meets the disruption, what success looks like, and when ordinary progression should return.

Without pathway explainability:

- different recovery approaches feel arbitrary;
- lighter load feels like demotion;
- knowledge reinforcement feels like “you forgot everything”;
- exam-phase recovery feels like panic;
- transitions feel like failure;
- escalation feels like abandonment.

With pathway explainability:

- the student trusts the tutor posture;
- disruption → warrant → pathway → success/resume stays visible;
- pathway change and escalation are honest educational events;
- confidence is protected (RO-05).

## 3. Traceability Obligation (Architectural)

Every material pathway recommendation must be traceable through:

| Trace link | Student-facing role |
|------------|---------------------|
| **Observed disruption** | “Here’s what interrupted your study…” |
| **Why meaningful (not fluctuation)** | “This goes beyond a quiet evening because…” |
| **Why this pathway type** | “The most educationally fitting recovery approach is…” |
| **How it addresses the disruption** | “This helps by…” |
| **Evidence basis (brief)** | “Looking at recent study / return / practice…” |
| **What success looks like** | “We’ll know this is working when…” |
| **Long-term plan faithfulness** | “This keeps your overall preparation intent intact by…” |
| **Boundary honesty** | “This stays within your current plan…” / “This needs a longer-term plan adjustment…” |
| **Resume expectation** | “We expect ordinary progression to resume when…” |

Internal IDs (RP-XX, RT-XX, RS-XX, RO-XX) may exist for algorithms and audits. They must not appear as student-facing jargon.

A pathway recommendation with no disruption link — or no educational reason for *this* pathway type — is invalid.

Architectural requirement restated:

> **Recovery pathways may adapt educational execution but must not independently modify long-term planning, educational strategy, or mastery judgements — and must say so honestly when escalation is required.**

## 4. Four-Question Framework (Pathway Specialisation)

Every material pathway decision must answer EIP-003’s four questions, specialised for pathways:

| EIP-003 question | Pathway specialisation |
|------------------|------------------------|
| **What is known?** | What disruption was observed; what evidence supports the disruption profile reading |
| **What is estimated?** | Provisional judgements (e.g. likely at-risk scope, likely capacity mismatch) — labelled provisional |
| **Why this guidance?** | Why recovery; why *this pathway type*; how it addresses the disruption and serves long-term success |
| **What next?** | Concrete restorative next step + what success looks like + when normal progression is expected to resume |

Students must hear all four — especially **why this pathway** and **when resume** — not only a lighter-load or quiz instruction.

---

## 5. Required Student Understandings

Material pathway speech must make the following understandable:

### 5.1 Why this pathway was selected

- Name the educational journey type in plain language (short restart, return after a longer break, confidence-first return, knowledge reinforcement, workload rebalance, consistency rebuild, foundation rebuild, exam-period recovery).
- Link it to the disruption profile (“because the break was long…”, “because confidence is the blocker…”, “because practice suggests a refresh on…”).
- Distinguish from moral framing (“you failed”) and from other pathway types the student might fear (e.g. reinforcement ≠ “you’ve forgotten everything”).

### 5.2 How it addresses the disruption

- Tie the pathway to restorative goods: momentum, consistency, understanding protection, confidence, feasible load.
- Explicitly reject “catch up at any cost” as the story.
- Show how near-term emphasis adapts **execution** while long-term intent stays faithful (“same destination; safer route back”).

### 5.3 What success looks like

- Give educational success signs for *this* pathway (sustainable sittings return; confidence unfreezes; named topics strengthen; load feels workable; cadence sticks; foundations support later work; exam-facing jobs resume sustainably).
- Avoid fake completion theatre (“pathway 100% complete”).
- Separate success from mastery claims.

### 5.4 When normal educational progression is expected to resume

- Give educational resume criteria (sustainable rhythm returned; plan jobs workable again; pathway success signs met; remaining learning issues handed to Learning Coach if appropriate).
- Avoid false calendar promises unless plan/scheduling authority actually supports them.
- When escalation is required, explain that normal progression resumes under an updated authorised plan — not under fake local certainty.
- When pathway *changes*, explain that recovery continues under a clearer route — not that the student “failed the previous mode.”

## 6. Narrative Rules

1. **Lead with intentional recovery type, not shame.** “We’re using a gradual return after a longer break…” beats “you fell behind.”  
2. **Name the pathway educationally.** Plain journey language — not optimiser jargon or internal IDs.  
3. **Separate disruption facts from learning claims.** Missed sessions ≠ forgotten syllabus.  
4. **Label estimates.** “It looks like X may need a refresh” ≠ “you’ve lost everything.”  
5. **Keep plan speech consistent.** Align with Canonical Study Plan / Recovered posture narration.  
6. **Keep Daily Coach speech consistent.** Today’s objective remains Day Coach’s to state; pathway explains the restorative frame.  
7. **Explain transitions.** Pathway change, resume, and escalation are named events.  
8. **Escalate honestly.** “Your plan needs a proper adjustment” is lawful; silent rewrite is not.  
9. **Close with resume hope.** Every pathway message should make return to ordinary progression imaginable.

---

## 7. Language Rules

### Prefer

- “After [disruption], the most educationally fitting recovery approach is a gradual restart / confidence-first return / focused refresh / workload rebalance / consistency rebuild / foundation rebuild / exam-period recovery.”
- “This addresses the disruption by restoring [momentum / confidence / understanding safety / feasible load / steady rhythm] rather than rushing to clear the calendar.”
- “We’ll know this is working when [success signs].”
- “We’ll return to your ordinary plan rhythm when [resume criteria].”
- “We’re changing the recovery approach because [new educational reading] — same destination, clearer route.”
- “This may need a plan update so recovery stays realistic.”

### Avoid

- “You failed pathway X / dropped to recovery mode.”
- “The algorithm selected RP-04.”
- “You need to catch up everything this week.”
- “Rest restored your mastery.”
- “You’ve forgotten everything.”
- Opaque scores, streak guilt, or comparison theatre.
- Internal IDs (RP-07, RT-04, RS-05) in student-facing copy.

## 8. Pathway-Specific Speech Cues

| Pathway | Prefer saying | Avoid saying |
|---------|---------------|--------------|
| **RP-01** | “Short interruption — clean, gentle restart.” | “You’re in full recovery crisis.” |
| **RP-02** | “Longer stretch away — rebuild gradually.” | “Clear the whole backlog this week.” |
| **RP-03** | “First job is rebuilding confidence with a doable next step.” | “Stop being weak / everyone else is ahead.” |
| **RP-04** | “Reinforce topics that may have softened — based on recent practice.” | “You’ve lost the syllabus.” |
| **RP-05** | “Rebalance load so progress stays sustainable.” | “You can’t handle this subject.” |
| **RP-06** | “Rebuild a steady rhythm that sticks.” | “One more miss and you’ve failed.” |
| **RP-07** | “Rebuild foundations before pushing further.” | “Skip ahead until you’re caught up.” |
| **RP-08** | “Recover in a way that protects revision and understanding.” | “Panic-cram everything into mocks.” |

## 9. Transition Speech Contracts

### 9.1 Resume (T1)

> “Your study rhythm looks sustainable again, so we’re returning to your ordinary plan coaching. The recovery approach has done its job — this isn’t a demotion ending, and it isn’t a claim that everything is ‘mastered.’”

### 9.2 Pathway change (T2)

> “We’re adjusting the recovery approach. [Previous route] helped with [X]; the clearer next educational job is [new route] because [profile evolution / evidence]. Same preparation destination — a better restorative route.”

### 9.3 Escalation (T3)

> “Local recovery isn’t enough to keep your plan realistic. We need to update the study timetable / plan through the proper planning path so progress stays sustainable. That’s not abandonment — it’s how we protect the long-term preparation.”

### 9.4 Learning Coach handoff (T4)

> “Attendance and continuity are back on track. The remaining work is about how learning itself is progressing — we’ll focus there next, separately from recovery.”

### 9.5 Hold / gather (T5)

> “We’re not jumping to a dramatic recovery story yet. We’ll take a careful next step and learn more from how study goes.”

## 10. Claim-Type Honesty

| Claim type | Pathway explainability cue |
|------------|----------------------------|
| **Observed disruption** | “You were away / sessions were missed / availability changed…” |
| **Pathway recommendation** | “So the recovery approach we’re taking is…” |
| **Provisional knowledge risk** | “Recent practice suggests [topic] may need a refresh…” |
| **Plan / scheduling fact** | “Your plan already includes recovery capacity…” / “Your plan needs updating…” |
| **Success / resume judgement** | “It looks workable to return to ordinary rhythm when…” |
| **Mastery / readiness** | **Do not claim** from pathway progress alone |

---

## 11. Surface Contracts

Any student-facing surface that presents Recovery Pathway guidance must:

1. Answer the four specialised questions (§4).  
2. Make the four required understandings (§5) available without detective work.  
3. Remain consistent with MS001 recovery explainability and Daily Coach / Study Plan narration.  
4. Disclose pathway change, resume, or escalation when relevant (§9).  
5. Preserve claim-type honesty (§10).  
6. Avoid shame-first framing.  
7. Refuse mastery speech from rest, resumed ticks, pathway “completion,” or backlog clearance.  
8. Never present numeric pathway scores as educational proof.

## 12. Good and Bad Examples

### Good — prolonged absence → RP-02

> “You’ve had a longer stretch away from study after illness. Rather than clearing everything at once, we’ll use a gradual return with a sustainable load so you can rebuild momentum and keep the understanding you’ve already built. We’ll know this is working when consistent sittings feel workable again — and that’s when we expect to return to your ordinary plan rhythm.”

### Bad — catch-up theatre

> “You’re on the prolonged absence pathway. Double your hours until RP-02 completes.”

### Good — pathway change to knowledge reinforcement

> “You’ve restarted well. Looking at recent practice, a few earlier topics may need a focused refresh, so we’re shifting the recovery emphasis to reinforcing those areas — not because you’ve failed, but because that now best protects your long-term understanding.”

### Bad — shame transition

> “Basic restart failed. You’ve been downgraded to knowledge recovery because you’re behind.”

### Good — exam-phase recovery

> “This interruption landed during your exam-focused period. We’ll recover in a way that protects revision and understanding — not by inventing a panic cram plan. If the remaining timetable can’t stay realistic, we’ll update the plan properly rather than pretending.”

### Bad — panic cram

> “Exam phase recovery means mocks only until you’re caught up.”

### Good — escalation

> “Adjusting today’s recovery approach isn’t enough to keep your preparation realistic. We need a proper plan update. That’s how we protect the long-term journey — not a judgement on you.”

### Bad — silent rewrite

> “I’ve changed your whole plan so you can finish the pathway.” *(without Master Planner / scheduling authority or explanation)*

## 13. Authority Duties for Explainability

| Authority | Pathway explainability duty |
|-----------|------------------------------|
| **Recovery Coach** | State disruption, pathway type, how it helps, success signs, resume / transition honesty |
| **Daily Coach** | State today’s objective under the pathway frame without contradicting pathway speech |
| **Learning Coach** | Keep progression / obstacle / intervention explanations distinct from recovery pathway continuity speech |
| **Master Planner / Scheduling** | Own explanations of structural plan / timetable change after escalation |
| **Surfaces** | Never invent certainty, shame, scores, or mastery from pathway compliance |
