## 1. Purpose

An expert IFoA tutor carries a mental catalogue of *kinds* of recovery after disruption: a brief safe restart after a short break; a careful return after prolonged absence; rebuilding belief that study is possible again; reinforcing knowledge at risk after a gap; rebalancing an unsustainable load; restoring broken consistency; reconstructing fragile prerequisites; and recovering wisely when disruption lands in an exam-facing phase.

This catalogue records those educational journey types so Recovery Coach selection, transitions, and explainability share one vocabulary — while remaining consistent with the Educational Recovery Model.

---

## 2. Catalogue Principles

1. **Educational first.** Pathways come from IFoA tutoring practice and restorative learning principles — not from which feature flag or calendar trick is easiest.
2. **Journey types, not tactics.** Strategies (RS-XX) live inside pathways; pathways are not a second strategy list under new IDs.
3. **Disruption-profile linked.** Each pathway declares the disruption character it is built for.
4. **Objective-aware.** Each pathway primarily advances specific RO-01…RO-05 goods.
5. **Non-exclusive.** Disruption may stack; prefer a **primary** pathway in speech.
6. **Reversible.** Pathways are provisional; they change as evidence and recovery progress evolve.
7. **Non-punitive.** Names describe restorative routes — never character or moral deficit.
8. **Plan-faithful.** Pathways preserve authorised phase meaning and long-term intent; they do not invent unofficial syllabus routes.
9. **No fitness scores.** Qualitative matching only — never numeric pathway ranks as educational law.

---

## 3. Framework Logic (Before Labels)

Before naming a pathway, the tutor asks, in order:

| Order | Tutor question | If unclear |
|-------|----------------|------------|
| 1 | Is there a lawful recovery warrant (not temporary fluctuation)? | Do not open a pathway |
| 2 | What primarily broke — continuity length, confidence, knowledge safety, capacity, consistency, foundations, or exam-phase timing? | Gather evidence; prefer gentler short-disruption reading when thin |
| 3 | Which RO-XX goods must lead? | Do not select a pathway that optimises calendar theatre |
| 4 | Can restoration stay inside current plan envelopes? | Prefer local pathway; escalate if envelopes break |
| 5 | Is the primary problem a learning obstacle rather than disruption continuity? | Defer to Learning Coach; do not invent a recovery pathway as diagnosis substitute |

Examples such as “Prolonged Absence Recovery” are **illustrative names** for journey types produced by this logic. Binding meanings are the definitions below.

## 4. Pathway Catalogue

Each entry records: educational meaning, suitable disruption profile, primary recovery objectives, typical triggers, emphasised strategies, what it is not, and a constructive speech cue.

IDs (`RP-XX`) exist for audit and cross-reference. They must not appear as student-facing jargon.

Detailed selection cards (evidence required, success signs, when another pathway is preferable) live in `PATHWAY_SELECTION.md`. Transition behaviour lives in `PATHWAY_TRANSITIONS.md`.

### RP-01 — Short Disruption Recovery

| Aspect | Provision |
|--------|-----------|
| **Educational meaning** | A brief restorative restart after a short but meaningful interruption — restoring safe motion without opening a long recovery arc |
| **Suitable disruption profile** | Limited gap relative to the student’s rhythm; plan still largely feasible; confidence and foundations largely intact; ordinary “resume next session” is slightly insufficient but heroic catch-up is unnecessary |
| **Primary objectives** | RO-01, RO-05 (then RO-02) |
| **Typical triggers** | Mild RT-02 clusters; short RT-01 relative to Profile; RT-06 only when interruption is brief and envelopes remain honest |
| **Emphasised strategies** | RS-01, RS-07 (when authorised recovery capacity exists), light RS-06 if needed |
| **What it is not** | A label for every missed evening (that is temporary fluctuation); a substitute for prolonged-absence care; a licence to invent double-speed catch-up “because the gap was short” |
| **Speech cue** | “A short interruption — we’ll restart cleanly and gently, then return to your ordinary rhythm.” |

---

### RP-02 — Prolonged Absence Recovery

| Aspect | Provision |
|--------|-----------|
| **Educational meaning** | A deliberate re-entry journey after an extended break from study, prioritising sustainable return, confidence, and protection of prior understanding before ordinary intensity |
| **Suitable disruption profile** | Multi-day or multi-week gap (illness, emergency, travel, life event) such that immediate full-intensity resume would be unsafe or unrealistic; student is returning after meaningful absence |
| **Primary objectives** | RO-01, RO-05, then RO-02; RO-03/RO-04 as evidence warrants |
| **Typical triggers** | RT-01; often co-occurring RT-06 when plan posture has engaged recovery capacity |
| **Emphasised strategies** | RS-01, RS-06, RS-07, RS-04; RS-05 / RS-02 when evidence shows slip or fragile foundations; RS-08 when envelopes cannot hold |
| **What it is not** | Punishment backlog clearance; treating a planned rest day as prolonged absence; claiming the syllabus was “forgotten” from calendar gap alone |
| **Speech cue** | “After a longer stretch away, we’ll rebuild study gradually so you return sustainably — not by trying to clear everything at once.” |

---

### RP-03 — Confidence Rebuilding

| Aspect | Provision |
|--------|-----------|
| **Educational meaning** | A recovery journey whose primary educational job is restoring the student’s belief that return is possible and worthwhile — through supportive framing and achievable lawful next steps |
| **Suitable disruption profile** | Disruption has left shame, abandonment risk, or freeze: the student can conceptually study but does not believe restart is safe or worthwhile; confidence is the blocker to any momentum |
| **Primary objectives** | RO-05, then RO-01 |
| **Typical triggers** | RT-01 / RT-02 / RT-04 when confidence (not content alone) blocks return; may accompany other pathways as primary when freeze is dominant |
| **Emphasised strategies** | RS-04 first; then RS-01 with deliberately achievable lawful jobs; coordinate with Learning Coach confidence-calibration interventions when diagnosed — without replacing that layer |
| **What it is not** | Empty pep talk without an educational next step; comparison theatre; a cure for undiagnosed learning obstacles dressed as “motivation” |
| **Speech cue** | “The first job is rebuilding confidence with a clear, doable next step — intentional recovery, not a judgement on you.” |

---

### RP-04 — Knowledge Reinforcement

| Aspect | Provision |
|--------|-----------|
| **Educational meaning** | A recovery journey that protects and re-anchors durable knowledge after disruption by emphasising focused retrieval and reinforcement on at-risk scope — without minting mastery from rest or quiz theatre |
| **Suitable disruption profile** | Evidence suggests meaningful slip or retention risk after a gap on previously progressing scope; continuity has resumed enough that knowledge safety (not mere attendance) is the leading educational problem |
| **Primary objectives** | RO-04, RO-03 |
| **Typical triggers** | RT-04; RT-01 after meaningful absence when evidence (not calendar alone) warrants reinforcement |
| **Emphasised strategies** | RS-05; RS-02 when foundations are implicated; RS-03 for lawful emphasis among plan jobs |
| **What it is not** | Mastery restoration claims from completing a question set; random syllabus-wide quiz mode; a bypass of Learning Coach progression / intervention meaning when an obstacle is diagnosed |
| **Speech cue** | “We’ll reinforce the understanding that may have softened during the gap — carefully, on the topics the evidence points to.” |

---

### RP-05 — Workload Rebalancing

| Aspect | Provision |
|--------|-----------|
| **Educational meaning** | A recovery journey that restores feasible study intensity by temporarily rebalancing load against Profile capacity — inside authorised envelopes or via escalation when envelopes must change |
| **Suitable disruption profile** | Sustained capacity / availability shock or overload pattern such that remaining sitting ambition is unrealistic; student collapses under ordinary intensity even when willing |
| **Primary objectives** | RO-01, RO-02, RO-05 |
| **Typical triggers** | RT-05; RT-01 / RT-02 when intensity (not merely missed days) is the continuity threat |
| **Emphasised strategies** | RS-06, RS-07, RS-01; RS-08 as soon as temporary local rebalancing cannot keep the journey honest |
| **What it is not** | Permanent shrink of ambition by Recovery Coach fiat; presenting lighter load as proof the student “can’t handle” the syllabus; inventing recovery capacity the plan does not authorise |
| **Speech cue** | “Your available study capacity has changed — we’ll rebalance the near-term load so progress stays sustainable and realistic.” |

---

### RP-06 — Consistency Restoration

| Aspect | Provision |
|--------|-----------|
| **Educational meaning** | A recovery journey that rebuilds reliable study cadence after clustered misses or stop–start patterns, so progress compounds again under a predictable rhythm |
| **Suitable disruption profile** | Pattern of repeated missed sessions or irregular engagement that threatens consistency and plan continuity; the student is not primarily absent for one long block, nor primarily blocked by confidence alone |
| **Primary objectives** | RO-02, then RO-01 |
| **Typical triggers** | RT-02; mild RT-06 when interruption is cadence-shaped rather than structural |
| **Emphasised strategies** | RS-01, RS-06, RS-07; RS-03 for lawful high-value emphasis that makes cadence stick; RS-08 if backlog cannot fit envelopes |
| **What it is not** | Streak theatre or punitive reminder culture as educational truth; forcing intensity Profile capacity cannot sustain; equating one miss with consistency collapse |
| **Speech cue** | “We’ll rebuild a steady study rhythm — consistency that sticks matters more than sporadic catch-up bursts.” |

---

### RP-07 — Prerequisite Reconstruction

| Aspect | Provision |
|--------|-----------|
| **Educational meaning** | A recovery journey that reconstructs fragile earlier foundations after disruption before pushing new first-pass ambition — protecting the plan’s assumption that later topics rest on earlier understanding |
| **Suitable disruption profile** | Disruption has left prerequisite scope unsafe: later work would be coverage theatre on brittle foundations; evidence or Learning Coach diagnosis implicates foundation gaps in a disruption context |
| **Primary objectives** | RO-03, RO-04 |
| **Typical triggers** | RT-01 / RT-04 with foundation risk; RT-03 when Learning Coach diagnoses prerequisite / structure obstacles in disruption context |
| **Emphasised strategies** | RS-02, RS-05; coordinate with Learning Coach prerequisite / reinforcement interventions; RS-03 among lawful repair windows; never invent unofficial syllabus shortcuts |
| **What it is not** | Rewriting phase order by Recovery Coach fiat; a substitute for Learning Coach diagnosis; advancing coverage while foundations remain unsafe “to catch up” |
| **Speech cue** | “Before pushing further, we’ll rebuild the foundations the later work depends on — so progress stays real.” |

---

### RP-08 — Exam-Phase Recovery

| Aspect | Provision |
|--------|-----------|
| **Educational meaning** | A recovery journey for meaningful disruption that lands while the Canonical Study Plan’s authorised emphasis is exam-facing (revision windows, exam technique sittings, late-phase consolidation) — restoring continuity without inventing a panic cram path or stealing protected revision for first-pass backlog theatre |
| **Suitable disruption profile** | Disruption during exam-facing plan phase / emphasis; student needs restorative coaching that respects revision protections, exam-readiness honesty, and remaining calendar truth |
| **Primary objectives** | RO-03, RO-04, RO-01; RO-05 always protected |
| **Typical triggers** | RT-01 / RT-02 / RT-05 / RT-06 when plan phase emphasis is exam-facing; RT-04 when slip threatens exam-facing scope |
| **Emphasised strategies** | RS-03 within lawful exam-facing jobs; RS-05 / RS-02 as evidence warrants; RS-01 / RS-06 for safe return; RS-07 if recovery capacity authorised; RS-08 promptly when remaining exam journey is infeasible |
| **What it is not** | Sudden mock-only cramming as Recovery Coach strategy redesign; consuming protected revision to clear unfinished first-pass under “exam panic”; claiming exam readiness from recovery compliance |
| **Speech cue** | “Disruption landed in your exam-focused period — we’ll recover in a way that protects revision and understanding, not by inventing a panic cram plan.” |

## 6. Relationship to MS001 Strategies

| Pathway | Typical primary strategies | Notes |
|---------|---------------------------|-------|
| RP-01 | RS-01, RS-07 | Keep the journey short; escalate only if brief restart fails |
| RP-02 | RS-01, RS-06, RS-07, RS-04 | Gradual re-entry; may later emphasise RS-05 / RS-02 |
| RP-03 | RS-04 → RS-01 | Confidence first; then motion |
| RP-04 | RS-05, RS-02 | Evidence-targeted reinforcement |
| RP-05 | RS-06, RS-07, RS-08 | Local rebalance or structural escalation |
| RP-06 | RS-01, RS-06, RS-03 | Cadence before volume |
| RP-07 | RS-02, RS-05 | Foundations before first-pass ambition |
| RP-08 | RS-03, RS-05, RS-01, RS-08 | Protect exam-facing intent; escalate early if infeasible |

Hard rule:

> **Pathways consume strategies.  
> Strategies do not invent pathways.  
> RS-08 remains the structural escalation tactic — not a student-facing “pathway finish.”**

## 7. Anti-Pathways (Forbidden)

The following are **not** lawful recovery pathways:

- **Punishment catch-up pathway** — double-speed backlog clearance as the journey type
- **Silent replan pathway** — rewriting the Canonical Study Plan inside Recovery Coach
- **Mastery-from-rest pathway** — treating absence recovery as understanding restored
- **Obstacle-bypass pathway** — using recovery labels to skip Learning Coach diagnosis
- **Fluctuation theatre pathway** — opening RP-XX for temporary fluctuation
- **Score-ranked pathway** — selecting journeys by numeric fitness scores
