> It never invents educational certainty or pass guarantees.**

Students should understand **why Kwalitec has adopted a particular educational strategy**.

---

## 1. Purpose

A strategy the student cannot understand will feel arbitrary — even if educationally correct.

Strategy explainability ensures that approach names remain intelligible, honest about claim types, and distinct from:

- **Profile diagnosis** (“You are building foundations”) — MS002;
- **Plan structure** (“This week you study topic X at Y intensity”) — MS001.

Strategy speech answers: *“What overall approach are we taking, and why?”*

---

## 2. Strategy Explainability Principles

1. **Every selected primary strategy explains itself.**
2. **One primary educational reason** — not a dump of every dimension.
3. **Facts and estimates stay distinct.** Coverage and calendar are plain; understanding and readiness are estimated or provisional.
4. **Soft signals are labelled subjective** when they drove Confidence Restoration.
5. **Uncertainty is named** under thin evidence or assumption-reliant profiles.
6. **Internal machinery stays invisible.** No twin facets, score vectors, registry IDs, optimiser names, or ES-codes in student speech.
7. **Non-punitive tone** for Recovery, Confidence Restoration, Exam Rescue, and Late Starter.
8. **Transitions narrate change.** Material strategy changes answer “what changed?”
9. **Advice does not masquerade as destiny.** Strategy is approach — not a pass prophecy.
10. **Silence beats theatre.** Prefer cautious strategy language when warrant is thin.
11. **Do not collapse layers.** Diagnosis ≠ strategy ≠ plan schedule.

---

## 3. Five-Question Contract (Strategy)

Every material strategy surface must answer:

| # | Question | Strategy-specific guidance |
|---|----------|----------------------------|
| 1 | **What** approach are we taking? | Plain strategy name + one-sentence meaning |
| 2 | **Why** this approach for you now? | One primary reason tied to Profile (coverage, time, practice, recovery, risk) |
| 3 | **What will this feel like** in your plan? | Approach implication — not a full timetable (e.g. “More practice on topics you’ve already studied”) |
| 4 | **Known vs estimated?** | What hard facts drove the choice vs provisional readings |
| 5 | **What would make us change?** | When material — name the kind of change that would trigger transition |

Optional sixth after a transition:

| # | Question | When required |
|---|----------|---------------|
| 6 | **What changed?** | After material strategy succession |

---

## 4. Claim Types in Strategy Speech

| Claim type | Strategy examples | Student cue |
|------------|-------------------|-------------|
| Observed Fact | Exam date; declared hours; topics marked studied; leave/gap | Plain factual language |
| Derived Fact | Coverage progress; days remaining; missed-week pattern | Plain derived measure |
| Evidence-backed Estimate | “Practice suggests topic X is still shaky”; provisional readiness | *Estimated* / *Provisional* / *Suggested* |
| Soft / Subjective | “You reported low confidence after the mock” | *You reported* / *It sounds like* |
| Educational Advice | Optional emphasis inside the strategy | *Recommended* / *Optional* |

Forbidden speech patterns:

- “This strategy guarantees you will pass”
- “Our model selected RescueMode_v3 (score 0.81)”
- “You are on the elite high-performer track” as identity branding
- “You fell behind because you lack discipline” for Recovery / Late Starter / Exam Rescue
- “You’ve mastered the syllabus because we chose Revision Intensive”
- Presenting assumptions as observed facts
- Collapsing “we chose Practice Intensive” into “you are Exam Ready”

---

## 5. Layer Separation (Required)

When multiple layers appear together, keep verbs distinct:

| Layer | Prefer speech like… |
|-------|---------------------|
| Profile | “You’re currently **building foundations**.” |
| Strategy | “So our approach is **Foundation Building** — focusing on early syllabus foundations first.” |
| Plan | “This month’s plan sequences topics in official order at a sustainable pace.” |

Do not say: “You’re Foundation Building” as if the strategy label were the diagnosis state — use Profile state language for diagnosis and strategy language for approach.

## 6. Strategy Explanation Templates

Templates are educational contracts for future copy — not final UI strings. Replace bracketed tokens with student-specific facts.

### Foundation Building

- **What:** We’re following a Foundation Building approach.
- **Why:** You’re early in [exam] coverage, so we prioritise solid first-pass foundations before heavier practice or revision claims.
- **Feel:** Your plan will emphasise sequential study of early and prerequisite topics at a pace you can keep.
- **Known vs estimated:** Starting coverage and timetable known from your profile; understanding still limited until practice evidence grows.
- **Change if:** Coverage and cadence become stable enough for steady progression, or time pressure forces a more compressed approach.

### Steady Progression

- **What:** We’re following a Steady Progression approach.
- **Why:** Your foundations are underway and your time looks workable, so consistent sequential progress is the honest main strategy.
- **Feel:** Regular study rhythm; syllabus order; light return to earlier topics without making revision the whole story yet.
- **Known vs estimated:** Capacity and coverage from your profile; readiness remains provisional.
- **Change if:** Retention risk rises, weak topics dominate, you need recovery, or the exam window demands intensive revision.

### Knowledge Consolidation

- **What:** We’re following a Knowledge Consolidation approach.
- **Why:** You’ve covered enough material that forgetting earlier topics is a real risk, so we protect retention while first-pass continues.
- **Feel:** Interleaved return to recent topics alongside ongoing learning — not full exam revision yet.
- **Known vs estimated:** Coverage observed; retention risk estimated from journey length and evidence patterns.
- **Change if:** First-pass nears completion (revision intensifies), or practice/weak-topic needs take over.

### Practice Intensive

- **What:** We’re following a Practice Intensive approach.
- **Why:** You’ve studied enough of the current material to make question practice the main way we strengthen understanding.
- **Feel:** More applied questions and feedback on topics you’ve already covered.
- **Known vs estimated:** Coverage known; strengths and weaknesses estimated from practice outcomes.
- **Change if:** Practice reveals topics that need reinforcement, or the sitting horizon shifts you into revision.

### Revision Intensive

- **What:** We’re following a Revision Intensive approach.
- **Why:** With [exam date] approaching and substantial material already studied, consolidation is now the main educational job.
- **Feel:** Protected revision; limited new first-pass expansion; deeper practice on what you’ve already covered.
- **Known vs estimated:** Calendar and coverage known; exam readiness still provisional until evidence supports it.
- **Change if:** Feasibility becomes critical (rescue), readiness stabilises (maintenance), or new weaknesses need targeted reinforcement.

### Weak Topic Reinforcement

- **What:** We’re following a Weak Topic Reinforcement approach.
- **Why:** Evidence suggests some important topics are still shaky, so we strengthen those before treating progress as secure.
- **Feel:** Targeted return and practice on fragile areas — this is repair, not punishment.
- **Known vs estimated:** Weaknesses estimated from practice or foundation checks (or declared until evidenced).
- **Change if:** Those areas regain warrant, then we return to progression, practice, or revision as your profile supports.

### Confidence Restoration

- **What:** We’re following a Confidence Restoration approach.
- **Why:** [You reported low confidence / the recent mock shook your confidence], and we need truthful, achievable study that rebuilds trust in the process.
- **Feel:** Sustainable sessions, clear reasons for each focus, less overwhelm — without false reassurance.
- **Known vs estimated:** Your reported confidence is subjective; coverage and calendar remain factual.
- **Change if:** Confidence stabilises and we can resume normal progression, practice, or revision.

### Recovery Strategy

- **What:** We’re following a Recovery Strategy.
- **Why:** Study was interrupted or the previous pace broke, so we re-establish a sustainable trajectory without pretending nothing was learned before.
- **Feel:** Lower intensity at first; rebuild rhythm; re-orient to where coverage and evidence stand now.
- **Known vs estimated:** Gap and return observed; confidence may be subjective.
- **Change if:** Cadence returns for a sustained period — then we move back toward steady progression or the approach your profile now warrants.

### Exam Rescue

- **What:** We’re following an Exam Rescue approach.
- **Why:** With limited time remaining relative to what’s left, we need an honest triage of what still counts — not an impossible catch-up plan.
- **Feel:** Focus on high-value work you can still strengthen; freeze unrealistic new frontiers; clear trade-offs.
- **Known vs estimated:** Calendar and remaining coverage derived from your profile; pass chances are not something we invent as a number.
- **Change if:** The sitting date or your available time changes materially, or the immediate crisis stabilises into intensive revision.

### Late Starter Strategy

- **What:** We’re following a Late Starter approach.
- **Why:** You’re beginning (or re-scoping) preparation with less time than a full leisurely journey would need, so we plan a compressed but honest path from the start.
- **Feel:** Clear priorities within syllabus order; early feasibility honesty; protected minimum revision even under compression.
- **Known vs estimated:** Start position and deadline known; how far compression can go remains an honest estimate.
- **Change if:** You gain more time, or time becomes so tight that rescue triage is required.

### High Performer Optimisation

- **What:** We’re following a High Performer Optimisation approach.
- **Why:** On present evidence, coverage and practice look strong, so we refine exam craft and polish rather than restart foundations.
- **Feel:** Timed practice, targeted edges, mock calibration — without inventing beginner work you don’t need.
- **Known vs estimated:** Strength is estimated from evidence; it is not a permanent identity or a pass guarantee.
- **Change if:** A mock or new evidence shows a material gap, or we shift to maintenance near the sitting.

### Balanced Maintenance

- **What:** We’re following a Balanced Maintenance approach.
- **Why:** Your preparation looks exam-credible on present evidence, so we maintain readiness rather than overhaul the plan.
- **Feel:** Light spaced return, modest practice, stable rhythm; avoid last-minute syllabus expansion.
- **Known vs estimated:** Readiness is provisional; we keep watching evidence and how you feel under timed work.
- **Change if:** Decay, a new weak area, anxiety, or time shock appears.

## 7. Transition Narration Patterns

When strategy changes, lead with continuity and cause:

**Pattern A — Progression**  
“We’re moving from Foundation Building to Steady Progression because your early coverage and study rhythm are stable enough to keep progressing through the syllabus.”

**Pattern B — Deepen**  
“We’re shifting toward Practice Intensive because you’ve covered enough of these topics for question practice to be the main way we learn what is strong or shaky.”

**Pattern C — Approach**  
“We’re moving into Revision Intensive because the exam is closer and consolidating what you’ve already studied now matters more than opening new topics.”

**Pattern D — Restore**  
“We’re switching to a Recovery Strategy because study was interrupted. We’ll rebuild a pace you can keep — your earlier progress still counts.”

**Pattern E — Triage**  
“We’re changing to an Exam Rescue approach because the remaining time no longer supports the previous plan honestly. We’ll focus on what still matters most.”

**Pattern F — Confidence**  
“We’re taking a Confidence Restoration approach for a while because the recent setback shook how you feel about the work. We’ll keep the facts honest and the sessions achievable.”

Always pair with Known vs estimated and what would change next if useful.

---

## 8. Surfaces That Require Strategy Explainability

Material surfaces include:

1. First publication of a long-term plan (strategy must be named and justified).
2. Student “why this plan” / approach overview.
3. Material strategy transition after replan.
4. Feasibility or rescue conversations driven by strategy change.
5. Any UI that displays an approach/strategy label.

Non-material: purely administrative copy that does not claim educational approach.

---

## 9. Coordination with Profile and Planning Speech

| If the student asks… | Primary contract |
|----------------------|------------------|
| “Where am I educationally?” | Profile Explainability |
| “What overall approach are we taking?” | Strategy Explainability (this document) |
| “Why does this week/month look like this?” | Planning Explainability |

A complete coach answer may combine all three — in that order of educational dependence — without mixing claim types.

---

## 10. Success Test

Strategy explainability is compliant when:

1. The five-question contract is answerable for every published primary strategy.
2. Catalogue meaning is preserved in plain language (no invented strategy brands).
3. Soft and estimated claims are labelled.
4. Transitions answer what changed.
5. No internal machinery or pass guarantees appear.
6. Recovery / rescue / late-starter speech remains non-punitive.
