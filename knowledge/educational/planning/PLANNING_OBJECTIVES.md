## 1. Purpose

An expert IFoA tutor does not optimise “topics scheduled.” The tutor optimises **honest readiness under a life a student can actually live**.

These objectives bind every Master Planner algorithm.

---

## 2. Primary Objective

### O1 — Maximise exam readiness (honest)

**Definition.** Increase the student’s likelihood of performing competently under official examination conditions for the chosen sitting, using only lawful educational meaning of readiness.

**Includes:**

- Completing first-pass coverage of the official syllabus in sustainable time.
- Building application and exam behaviour through practice and mocks.
- Entering the sitting with protected revision completed — not skipped.

**Excludes:**

- Inflating readiness percentages without evidence warrant.
- Treating Study Progress completion as mastery.
- Scheduling impossible coverage that looks complete on paper.

**Tutor rationale.** Professional exams reward durable understanding and exam craft. Coverage without consolidation is false readiness.

---

## 3. Supporting Objectives

### O2 — Protect long-term retention

**Definition.** Prefer study structures that return to material over time so earlier topics remain usable at examination.

**Tutor rationale.** IFoA syllabuses are long. First-pass without consolidation produces late-stage amnesia. Retention protection is not optional “nice spacing”; it is part of readiness.

**Manifestations in planning:**

- Consolidation windows during first pass.
- Protected revision phase near the exam.
- Avoidance of endless one-way topic marching with zero return.

---

### O3 — Maintain consistency

**Definition.** Prefer steady, repeatable study rhythms over volatile boom–bust intensity.

**Tutor rationale.** Students who study most days at a sustainable intensity outperform those who plan heroic weekends they never keep. Consistency compounds; volatility burns trust.

**Manifestations:**

- Realistic weekly patterns aligned to working schedule.
- Avoidance of single-day overload spikes as the primary strategy.
- Stable phase transitions with disclosed reasons.

---

### O4 — Prevent burnout

**Definition.** Keep cognitive and calendar load inside sustainable bounds for the student’s declared capacity and life context.

**Tutor rationale.** Burned-out candidates abandon plans, then lose sequence and confidence. An expert tutor reduces intensity before inventing catch-up theatre.

**Manifestations:**

- Hard ceilings on daily/weekly load (see Constraints).
- Recovery allowance after mocks or dense blocks.
- Explicit downshifts when leave, illness, or missed weeks occur.

---

### O5 — Reserve revision capacity

**Definition.** Guarantee a non-trivial revision window before the sitting that first-pass learning must not consume.

**Tutor rationale.** Revision is where coverage becomes exam-usable. Plans that “finish learning the night before” are educationally negligent.

**Manifestations:**

- Backward planning from exam date with revision reserved first.
- Freeze rules for late first-pass expansion.
- Clear student explanation of why some topics wait or are deferred.

---

### O6 — Support recovery

**Definition.** Enable educationally sound restart after interruption without shame, false diagnosis, or invented mastery loss.

**Tutor rationale.** Actuarial candidates have jobs, leave, and life shocks. A tutor plans buffers and replanning posture; they do not punish honesty about missed weeks.

**Manifestations:**

- Buffer allocation in the calendar.
- Replan pathways that protect what still counts.
- Load reduction before heroic compression when remaining time shrinks.

---

### O7 — Promote confidence (earned)

**Definition.** Build justified confidence through clear sequence, visible progress, and honest practice — never through empty reassurance.

**Tutor rationale.** Confidence without warrant is dangerous; despair without guidance is useless. Plans should make progress legible and next actions obvious.

**Manifestations:**

- Transparent milestones (coverage checkpoints, revision start, mock windows).
- Plain explanations of why the plan looks as it does.
- Avoidance of catastrophic messaging when recovery is still feasible.

---

### O8 — Avoid unrealistic plans

**Definition.** Refuse or redesign trajectories that cannot fit available time, prerequisite order, and protected phases.

**Tutor rationale.** An expert tutor tells the truth early: “This sitting is not feasible at your current hours” — or proposes a reduced scope / later sitting — rather than publishing a fantasy schedule.

**Manifestations:**

- Feasibility checks before plan publication.
- Explicit trade-off communication when time is insufficient.
- Preference for honest reduced ambition over silent impossibility.

## 4. Secondary Quality Objectives

These improve plan quality but never override O1–O8 or permanent constraints.

| ID | Objective | Educational meaning |
|----|-----------|---------------------|
| O9 | Prefer prerequisite clarity | Students always know why topic B follows topic A |
| O10 | Prefer exam-craft rehearsal | Mocks and timed practice appear when educationally meaningful |
| O11 | Prefer preference fit | Honour study preferences *within* educational law |
| O12 | Prefer minimal decision burden | Plan should reduce daily “what next?” friction |
| O13 | Prefer continuity of history | Replans preserve lawful Study Progress and evidence posture |

---

## 5. Conflict Resolution Order

When objectives conflict, resolve in this order (highest wins):

1. **Educational truth & claim lawfulness** (Constitution) — never optimise by lying.
2. **Feasibility / avoid unrealistic plans (O8)** — impossible plans are invalid.
3. **Prevent burnout (O4)** and **maintain consistency (O3)** — sustainability beats heroic coverage.
4. **Reserve revision capacity (O5)** — revision is not residual scrap time.
5. **Maximise honest exam readiness (O1)** under the above.
6. **Protect retention (O2)** and **support recovery (O6)**.
7. **Promote earned confidence (O7)**.
8. Secondary quality objectives (O9–O13).

### Worked conflict examples

| Conflict | Lawful resolution |
|----------|-------------------|
| Cover every remaining topic vs protect revision | Protect revision; defer or escalate infeasibility rather than erase revision |
| Higher intensity to “catch up” vs burnout prevention | Reduce scope / intensity; replan; do not schedule unsustainable catch-up as default |
| Student preference to skip foundations vs prerequisite order | Prerequisite order wins; explain why |
| Optimistic readiness messaging vs thin evidence | Understate; label estimates; do not manufacture confidence |

---

## 6. What Plans Must Not Optimise

The following are **not** educational objectives for Master Planner:

| Anti-objective | Why forbidden |
|----------------|---------------|
| Maximise topics scheduled regardless of retention | Produces coverage theatre |
| Maximise engagement metrics / session counts | Can encourage shallow clicking |
| Maximise opaque score movement | Disconnects from educational meaning |
| Minimise student discomfort by hiding hard truths | Destroys trust and feasibility |
| Maximise “personalisation” that breaks syllabus order silently | Violates curriculum primacy |

## 7. Measurement Guidance (Non-Implementing)

Future algorithms may define metrics, but metrics must map to these objectives without collapsing concepts:

| Objective | Lawful signal families (illustrative) |
|-----------|----------------------------------------|
| O1 Readiness | Coverage vs syllabus; practice evidence; mock completion; revision completion — all labelled correctly |
| O2 Retention | Presence of consolidation/revision windows; return frequency policy |
| O3 Consistency | Variance of planned weekly load; adherence-compatible patterns |
| O4 Burnout | Peak daily load; consecutive high-intensity days; recovery gaps |
| O5 Revision | Reserved days/hours before sitting; first-pass freeze date |
| O6 Recovery | Buffer days; replan triggers; post-interruption load ceilings |
| O7 Confidence | Explainability completeness; milestone clarity — not pep-talk volume |
| O8 Realism | Capacity vs required hours; infeasibility flags |

Metrics never authorise claiming mastery from coverage alone.
