## 1. Purpose

Without explicit recovery objectives, “get back on track” collapses into punishment volume, stolen revision, or false mastery speech.

With explicit objectives, an expert tutor can choose restorative strategies that rebuild a durable journey — momentum, consistency, understanding, confidence — while remaining faithful to long-term plan intent.

---

## 2. Objective Catalogue

### RO-01 — Restore learning momentum

**Definition.** Help the student re-enter a workable rhythm of study after disruption so that educational progress can resume without requiring heroic first days.

**Tutor rationale.** After illness, travel, or life shock, the first educational job is often *returning to motion* — not clearing the entire backlog. Momentum that sticks beats a single overloaded catch-up day that triggers another collapse.

**Success looks like:**

- The student can name and start a lawful next study job.
- Sessions resume at sustainable frequency relative to Profile capacity.
- Recovery speech emphasises restart, not shame for the gap.

**Must not:**

- Equate momentum with covering every missed cell in one week.
- Treat calendar density alone as proof of restored learning.

---

### RO-02 — Rebuild consistency

**Definition.** Re-establish reliable study continuity — regular, plan-faithful sittings — so that progress compounds again after interruption.

**Tutor rationale.** IFoA preparation rewards consistency more than sporadic intensity. Recovery that rebuilds a steady cadence protects both learning and wellbeing.

**Success looks like:**

- Missed-session clusters reduce under a sustainable pattern.
- Daily Coach can again select ordinary plan jobs without perpetual crisis mode.
- The student experiences study as predictable enough to trust.

**Must not:**

- Rebuild consistency through streak theatre or punitive reminders as educational truth.
- Force intensity that Profile capacity cannot sustain.

---

### RO-03 — Protect long-term understanding

**Definition.** Prefer restorative choices that preserve durable understanding of already-studied material over superficial coverage of everything that was “missed on the calendar.”

**Tutor rationale.** Disruption often threatens retention of earlier work. An expert tutor protects foundations and understanding before inventing first-pass overload that leaves prior learning brittle.

**Success looks like:**

- Prerequisite and retrieval emphasis appear when the trail warrants them.
- Protected revision intent is not silently consumed to clear first-pass backlog.
- Understanding claims remain evidence-honest (EIP-002 / EIP-006).

**Must not:**

- Narrate rest or resumed ticks as restored understanding.
- Sacrifice durable knowledge for calendar catch-up theatre.

---

### RO-04 — Minimise educational regression

**Definition.** Limit loss of demonstrated learning progress and plan continuity attributable to disruption — without inventing regression from thin evidence.

**Tutor rationale.** Meaningful absence can weaken retrieval and application. Recovery should contain that risk through focused restoration, not pretend nothing changed — and not invent catastrophic decay from one quiet week.

**Success looks like:**

- Where evidence suggests slip, recovery emphasises retrieval / reinforcement of at-risk scope.
- Where evidence is thin, recovery gathers warrant rather than claiming collapse.
- Continuity of learner history is respected (EIP-005); disruption is not narrated as wiping identity.

**Must not:**

- Infer mastery loss from missed sessions alone.
- Use “you’ve forgotten everything” as motivational copy.

---

### RO-05 — Preserve learner confidence

**Definition.** Protect the student’s belief that return is possible and worthwhile — framing recovery as intentional coaching, not moral repair for failure.

**Tutor rationale.** Candidates who feel ashamed after disruption abandon plans. Confidence preservation is an educational good: it enables RO-01 and RO-02.

**Success looks like:**

- Explanations are supportive and concrete.
- Workload is temporarily adjusted without implying the student is “behind as a person.”
- Resume criteria are hopeful and educationally specific.

**Must not:**

- Lead with “you fell behind” as the primary educational reason.
- Convert recovery into comparison theatre against other candidates’ pace.

---

## 3. Priority Ordering Among Objectives

When recovery objectives compete, apply this binding order:

1. **RO-05 — Confidence** when shame or abandonment risk would block any return  
2. **RO-01 — Momentum** when the student cannot yet re-enter study at all  
3. **RO-02 — Consistency** once restart is possible  
4. **RO-03 — Long-term understanding** and **RO-04 — Regression minimisation** as evidence warrants — never sacrificed for volume catch-up  
5. Calendar completeness / backlog clearance — **never** a recovery objective in itself

Hard rule:

> **Sustainable educational progress outranks simply catching up.**

## 5. Anti-Objectives (Forbidden Optimisations)

Recovery must **not** optimise for:

- maximising missed-session clearance in the shortest calendar window;
- maximising engagement metrics, streaks, or guilt;
- inventing intensity that breaks Profile capacity or plan protections;
- appearing “caught up” while understanding and confidence degrade;
- silent strategy or plan rewrite to make the numbers look tidy.

---

## 6. How Objectives Bind Strategies

Every named recovery strategy in `RECOVERY_STRATEGIES.md` must declare which RO-XX goods it primarily advances.

A strategy that advances calendar theatre without advancing at least one of RO-01…RO-05 is educationally invalid for Recovery Coach use.
