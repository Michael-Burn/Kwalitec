## 1. Purpose

Students should never have to guess why Kwalitec has shifted into recovery — or feel punished for needing it.

Recovery explainability exists so every material recovery recommendation answers — in plain educational language — why recovery, what disruption, how it helps the long-term journey, and when ordinary progression should return.

Without recovery explainability:

- lighter load feels like abandonment or demotion;
- catch-up pressure feels arbitrary or shaming;
- disruption and learning problems blur into opaque advice;
- escalation feels like failure;
- temporary fluctuation is dressed as crisis.

With recovery explainability:

- the student trusts the tutor posture;
- disruption → warrant → strategy → resume stays visible;
- local restoration vs structural replan is honest;
- confidence is protected (RO-05).

---

## 2. Traceability Obligation (Architectural)

Every material recovery recommendation must be traceable through:

| Trace link | Student-facing role |
|------------|---------------------|
| **Observed disruption** | “Here’s what interrupted your study rhythm…” |
| **Why meaningful (not fluctuation)** | “This goes beyond a quiet evening because…” |
| **Evidence basis (brief)** | “Looking at recent study / practice / return…” |
| **Recovery objectives** | “Right now we’re prioritising rebuilding [momentum / consistency / confidence / …]…” |
| **Strategy** | “So the educational approach is…” |
| **Long-term plan faithfulness** | “This keeps your overall preparation intent intact by…” |
| **Boundary honesty** | “This stays within your current plan…” / “This needs a longer-term plan adjustment…” |
| **Resume expectation** | “We’ll expect ordinary progression to resume when…” |

Internal IDs (RT-XX, RS-XX, RO-XX) may exist for algorithms and audits. They must not appear as student-facing jargon.

A recovery recommendation with no disruption link (or no explicit “temporary fluctuation — no recovery episode” account when declining crisis speech) is invalid — even if the tip sounds motivating.

Architectural requirement restated:

> **Recovery recommendations must be traceable to observed educational disruption and accumulated evidence.**

---

## 3. Four-Question Framework (Recovery Specialisation)

Every material Recovery Coach recommendation must answer EIP-003’s four questions, specialised for recovery:

| EIP-003 question | Recovery specialisation |
|------------------|-------------------------|
| **What is known?** | What disruption was observed; what evidence exists about continuity / regression |
| **What is estimated?** | Provisional judgements (e.g. likely at-risk scope) — labelled as provisional |
| **Why this guidance?** | Why recovery (not ordinary continuity); why this strategy; how it serves long-term success |
| **What next?** | Concrete restorative next step + when normal progression is expected to resume |

Students must hear all four — especially **why recovery** and **when resume** — not only a lighter-load instruction.

---

## 4. Required Student Understandings

Material recovery speech must make the following understandable:

### 4.1 Why recovery is needed

- Name the educational reason in plain language (absence, repeated misses, availability change, interrupted plan, evidence of slip after a gap).
- Distinguish from “you failed” moral framing.
- If recovery is *not* warranted, say so and coach ordinary continuity instead.

### 4.2 What educational disruption was detected

- Describe the disruption as study-life / continuity fact.
- Do not inflate temporary fluctuation into crisis.
- Where regression is claimed, keep it evidence-honest and provisional.

### 4.3 How recovery supports long-term success

- Tie strategy to durable goods: momentum, consistency, understanding protection, confidence.
- Explicitly reject “catch up at any cost” as the story.
- Show faithfulness to the long-term plan intent (“same destination; safer path back”).

### 4.4 When normal progression is expected to resume

- Give educational resume criteria (sustainable rhythm returned; plan jobs workable again; regression warrant addressed).
- Avoid false calendar promises (“you’ll be fully caught up by Friday”) unless plan/scheduling authority actually supports them.
- When escalation is required, explain that normal progression resumes under an updated authorised plan — not under fake local certainty.

---

## 5. Narrative Rules

1. **Lead with intentional recovery, not shame.** “We’ve shifted into a recovery approach because…” beats “you fell behind.”
2. **Separate disruption facts from learning claims.** Missed sessions ≠ forgotten syllabus.
3. **Label estimates.** “It looks like X may need a refresh” ≠ “you’ve lost everything.”
4. **Name the strategy educationally.** Gradual restart, lighter load, focused retrieval — not optimiser jargon.
5. **Keep plan speech consistent.** Align with Canonical Study Plan / Recovered posture narration.
6. **Keep Daily Coach speech consistent.** Today’s objective remains Day Coach’s to state; recovery explains the restorative frame.
7. **Escalate honestly.** “Your plan needs a proper adjustment” is lawful; silent rewrite is not.
8. **Close with resume hope.** Every recovery message should make return to ordinary progression imaginable.

---

## 6. Language Rules

### Prefer

- “After [disruption], the most educationally sensible next step is a gradual restart.”
- “We’re protecting your long-term understanding rather than rushing to clear the calendar.”
- “Lighter load for a while — intentional recovery, not punishment.”
- “We’ll return to your ordinary plan rhythm when consistent sittings feel sustainable again.”
- “This may need a plan update so your preparation stays realistic.”

### Avoid

- “You fell behind / failed your plan.”
- “You need to catch up everything this week.”
- “Rest restored your mastery.”
- “The algorithm decided you’re in recovery mode.”
- Opaque scores, streak guilt, or comparison theatre.
- Internal IDs (RT-04, RS-05) in student-facing copy.

---

## 7. Claim-Type Honesty

| Claim type | Recovery Coach cue |
|------------|-------------------|
| **Observed disruption** | “You were away / sessions were missed / availability changed…” |
| **Provisional regression risk** | “Recent practice suggests [topic] may need a refresh…” |
| **Recovery recommendation** | “So we recommend…” |
| **Plan / scheduling fact** | “Your plan already includes recovery capacity…” / “Your plan needs updating…” |
| **Mastery / readiness** | **Do not claim** from recovery alone |

---

## 8. Surface Contracts

Any student-facing surface that presents Recovery Coach guidance must:

1. Answer the four specialised questions (§3).
2. Make the four required understandings (§4) available without detective work.
3. Preserve claim-type honesty (§7).
4. Remain consistent with Daily Coach and Study Plan narration.
5. Disclose local restoration vs escalation (RS-08) when relevant.
6. Avoid shame-first framing.
7. Refuse mastery speech from rest, resumed ticks, or backlog clearance.

## 9. Good and Bad Examples

### Good — post-illness return

> “You’ve had a stretch away from study after illness. Rather than trying to clear everything at once, we’ll restart gradually with a lighter, sustainable load so you can rebuild momentum and keep the understanding you’ve already built. This stays within your preparation plan’s intent. We’ll move back to your ordinary study rhythm once consistent sittings feel workable again.”

### Bad — punishment catch-up

> “You’re behind. Double your hours this week until you’re caught up.”

### Good — escalation

> “The gap is large enough that adjusting today’s emphasis isn’t enough to keep your plan realistic. We need to update the study timetable / plan so recovery stays sustainable. That’s not a failure — it’s how we protect the long-term preparation.”

### Bad — silent rewrite

> “I’ve changed your whole plan so you can catch up.” *(without Master Planner / scheduling authority or explanation)*

### Good — temporary fluctuation (no recovery episode)

> “Missing one evening doesn’t require a recovery reset. Tomorrow we’ll continue with the next sensible job on your plan.”

### Bad — crisis theatre

> “One miss means you’re in recovery mode and at risk of failing.”

## 10. Authority Duties for Explainability

| Authority | Recovery explainability duty |
|-----------|------------------------------|
| **Recovery Coach** | State warrant, disruption, strategy, resume criteria, boundary honesty |
| **Daily Coach** | State today’s objective under recovery frame without contradicting recovery speech |
| **Learning Coach** | Keep progression / obstacle / intervention explanations distinct from recovery continuity speech |
| **Master Planner / Scheduling** | Own explanations of structural plan / timetable change after RS-08 |
| **Surfaces** | Never invent certainty, shame, or mastery from recovery compliance |
