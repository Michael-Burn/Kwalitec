> **Students should understand:  
> why recovery is considered successful (or not yet),  
> what evidence supports that conclusion,  
> what changes next,  
> and what ongoing monitoring remains.  
> Explanation is supportive — never judgement.  
> Explainability never invents educational certainty.  
> Completion never implies mastery.**

## 1. Purpose

Students should never have to guess whether recovery is over — or feel that “you’re fine now” was invented from a streak counter.

Completion explainability specialises MS001 recovery explainability and MS002 pathway explainability so every material completion judgement answers — in plain educational language — why success (or not), what evidence, what changes next, and what watchfulness remains.

Without completion explainability:

- ending recovery feels arbitrary or premature;
- continuing recovery feels like punishment or permanent demotion;
- students confuse restored continuity with mastery;
- monitoring feels like distrust;
- escalation or pathway change feels like failure;
- Daily Coach return feels unexplained.

With completion explainability:

- the student trusts the tutor posture;
- warrant → pathway → completion → next step stays visible;
- mastery honesty is preserved;
- confidence is protected (RO-05).

## 3. Traceability Obligation (Architectural)

Every material completion judgement must be traceable through:

| Trace link | Student-facing role |
|------------|---------------------|
| **Recovery context** | “We’ve been rebuilding after…” |
| **Why success / not yet** | “We can return to ordinary study pacing because…” / “We’re not ready to call this complete because…” |
| **Evidence basis (brief)** | “Looking at your recent study rhythm / engagement / foundation work…” |
| **Explicit non-grounds (when useful)** | “This isn’t just because a week passed / sessions were ticked…” |
| **What changes next** | “From here, ordinary daily guidance resumes…” / “We’ll adjust the recovery approach…” / “Your longer-term plan needs an authorised update…” |
| **Monitoring remaining** | “We’ll still keep an eye on…” / “No special recovery watch needed beyond ordinary coaching…” |
| **Mastery honesty** | “This means continuity is back — not that everything is mastered.” |

Internal IDs (RCC-XX, RCE-XX, CT-XX) may exist for algorithms and audits. They must not appear as student-facing jargon.

Architectural requirement restated:

> **Recovery completion must be justified by accumulated educational evidence.**

A completion message with no evidence link — or one that cites only attendance, ticks, or elapsed time — is invalid.

---

## 4. Four-Question Framework (Completion Specialisation)

Every material completion judgement must answer EIP-003’s four questions, specialised for completion:

| EIP-003 question | Completion specialisation |
|------------------|---------------------------|
| **What is known?** | What restorative continuity / engagement / confidence / safety observations accumulated |
| **What is estimated?** | Provisional judgements (e.g. residual fragility) — labelled as provisional |
| **Why this guidance?** | Why completion is affirmed or withheld; why this CT transition |
| **What next?** | Concrete next coaching posture + monitoring statement |

Students must hear all four — especially **why successful / not yet** and **what monitoring remains** — not only “back to normal.”

---

## 5. Required Student Understandings

Material completion speech must make the following understandable:

### 5.1 Why recovery is considered successful (or not yet)

**When successful (CT-01):**

- Name restored educational conditions in plain language (consistency, engagement, sustainable rhythm, confidence if relevant, foundation safety if relevant).
- Tie success to the original disruption without shame.
- State clearly that ordinary progression can resume.

**When not yet:**

- Name which restorative conditions are still missing, without moral framing.
- Prefer concrete educational gaps (“your study rhythm is still fragile after the long break”) over vague “not ready.”

### 5.2 What evidence supports that conclusion

- Brief, plain account of the trail (recent rhythm, sustained engagement, foundation practice where relevant).
- Distinguish known observations from provisional estimates.
- Where useful, say what was *not* treated as proof (time alone, ticks alone).
- Never invent observations to make a tidy story.

### 5.3 What changes next

| Transition | Student-facing cue (illustrative posture, not copy-deck) |
|------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| **CT-01** | Ordinary daily guidance resumes under your plan |
| **CT-02** | We continue with light watch / keep gathering a clearer picture |
| **CT-03** | We change the recovery approach to better fit what we’re seeing |
| **CT-04** | Your plan needs an authorised longer-term adjustment — recovery alone isn’t enough |
| **CT-05** | Continuity is back; next we’ll focus on how learning itself is going |

### 5.4 What ongoing monitoring remains

- If residual risk exists, name it specifically and supportively (e.g. confidence after return; foundation safety).
- If no special recovery watch remains, say that ordinary coaching vigilance is enough — do not invent probation.
- Monitoring must not sound like distrust or punishment.

### 5.5 What completion does *not* mean

Material success speech must not allow the student to reasonably conclude that:

- topics are mastered because recovery ended;
- the exam is now assured;
- the disruption history was erased;
- punishment catch-up is still owed “to be safe.”

---

## 6. Tone and Language Rules

1. **Supportive, never judgement.** Completion is coaching wisdom, not a moral verdict.  
2. **Hopeful and specific.** “Ordinary progression can resume because your rhythm has stabilised” beats “good job surviving recovery mode.”  
3. **No shame archaeology.** Do not reopen “you fell behind” as the lead story at completion.  
4. **No mastery ceremony.** Forbid “fully recovered knowledge,” “back to mastery,” or exam-readiness claims from completion.  
5. **No timer speech.** Forbid “your two weeks are up.”  
6. **No streak speech as proof.** Streaks may be UX chrome; they are not educational evidence in student explanation.  
7. **Honest escalation.** CT-04 is care for feasibility, not abandonment.  
8. **Honest incomplete pathway change.** CT-03 is clearer route, not failure.  
9. **Close with next step.** Every completion message names what happens tomorrow educationally.  
10. **Protect confidence (RO-05)** especially when withholding CT-01.

## 7. Worked Explainability Sketches (Non-Normative Copy)

These illustrate posture. Product copy may differ; educational obligations may not.

### 7.1 CT-01 success after short disruption

> You’ve rebuilt a steady study rhythm after that interruption, and you’ve been sustaining real study work again — not just checking in. That means we can return to ordinary daily guidance under your plan. This doesn’t mean everything is mastered; it means continuity is back. We’ll coach the next lawful topics as usual.

### 7.2 CT-01 with light CT-02 monitoring after prolonged absence

> Your return has stuck: consistent sittings, sustainable pace, and you’re starting work without the freeze that blocked you earlier. Ordinary daily guidance can resume. We’ll still keep a light eye on confidence over the next stretch — not as a test, just to make sure the return stays comfortable.

### 7.3 Not yet — CT-02 gather

> You’re moving again, which matters. We’re not calling recovery complete yet because the trail after such a long gap is still thin — especially for the foundation topics we flagged. Next we keep the gentle return plan and gather clearer evidence before we change posture.

### 7.4 CT-03 alternative pathway

> Restarting got you moving, but what we’re seeing now points more to fragile foundations than to a simple gap in attendance. We’ll shift the recovery approach to rebuild those prerequisites safely. Same destination; clearer restorative route. This isn’t a demotion.

### 7.5 CT-04 escalation

> Local recovery isn’t enough to make the remaining plan feasible without overload. The honest next step is an authorised plan adjustment — not another burst of catch-up. We’ll keep supporting you while that longer-term update is made. You’re not being abandoned.

### 7.6 CT-05 Learning Coach handoff

> Your study continuity has returned enough that disruption is no longer the main story. What still needs attention is how learning itself is progressing on the topics you’ve resumed. We’ll hand that to learning-focused coaching. Finishing recovery doesn’t mean those topics are mastered.

### 7.7 Forbidden sketches

> ~~Your recovery period has expired.~~  
> ~~You completed five sessions — recovery achieved.~~  
> ~~You’re fully caught up and back to mastery.~~  
> ~~We’ll keep you in recovery mode indefinitely to be safe.~~  
> ~~Others are further ahead, so you’re not done.~~

---

## 8. Surface Obligations

Any student-facing surface that presents recovery completion must:

1. Answer the four EIP-003 questions as specialised above.  
2. Make §5 understandings available without requiring the student to infer them.  
3. Keep internal IDs invisible.  
4. Refuse mastery / exam-readiness speech from completion.  
5. Refuse time-only / attendance-only / tick-only justifications.  
6. Align with Daily Coach explainability after CT-01 — do not keep a parallel crisis narrator.  
7. Remain consistent with MS001 / MS002 disruption and pathway stories already told.

---

## 9. Anti-Patterns (Forbidden)

- Silent completion with no evidence account  
- Completion celebrated as mastery graduation  
- “Monitoring” framed as probation or distrust  
- Escalation framed as personal failure  
- Pathway change framed as the student “failing recovery”  
- Dual messaging: “complete” while still demanding punishment catch-up  
- Jargon dumps (RCC, RCE, CT, RP, Twin, scores)
