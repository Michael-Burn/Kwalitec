> why this examination strategy was selected,

> Explainability never invents educational certainty.**

## 1. Purpose

Students should never have to guess why Kwalitec chose *this kind* of examination preparation — or feel that strategy changes are opaque mode switches, punishments for nerves, or euphemisms for unfinished first-pass, unfinished revision, or Recovery catch-up.

Strategy explainability specialises MS001 examination explainability so every material strategy decision answers — in plain educational language — why this approach, what evidence, what outcome, and what would select a different strategy.

Without strategy explainability:

- different assessment approaches feel arbitrary;
- final consolidation feels like panic or false readiness;
- confidence speech feels like pep talk or shame;
- rehearsal feels like pointless grinding;
- transitions feel like failure;
- Revision / Recovery / Learning handoffs feel like abandonment;
- endless exam tips feel like the only answer even when they are not.

With strategy explainability:

- the student trusts the assessment-facing tutor posture;
- warrant → approach → evidence → expected outcome → change conditions stays visible;
- strategy change and upstream handoffs are honest educational events;
- examination performance purpose remains speakable without pass promises.

## 3. Traceability Obligation (Architectural)

Every material strategy recommendation must be traceable through:

| Trace link | Student-facing role |
|------------|---------------------|
| **Prior preparation** | “You’ve already learned and revised the material we’re preparing to show…” |
| **Why examination preparation now** | “We’re in assessment-facing preparation because…” |
| **Why this approach** | “The most educationally fitting examination approach is…” |
| **Evidence basis** | “Looking at your recent learning / revision / rehearsal / sitting timing…” |
| **Expected preparation outcome** | “If this goes well, you should be better able to…” |
| **What would change the strategy** | “We’d switch approach if…” / “We’d pause exam prep if…” |
| **Objectives link** | “The educational goal is to strengthen [demonstration / exam planning / conditions / confidence / sitting decisions]…” |
| **Boundary honesty** | “This is assessment preparation of material you’ve already prepared…” / “This isn’t an exam approach yet — we need first learning / revision / recovery / a learning diagnosis…” |
| **Plan faithfulness** | “This stays within your plan’s examination intent by…” |

Internal IDs (EXS-XX, EPT-XX, EXO-XX, EXP-XX, EST-XX) may exist for algorithms and audits. They must not appear as student-facing jargon.

A strategy recommendation with no prior-preparation link, no educational reason for *this* approach type, or no expected outcome / change conditions is invalid — even if the tip sounds motivating.

Architectural requirement restated:

> **Each strategy must be traceable to accumulated educational evidence.  
> Strategies may optimise assessment preparation —  
> they must not compensate for absent learning, override Revision Coach recommendations,  
> redefine mastery, or modify the Canonical Study Plan —  
> and must say so honestly when upstream handoff is required.**

## 4. Four-Question Framework (Strategy Specialisation)

Every material Exam Coach strategy recommendation must answer EIP-003’s four questions, specialised for strategy:

| EIP-003 question | Strategy specialisation |
|------------------|-------------------------|
| **What is known?** | What prior learning / revision / continuity evidence exists; what sitting context is bound |
| **What is estimated?** | Provisional judgements (e.g. likely conditions fragility) — labelled as provisional |
| **Why this guidance?** | Why *this kind* of examination preparation now; why not another approach; how it serves examination objectives |
| **What next?** | Concrete assessment-facing next step + expected preparation outcome + what would select a different strategy |

Students must hear all four — especially **why this approach**, **expected outcome**, and **change conditions** — not only a strategy label.

---

## 5. Required Student-Facing Elements

Every material strategy narration must include:

1. **Approach reason** — why this kind of examination preparation was selected.  
2. **Evidence support** — what educational evidence supports it (brief, non-shaming).  
3. **Expected preparation outcome** — what should get better if the approach is followed.  
4. **Change conditions** — what would cause a different strategy (or upstream handoff) to be selected.  
5. **Assessment-facing honesty** — that this prepares demonstration of already-prepared material (or an honest handoff if not).  

Optional but encouraged when relevant:

- link to plan examination window / sitting proximity without panic theatre;
- contrast with approaches *not* chosen (“not full paper rehearsal yet — steadying demonstration first”);
- transition cue when moving toward final preparation or upstream coaches.

## 6. Speech Patterns by Strategy (Constructive Cues)

| Strategy | Supportive speech pattern (illustrative) |
|----------|------------------------------------------|
| **EXS-01 Readiness Consolidation** | “You’ve built the understanding — we’ll consolidate how you show it under exam-facing practice.” |
| **EXS-02 Examination Rehearsal** | “We’ll rehearse under real sitting conditions with material you already know.” |
| **EXS-03 Time-Management Preparation** | “We’ll practise how you use the clock — protecting what you can already do.” |
| **EXS-04 Question-Selection Practice** | “We’ll practise choosing the right approach inside the paper.” |
| **EXS-05 Confidence Stabilisation** | “We’ll steady your confidence to match what the evidence actually shows.” |
| **EXS-06 Final Consolidation** | “In this final stretch we’ll consolidate your examination approach — not invent coverage.” |
| **EXS-07 Post-Disruption Examination Preparation** | “You’ve restored study continuity — we’ll re-enter examination preparation carefully.” |

Tone rules:

- Supportive, tutor-like, specific.  
- Never shame ordinary exam nerves.  
- Never claim certainty of pass or mastery.  
- Never present final consolidation as guaranteed success or confidence work as pep-talk theatre.

## 7. Explaining Transitions

| Transition class | Student must hear |
|------------------|-------------------|
| **EST1 Toward final preparation** | Why the final stretch changes emphasis; that this protects known strengths rather than inventing coverage; that this is not a pass declaration |
| **EST2 Revision Coach handback** | Why consolidating revision is now primary; that exam approach waits on strengthening known material; that this is honesty, not demotion |
| **EST3 Recovery Coach handoff** | That disruption continuity comes first; examination approaches pause or wait until recovery allows |
| **EST4 Learning Coach escalation** | Why exam tips alone are not enough; that a learning diagnosis / response is the honest next step; that this is support, not abandonment |
| **EST5 Strategy change** | What changed in the evidence / need; why the new approach fits; what outcome is now expected; what would change it again |
| **EST6 Structural escalation** | That the plan / timetable needs adjustment beyond local examination approach change |
| **EST7 Post-examination reflection** | That the sitting is done; preparation mode ends; reflection and honest next routing begin |
| **EST8 Hold / gather** | That we are checking carefully rather than inventing crisis or false readiness |

Hard rule for EST2–EST4 speech:

> **Never imply that more of the same examination strategy will fix  
> unfinished revision, Recovery-owned disruption, or a deeper learning problem  
> after evidence shows Exam Coach is no longer the honest owner.**

---

## 8. Forbidden Speech

Strategy explainability must **not**:

- use internal IDs (EXS-03, EPT-02, EST4) in student-facing copy;
- claim “exam ready” or redefine mastery from strategy compliance;
- present numeric strategy scores, readiness percentages, or optimiser ranks as educational truth;
- call unlearned content “exam prep” or “final consolidation”;
- disguise Recovery catch-up as an examination approach;
- override or dismiss Revision Coach consolidating recommendations in speech;
- shame the student for ordinary nerves or a weak mock;
- hide Learning / Recovery / Revision handoffs behind endless “one more paper” promises;
- invent prior preparation or evidence that does not exist;
- narrate final consolidation as guaranteed success;
- narrate post-examination reflection as identity failure from a single sitting.

## 9. Worked Explainability Examples

### 9.1 Lawful — Examination rehearsal

> “You’ve already learned and revised the core methods we’re preparing to show. In timed practice this week, format and pacing still threw you more than the methods themselves. So we’re focusing on examination rehearsal: working under real sitting conditions with material you know. If this goes well, timing and format should feel less surprising. We’d change approach if the methods themselves prove fragile — that would be consolidating revision, not more timed grinding of weak foundations.”

### 9.2 Lawful — Toward final consolidation

> “Your plan’s final examination window is open, and your recent practice shows workable demonstration of the prepared scope. We’re shifting to final consolidation: protect known strengths, light sitting-plan rehearsal, and confidence matched to evidence — not a last-minute pass through unread chapters. We’d pause this if a disruption hit or if revision evidence showed a known hub still needs consolidating first.”

### 9.3 Lawful — Revision Coach handback

> “The timed paper showed that one chapter you studied still won’t hold under pressure. Continuing to call that ‘exam strategy’ wouldn’t be honest. The next step is consolidating revision of that known material so assessment practice has something solid to show. Examination approach can resume once that consolidating job is in better shape.”

### 9.4 Lawful — Post-examination reflection

> “The sitting is done, so we’re leaving examination preparation mode. Next we’ll reflect on what the paper showed — including time use and method selection — and decide honestly whether the next work is revision, learning diagnosis, recovery, or planning for a later sitting. That reflection is support, not a verdict on your worth.”

### 9.5 Unlawful — Final consolidation theatre

> ~~“Exam strategy selected: Final Consolidation (score 94). You are exam ready for the whole syllabus.”~~  
> Unlawful: numeric theatre, mastery minting, possible coverage invention, internal jargon.

## 10. Audit Checklist (Before Student-Facing Release)

Before releasing strategy narration, confirm:

- [ ] Prior-preparation honesty stated or honest handoff used  
- [ ] Why *this* approach (not only why exam prep)  
- [ ] Evidence basis present and claim-typed  
- [ ] Expected preparation outcome stated  
- [ ] Change conditions stated (what would select a different strategy / handoff)  
- [ ] EXO goods speakable without jargon IDs  
- [ ] No mastery / pass certainty  
- [ ] No Recovery / first-learning / Revision-override disguise  
- [ ] Upstream handoffs spoken honestly when EST2–EST4 apply  
- [ ] Consistent with MS001 examination explainability  

If any required element is missing, the strategy speech is not educationally releasable.
