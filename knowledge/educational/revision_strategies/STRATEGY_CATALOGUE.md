## 1. Purpose

An expert IFoA tutor carries a mental catalogue of *kinds* of revision: reinforcing durability so knowledge survives spacing; strengthening retrieval under exam-like production; stabilising fragile knowledge that looks covered but breaks under variation; refreshing prerequisites that later work depends on; integrating related known topics into a usable network; consolidating exam-facing strength of what is already known; and maintaining healthy known material without inventing crisis.

This catalogue records those educational approaches so Revision Coach selection, transitions, and explainability share one vocabulary — while remaining consistent with the Educational Revision Model.

---

## 2. Catalogue Principles

1. **Educational first.** Strategies come from IFoA tutoring practice and consolidating learning principles — not from which feature flag or calendar trick is easiest.
2. **Approaches, not topic lists.** Strategies shape *how* known material is revised; MS001 priority still decides *which* material leads.
3. **Evidence-profile linked.** Each strategy declares the educational need profile it is built for.
4. **Objective-aware.** Each strategy primarily advances specific RVO-01…RVO-05 goods.
5. **Non-exclusive.** Needs may stack; prefer a **primary** strategy in speech.
6. **Reversible.** Strategies are provisional; they change as evidence and consolidating progress evolve.
7. **Non-punitive.** Names describe consolidating approaches — never character or moral deficit (“you forgot because you were lazy”).
8. **Plan-faithful.** Strategies preserve authorised revision-window meaning; they do not invent unofficial syllabus routes.
9. **Learning-Coach-humble.** Strategies refuse to compensate for deeper learning problems through endless consolidation loops.
10. **No fitness scores.** Qualitative matching only — never numeric strategy ranks as educational law.

## 3. Framework Logic (Before Labels)

Before naming a strategy, the tutor asks, in order:

| Order | Tutor question | If unclear |
|-------|----------------|------------|
| 1 | Is there a lawful revision warrant with prior exposure (not first learning / not Recovery-primary)? | Do not open a strategy |
| 2 | What primarily needs consolidating — durability, retrieval, fragile understanding, prerequisites, integration, exam-facing readiness, or ordinary maintenance? | Gather evidence; prefer gentler maintenance when the trail is healthy |
| 3 | Which RVO-XX goods must lead? | Do not select a strategy that optimises volume theatre |
| 4 | Does Learning Coach evidence suggest a deeper learning problem rather than consolidating need? | Escalate to Learning Coach; do not invent a strategy as diagnosis substitute |
| 5 | Can the approach stay inside current plan / day envelopes? | Prefer local strategy; escalate structural change honestly |

Examples such as “Durability Reinforcement” are **illustrative names** for approach types produced by this logic. Binding meanings are the definitions below.

## 4. Strategy Catalogue

Each entry records: educational meaning, suitable evidence profile, primary revision objectives, typical triggers, expected educational outcome, what it is not, and a constructive speech cue.

IDs (`RVS-XX`) exist for audit and cross-reference. They must not appear as student-facing jargon.

Detailed selection cards (evidence required, success signs, when another strategy is preferable) live in `STRATEGY_SELECTION.md`. Transition behaviour lives in `STRATEGY_TRANSITIONS.md`.

### RVS-01 — Durability Reinforcement

| Aspect | Provision |
|--------|-----------|
| **Educational meaning** | A consolidating approach that strengthens the likelihood previously demonstrated understanding persists across spacing — so known material survives the weeks between study and examination |
| **Suitable evidence profile** | Prior exposure exists; return / spacing trail shows durability risk or planned spaced return is the honest educational job; understanding was previously demonstrated but needs lasting strength |
| **Primary objectives** | RVO-02, then RVO-01 |
| **Typical triggers** | RVT-02 (planned spaced review); RVT-01 / RVT-04 when durability (not only same-day retrieval) is the leading concern |
| **Expected educational outcome** | Knowledge remains usable after intentional spacing; durability claims stay provisional and evidence-honest |
| **What it is not** | Cramming unlearned content; inventing catastrophic forgetting from one quiet week; a substitute for Learning Coach when genuine understanding never formed |
| **Speech cue** | “You’ve met this material before — we’ll return to it so it stays with you across time, not only on the day you first studied it.” |

---

### RVS-02 — Retrieval Strengthening

| Aspect | Provision |
|--------|-----------|
| **Educational meaning** | A consolidating approach that improves the student’s ability to recall and produce previously learned knowledge and methods without undue prompting — so known material is findable under exam conditions |
| **Suitable evidence profile** | Prior exposure exists; evidence shows weak or effortful retrieval on return, passive familiarity without production, or need for active recall of previously workable methods |
| **Primary objectives** | RVO-01, then RVO-02 / RVO-05 as warranted |
| **Typical triggers** | RVT-01 (forgetting / weak return); RVT-03 when exam-facing retrieval of known material is urgent; RVT-04 when fragility shows as retrieval failure |
| **Expected educational outcome** | Key results, definitions, and methods become more retrievable with less scaffolding than on recent weak return |
| **What it is not** | Passive re-reading labelled as retrieval; teaching an unread chapter; minting readiness from one same-day success |
| **Speech cue** | “We’ll practise bringing this back without the notes — so you can find it when you need it.” |

---

### RVS-03 — Fragile Knowledge Stabilisation

| Aspect | Provision |
|--------|-----------|
| **Educational meaning** | A consolidating approach that stabilises brittle previously learned understanding — material that looks “covered” but fails under spacing, variation, or mild exam pressure — without narrating it as brand-new first learning when prior exposure still holds |
| **Suitable evidence profile** | Prior exposure exists; same-day or thin success without durable grip; brittle pattern-matching; Learning Coach thin / falsely ready posture on consolidating candidates where consolidation (not obstacle diagnosis) remains the honest frame |
| **Primary objectives** | RVO-03, RVO-02, RVO-01 |
| **Typical triggers** | RVT-04 (fragile durability); often with RVT-01 |
| **Expected educational outcome** | Understanding becomes less brittle; the student can explain and apply known ideas under variation with evidence-honest consolidating speech |
| **What it is not** | Endless revision loops when Learning Coach diagnosis is required; teaching an entirely new syllabus unit; shame for ordinary fragility |
| **Speech cue** | “This topic was covered, but it’s still fragile — we’ll steady it so it holds under pressure.” |

---

### RVS-04 — Prerequisite Refresh

| Aspect | Provision |
|--------|-----------|
| **Educational meaning** | A consolidating approach that refreshes previously learned foundations that later authorised work depends on — so subsequent learning remains safe without replacing that later first learning |
| **Suitable evidence profile** | Prior exposure to the foundation exists; upcoming or current authorised work depends on it; return evidence or Profile / Learning Coach posture shows prerequisite fragility on studied foundations |
| **Primary objectives** | RVO-03, RVO-01 (supports later first learning without absorbing it) |
| **Typical triggers** | RVT-05 (prerequisite reinforcement) |
| **Expected educational outcome** | Foundations are firm enough for safe onward learning; prerequisite speech remains consolidating, not first-pass disguise |
| **What it is not** | Teaching the prerequisite for the first time under a revision label; expanding into unrelated new content; delaying all onward learning indefinitely as revision theatre |
| **Speech cue** | “Before we move on, we’ll refresh the earlier foundation this next work depends on — you’ve studied it; we’re making it safe again.” |

---

### RVS-05 — Concept Integration

| Aspect | Provision |
|--------|-----------|
| **Educational meaning** | A consolidating approach that connects previously learned topics that depend on, reinforce, or commonly appear together — so knowledge forms a usable network rather than isolated ticks |
| **Suitable evidence profile** | Prior exposure exists for each linked unit; integration gaps on return; curriculum or exam interdependence makes joint consolidation educationally coherent |
| **Primary objectives** | RVO-04, with RVO-01 / RVO-05 as warranted |
| **Typical triggers** | RVT-06 (topic interdependence); RVT-03 when exam papers reward connected use of known material |
| **Expected educational outcome** | The student can see and use related known ideas together; integration cites dependency / exam relevance without inventing new coverage |
| **What it is not** | Forcing joint revision when one topic lacks prior exposure; breadth-first new learning labelled as integration; mosaic of unlearned chapters |
| **Speech cue** | “You’ve studied these parts separately — we’ll practise how they work together, the way the exam often asks.” |

---

### RVS-06 — Examination Consolidation

| Aspect | Provision |
|--------|-----------|
| **Educational meaning** | A consolidating approach that maintains and sharpens exam-usable strength of *already learned* material as the sitting approaches — timed retrieval, method selection, and application under authentic conditions — without claiming guaranteed success |
| **Suitable evidence profile** | Prior exposure exists for the target scope; examination proximity and plan posture make exam-facing consolidation of known material the honest educational job; revision windows protect consolidating substance |
| **Primary objectives** | RVO-05, with RVO-01 / RVO-02 / RVO-04 as warranted |
| **Typical triggers** | RVT-03 (approaching examination); RVT-02 windows used for exam-facing consolidating substance |
| **Expected educational outcome** | Known material becomes more exam-usable under pressure; readiness speech remains provisional and evidence-honest |
| **What it is not** | Introducing new syllabus as “last-minute revision”; claiming “exam ready” from phase labels or volume; panic theatre that overrides prior-exposure honesty |
| **Speech cue** | “As the exam approaches, we’ll consolidate what you already know into exam-usable strength — not invent coverage you haven’t studied.” |

---

### RVS-07 — Maintenance Revision

| Aspect | Provision |
|--------|-----------|
| **Educational meaning** | A light consolidating approach that keeps healthy previously learned material from unnecessary decay — ordinary protective return without inventing crisis or opening an intensive reinforcement arc |
| **Suitable evidence profile** | Prior exposure exists; evidence shows reasonably durable known material; authorised spaced return or protected window calls for maintenance rather than intensive repair; confidence and foundations largely intact on this unit |
| **Primary objectives** | RVO-02, light RVO-01; RVO-05 only as proximity gently intensifies form |
| **Typical triggers** | RVT-02 when the honest job is keep-alive return; may follow successful RVS-01 / RVS-02 / RVS-03 once success signs emerge |
| **Expected educational outcome** | Healthy known material stays available without over-training or panic; student understands this is protection, not a verdict of failure |
| **What it is not** | A label for every quiet evening; a substitute when forgetting / fragility evidence clearly requires intensive RVS-01–RVS-03; a licence to skip Learning Coach when understanding never formed |
| **Speech cue** | “This is in good shape — a light return now keeps it that way without treating it as a crisis.” |

## 6. Explicit Non-Strategies (Refuse)

The following are **not** lawful entries in this catalogue:

| Unlawful pseudo-strategy | Why refused | Lawful alternative |
|--------------------------|-------------|--------------------|
| First-pass under revision label | No prior exposure | First learning / Daily Coach Learning Mode |
| Recovery catch-up as revision strategy | Disruption owns the moment | Recovery Coach |
| Obstacle cure by more revision loops | Deeper learning problem | Learning Coach diagnosis → intervention |
| Whole-syllabus cram including unlearned units | Invents coverage | Split: revision for known; first learning for unknown |
| Mastery minting / “exam ready” declaration | EIP-006 honesty | Provisional consolidating outcome speech |
| Numeric strategy optimiser as educational law | Non-scoring corpus | Qualitative selection in `STRATEGY_SELECTION.md` |
