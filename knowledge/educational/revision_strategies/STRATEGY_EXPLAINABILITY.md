## 1. Purpose

Students should never have to guess why Kwalitec chose *this kind* of revision — or feel that strategy changes are opaque mode switches, punishments for forgetting, or euphemisms for unfinished first-pass.

Strategy explainability specialises MS001 revision explainability so every material strategy decision answers — in plain educational language — why this approach, what evidence, what improvement, and how success is recognised.

Without strategy explainability:

- different consolidating approaches feel arbitrary;
- maintenance feels like demotion;
- fragile-knowledge speech feels like shame;
- exam consolidation feels like panic or false readiness;
- transitions feel like failure;
- Learning Coach escalation feels like abandonment;
- repeated revision feels like the only answer even when it is not.

With strategy explainability:

- the student trusts the consolidating tutor posture;
- warrant → approach → evidence → expected improvement → success recognition stays visible;
- strategy change and Learning Coach escalation are honest educational events;
- long-term retention purpose remains speakable.

Strategy speech must remain consistent with MS001 revision speech. It adds approach-type clarity; it does not invent a second prior-exposure story or a rival warrant.

## 3. Traceability Obligation (Architectural)

Every material strategy recommendation must be traceable through:

| Trace link | Student-facing role |
|------------|---------------------|
| **Prior exposure** | “You’ve studied this before when…” |
| **Why revise now** | “We’re returning to it now because…” |
| **Why this approach** | “The most educationally fitting revision approach is…” |
| **Evidence basis** | “Looking at your recent return / practice / spacing…” |
| **Expected improvement** | “If this goes well, you should be better able to…” |
| **How success is recognised** | “We’ll know this is working when…” |
| **Objectives link** | “The educational goal is to strengthen [retrieval / durability / understanding / integration / exam readiness]…” |
| **Boundary honesty** | “This is consolidation of material you already met…” / “This isn’t a revision approach — we need first learning / recovery / a learning diagnosis…” |
| **Long-term retention / plan faithfulness** | “This helps your knowledge stay usable through to the exam by…” / “This stays within your plan’s revision intent by…” |

Internal IDs (RVS-XX, RVT-XX, RVO-XX, RVP-XX) may exist for algorithms and audits. They must not appear as student-facing jargon.

A strategy recommendation with no prior-exposure link, no educational reason for *this* approach type, or no expected improvement / success recognition is invalid — even if the tip sounds motivating.

Architectural requirement restated:

> **Each strategy must be traceable to accumulated educational evidence and educational objectives.  
> Strategies may optimise how previously learned knowledge is reinforced —  
> they must not introduce first learning, bypass Recovery Coach, redefine mastery, or modify long-term planning —  
> and must say so honestly when Learning Coach escalation is required.**

## 4. Four-Question Framework (Strategy Specialisation)

Every material Revision Coach strategy recommendation must answer EIP-003’s four questions, specialised for strategy:

| EIP-003 question | Strategy specialisation |
|------------------|-------------------------|
| **What is known?** | What prior exposure exists; what return / practice / spacing evidence supports this approach |
| **What is estimated?** | Provisional judgements (e.g. likely fragility) — labelled as provisional |
| **Why this guidance?** | Why *this kind* of revision now; why not another approach; how it serves consolidating objectives |
| **What next?** | Concrete consolidating next step + expected improvement + how success will be recognised |

Students must hear all four — especially **why this approach**, **expected improvement**, and **success recognition** — not only a strategy label or topic name.

---

## 5. Required Student-Facing Elements

Every material strategy narration must include:

1. **Approach reason** — why this kind of revision was selected.  
2. **Evidence support** — what educational evidence supports it (brief, non-shaming).  
3. **Expected improvement** — what should get better if the approach is followed.  
4. **Success recognition** — how we will know the approach is working.  
5. **Consolidating honesty** — that this is reinforcement of previously learned material (or an honest handoff if not).  

Optional but encouraged when relevant:

- link to plan revision window / exam proximity without panic theatre;
- contrast with approaches *not* chosen (“not intensive repair — light maintenance”);
- transition cue when moving toward maintenance or Learning Coach.

## 6. Speech Patterns by Strategy (Constructive Cues)

| Strategy | Supportive speech pattern (illustrative) |
|----------|------------------------------------------|
| **RVS-01 Durability Reinforcement** | “You’ve met this before — we’ll return so it stays with you across time.” |
| **RVS-02 Retrieval Strengthening** | “We’ll practise bringing this back without the notes.” |
| **RVS-03 Fragile Knowledge Stabilisation** | “This was covered, but it’s still fragile — we’ll steady it so it holds under pressure.” |
| **RVS-04 Prerequisite Refresh** | “Before we move on, we’ll refresh the foundation the next work depends on.” |
| **RVS-05 Concept Integration** | “You’ve studied these parts separately — we’ll practise how they work together.” |
| **RVS-06 Examination Consolidation** | “We’ll consolidate what you already know into exam-usable strength — not invent coverage.” |
| **RVS-07 Maintenance Revision** | “This is in good shape — a light return keeps it that way.” |

Tone rules:

- Supportive, tutor-like, specific.  
- Never shame ordinary decay.  
- Never claim certainty of pass or mastery.  
- Never present maintenance as demotion or intensive repair as punishment.

## 7. Explaining Transitions

| Transition class | Student must hear |
|------------------|-------------------|
| **ST1 Maintenance after success** | What improved; why light keep-alive (or ordinary rhythm) is enough now; that this is not mastery declaration |
| **ST2 Strategy change** | What changed in the evidence / need; why the new approach fits; what improvement is now expected |
| **ST3 Learning Coach escalation** | Why revision alone is not enough; that a learning diagnosis / response is the honest next step; that this is support, not abandonment |
| **ST4 Recovery handoff** | That disruption continuity comes first; revision approaches pause or wait until recovery allows |
| **ST5 First-learning handoff** | That this is not revision yet because the material has not been studied |
| **ST6 Structural escalation** | That the plan / timetable needs adjustment beyond local revision approach change |
| **ST7 Hold / gather** | That we are checking carefully rather than inventing crisis or false completion |

Hard rule for ST3 speech:

> **Never imply that more of the same revision will fix a deeper learning problem  
> after evidence shows revision is insufficient.**

---

## 8. Forbidden Speech

Strategy explainability must **not**:

- use internal IDs (RVS-03, RVT-04) in student-facing copy;
- claim “exam ready” or redefine mastery from strategy compliance;
- present numeric strategy scores, urgency percentages, or optimiser ranks as educational truth;
- call unlearned content “revision” or “consolidation”;
- disguise Recovery catch-up as a revision approach;
- shame the student for ordinary forgetting or fragility;
- hide Learning Coach escalation behind endless “one more revision” promises;
- invent prior exposure or evidence that does not exist;
- narrate maintenance as failure or demotion;
- narrate exam consolidation as guaranteed success.

## 9. Worked Explainability Examples

### 9.1 Lawful — Retrieval strengthening

> “You’ve studied contingent life assurances before. On return this week, the methods were hard to find without notes. So we’re focusing on retrieval practice: bringing the results back actively. If this goes well, you should retrieve the key steps with less prompting. We’ll recognise progress when you can produce them more reliably on a spaced return — not because a checklist was ticked.”

### 9.2 Lawful — Maintenance after success

> “Your last returns on that chapter looked steadier, so we’re shifting to a light maintenance return — enough to keep it available, without treating it as a crisis. That doesn’t mean ‘exam ready forever’; it means the intensive reinforcement job has done enough for now.”

### 9.3 Lawful — Learning Coach escalation

> “We’ve tried consolidating this covered topic in more than one way, and it’s still not becoming usable understanding. Continuing to call it revision wouldn’t be honest. The next step is a learning diagnosis — figuring out what’s blocking progress — so we can respond properly rather than repeating revision that isn’t working.”

### 9.4 Unlawful — Exam consolidation theatre

> ~~“Revision mode selected: Examination Consolidation (score 92). You are exam ready for the whole syllabus.”~~  
> Unlawful: numeric theatre, mastery minting, possible coverage invention, internal jargon.

---

## 10. Audit Checklist (Before Student-Facing Release)

Before releasing strategy narration, confirm:

- [ ] Prior exposure stated or honest handoff used  
- [ ] Why *this* approach (not only why revise)  
- [ ] Evidence basis present and claim-typed  
- [ ] Expected improvement stated  
- [ ] Success recognition stated  
- [ ] RVO goods speakable without jargon IDs  
- [ ] No mastery / pass certainty  
- [ ] No Recovery / first-learning disguise  
- [ ] Learning Coach escalation spoken honestly when ST3 applies  
- [ ] Consistent with MS001 revision explainability  

If any required element is missing, the strategy speech is not educationally releasable.
