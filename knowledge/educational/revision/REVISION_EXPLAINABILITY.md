## 1. Purpose

Students should never have to guess why Kwalitec asks them to return to previously learned material — or feel that “revision” is a euphemism for unfinished first-pass or punishment catch-up.

Revision explainability exists so every material revision recommendation answers — in plain educational language — why this topic, what evidence, what consolidating outcome, and how it serves long-term retention and exam-usable strength.

Without revision explainability:

- revision feels arbitrary or like busywork;
- new content disguised as revision erodes trust;
- forgetting speech becomes shaming;
- priority among topics feels like an opaque optimiser;
- durability and readiness are overclaimed.

With revision explainability:

- the student trusts the consolidating tutor posture;
- prior exposure → warrant → priority → expected outcome stays visible;
- wrong-mode handoffs (first learning / recovery) are honest;
- long-term retention purpose remains speakable.

---

## 2. Traceability Obligation (Architectural)

Every material revision recommendation must be traceable through:

| Trace link | Student-facing role |
|------------|---------------------|
| **Prior exposure** | “You’ve studied this before when…” |
| **Why revise now** | “We’re returning to it now because…” |
| **Evidence basis** | “Looking at your recent return / practice / spacing…” |
| **Revision objectives** | “The educational goal is to strengthen [retrieval / durability / understanding / integration / exam readiness]…” |
| **Priority rationale** | “This comes before other known topics because…” |
| **Expected outcome** | “If this goes well, you should be better able to…” |
| **Long-term retention link** | “This helps your knowledge stay usable through to the exam by…” |
| **Boundary honesty** | “This is consolidation of material you already met…” / “This isn’t revision yet — it’s first learning / recovery…” |

Internal IDs (RVT-XX, RVO-XX, RVP-XX) may exist for algorithms and audits. They must not appear as student-facing jargon.

A revision recommendation with no prior-exposure link and no evidence / plan-window warrant is invalid — even if the tip sounds motivating.

Architectural requirement restated:

> **Revision recommendations must be traceable to accumulated educational evidence  
> and remain consistent with the Learning Coach, Recovery Coach and Canonical Study Plan.**

---

## 3. Four-Question Framework (Revision Specialisation)

Every material Revision Coach recommendation must answer EIP-003’s four questions, specialised for revision:

| EIP-003 question | Revision specialisation |
|------------------|-------------------------|
| **What is known?** | What prior exposure exists; what return / practice / spacing evidence exists |
| **What is estimated?** | Provisional judgements (e.g. likely fragility) — labelled as provisional |
| **Why this guidance?** | Why revise this topic now; why this priority among known material; how it serves long-term retention |
| **What next?** | Concrete consolidating next step + expected educational outcome |

Students must hear all four — especially **why this topic** and **expected outcome** — not only a topic name.

---

## 4. Mandatory Student Understandings

Each material recommendation must make the following understandable (not necessarily as four labelled headings in UI, but present in substance):

### 4.1 Why this topic is being revised

Explain the consolidating reason in educational language:

- forgetting / weak return on known material;
- planned spaced review / protected revision window;
- exam approach on already learned scope;
- fragile durability;
- prerequisite reinforcement for upcoming work;
- integrating related known topics.

Refuse vague speech such as “the algorithm selected this” or “you should revise everything.”

### 4.2 What evidence supports the recommendation

Cite educational evidence classes honestly:

- prior study / practice trail (exposure);
- spaced return or practice recency;
- Learning Coach longitudinal meaning where present;
- plan-authorised revision window as educational intent (not packing leftover).

Label estimates as estimates. Do not invent observations.

### 4.3 What educational outcome is expected

Name the consolidating good in student language:

- stronger recall under less prompting (RVO-01);
- knowledge that lasts across weeks (RVO-02);
- clearer grasp of why methods work (RVO-03);
- ability to connect related ideas (RVO-04);
- more exam-usable strength of what you already know (RVO-05).

Do not promise a pass or redefine mastery.

### 4.4 How revision contributes to long-term retention

Link today’s consolidating job to the longer journey:

- return strengthens memory traces that first learning alone does not finish;
- spacing and retrieval protect against decay before the sitting;
- integration and exam-facing practice make retained knowledge usable, not merely stored.

---

## 5. Tone and Integrity Rules

1. **Supportive, not shaming.** Ordinary forgetting is expected on long IFoA journeys; speech must not moralise decay.
2. **Consolidating, not demoting.** Revision is strengthening — not a verdict that the student “failed” first learning.
3. **Honest about mode.** If the need is first learning or recovery, say so clearly.
4. **No numeric theatre.** Do not present revision scores, urgency percentages, or mastery bars as proof.
5. **No certainty inflation.** Durability and exam readiness remain provisional where evidence is incomplete.
6. **Consistent with siblings.** Do not narrate consolidating advice that contradicts Learning Coach evidence or Recovery Coach restorative posture.
7. **Plan-faithful.** Do not imply the Revision Coach rewrote the student’s authorised plan.

---

## 6. Speech Patterns (Lawful vs Unlawful)

| Situation | Lawful speech | Unlawful speech |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|
| Weak spaced return on a studied topic | “When you last returned to [topic], retrieval was weaker than after you first practised it. Revisiting it now should strengthen recall so it lasts.” | “You’ve forgotten everything. Score: 12% retention.” |
| Protected revision window | “Your plan set aside this time to consolidate what you’ve already covered. We’ll focus on [topic] because it’s both fragile and important for later topics.” | “Random revision day — pick anything.” |
| Unread chapter requested as revision | “That chapter is still first learning for you. We’ll study it as new material, not as revision.” | “Let’s revise Chapter 12” (never studied) |
| Exam approaching | “As the sitting nears, we’ll consolidate exam-usable strength on topics you’ve already learned — starting with [topic] because…” | “You’re exam ready if you grind papers all week.” |
| Disruption primary | “After the break, we need to rebuild a sustainable study rhythm first; consolidating revision comes once continuity is stable.” | “Just revise hard to catch up.” |

---

## 7. Explaining Priority Without Scores

When multiple known topics compete, explain priority using RVP factors in plain language:

- durability / forgetting risk;
- prerequisite importance for upcoming work;
- curriculum centrality / interdependence;
- examination relevance of already learned material;
- strength of evidence;
- honesty of the current plan window.

Example pattern:

> “Both [A] and [B] are revision candidates because you’ve studied them. We’re prioritising [A] today because your last return showed it was more fragile, and it underpins [upcoming topic], which matters for the paper.”

Never:

> “A scored 87 urgency; B scored 64.”

---

## 8. Explaining Refusals and Handoffs

When Revision Coach refuses a revision frame, explainability still applies:

| Refusal / handoff | Student-facing substance |
|-------------------|--------------------------|
| No prior exposure | Why this is first learning, not revision |
| Recovery primary | Why restorative continuity comes first |
| Learning Coach diagnosis primary | Why consolidating tips wait on understanding the barrier |
| Protection conflict | Why protected revision cannot be stolen for first-pass without plan change |
| Thin evidence | Why Kwalitec will not invent urgency; what warrant would look like |

## 9. Relationship to Sibling Explainability

| Corpus | Relationship |
|--------|--------------|
| EIP-003 | Parent four-question standard — this document specialises it for revision |
| Daily Coach explainability | Day objective speech may consume revision rationale when today’s job is consolidating |
| Learning Coach explainability | Progression / obstacle speech remains authoritative; revision must not contradict it |
| Recovery explainability | Disruption / resume speech remains authoritative when recovery is primary |
| Study Plan explainability | Plan / window intent may be cited; Revision Coach does not rewrite plan speech |

---

## 10. Anti-Patterns

Forbidden explainability behaviours:

- “Because the system said so”
- Revision labelled without stating prior exposure
- Shame for ordinary spacing decay
- Mastery or pass promises from revision activity
- Numeric urgency / retention scores as educational proof
- Hiding that advice is actually first learning or recovery
- Contradicting Learning Coach or Canonical Study Plan protections in narration
