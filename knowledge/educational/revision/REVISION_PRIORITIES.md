## 1. Purpose

Students often face more previously learned material than one sitting can consolidate. An expert IFoA tutor does not assign opaque points. The tutor asks which consolidating job most protects durable, exam-usable understanding *now*.

This document records that qualitative priority reasoning so Revision Coach emphasis remains explainable and consistent with Programme VI authorities.

---

## 2. Binding Non-Scoring Rule

Revision priority **must not** be expressed as:

- a numeric revision score, urgency index, or decay percentage presented as educational truth;
- a black-box ranking that students cannot understand in educational language;
- a formula that redefines mastery, Learning Coach evidence, or Canonical Study Plan protections.

Internal implementation may later use proxies. Those proxies **must not redefine** the educational meanings here, and student-facing speech must remain qualitative and claim-typed (EIP-003).

---

## 3. Eligibility Before Priority

A topic enters the priority field only if:

1. Prior exposure exists (`REVISION_TRIGGERS.md` preconditions).
2. A lawful revision warrant (one or more RVT-XX) applies — or an authorised revision window is being filled with consolidating substance.
3. Revision Coach is the correct primary mode (not first learning; not Recovery Coach as primary).

Ineligible topics are not “low priority revision” — they are **out of revision field**.

## 4. Priority Factor Catalogue

An expert tutor weighs the following educational factors. Factors are **influences on judgement**, not additive points.

### RVP-01 — Durability posture

**Meaning.** How fragile or robust is retention of this previously learned material, based on accumulated evidence of return, spacing, and practice recency?

**Raises priority when:**

- Spaced return is weak or absent despite prior success
- Fragile durability (RVT-04) or forgetting evidence (RVT-01) is clear
- Profile decay risk is elevated on covered material

**Lowers priority when:**

- Recent return shows durable retrieval on this unit
- Other candidates are far more fragile and time is scarce

**Must not:** Convert durability into a single scalar as educational law; invent catastrophic decay from thin evidence.

---

### RVP-02 — Prerequisite importance

**Meaning.** How far does safe progress on upcoming authorised learning depend on consolidating this previously learned foundation?

**Raises priority when:**

- Imminent plan work depends on this foundation (RVT-05)
- Weak prerequisites would make next first-pass unsafe or hollow

**Lowers priority when:**

- No near-term dependency exists and other exam-critical known material is more fragile

**Must not:** Use prerequisite language to smuggle first-time teaching of unlearned foundations into revision priority.

---

### RVP-03 — Curriculum dependency

**Meaning.** How central is this unit in the official syllabus structure — as a hub that other previously learned topics rely on, or as a connector in the curriculum graph?

**Raises priority when:**

- The unit unlocks or stabilises multiple dependent previously learned topics
- Interdependence (RVT-06) makes consolidating a hub more valuable than an isolated leaf topic

**Lowers priority when:**

- The unit is peripheral relative to the student’s authorised exam scope and other hubs are at risk

**Must not:** Ignore official syllabus structure in favour of engagement theatre; invent dependency not present in curriculum ordering.

---

### RVP-04 — Examination relevance

**Meaning.** How strongly does consolidating this previously learned material serve authentic performance on the target professional examination (methods commonly assessed, high-yield known topics, exam-facing application)?

**Raises priority when:**

- Exam approaches (RVT-03) and the unit is known *and* exam-usable
- Integration or application of this known material is commonly decisive in papers
- Maintaining exam readiness (RVO-05) honestly depends on this consolidating job

**Lowers priority when:**

- The unit is previously learned but low-yield for the sitting relative to other fragile known material
- “Relevance” would be used as an excuse to skip necessary prerequisite reinforcement

**Must not:** Claim examination relevance for unlearned content; equate past-paper volume with educational priority law.

---

### RVP-05 — Evidence strength and recency of warrant

**Meaning.** How well-supported is the consolidating need by Educational Evidence quality and Learning Coach longitudinal reading?

**Raises priority when:**

- Multiple congruent observations support forgetting / fragility
- Learning Coach progression meaning honestly flags decay or thin durability on this unit

**Lowers / pauses priority when:**

- Evidence is thin — prefer gathering warrant or following planned spacing over invented urgency
- Learning Coach diagnosis indicates an obstacle that must be addressed before consolidation theatre

**Must not:** Override Learning Coach evidence to force a preferred revision topic; treat a single noisy sitting as decisive without context.

---

### RVP-06 — Plan window and protection honesty

**Meaning.** Does the Canonical Study Plan currently authorise consolidating attention here — including protected revision windows — without stealing first-pass or recovery capacity unlawfully?

**Raises priority when:**

- The present window is intended for revision / consolidation of this (or this class of) previously learned material
- Using the window for this unit preserves protection honesty

**Lowers / forbids as revision priority when:**

- Selecting the topic would consume protected revision for unfinished first-pass
- Recovery capacity / restorative posture should own the sitting instead

**Must not:** Reorder the plan by Revision Coach fiat; treat leftover empty cells as automatic highest priority.

---

## 5. How an Expert Tutor Combines Factors

There is **no numerical weighting scheme** in this Model. The tutor combines factors through educational narrative:

1. **Exclude** ineligible topics (no prior exposure; wrong mode).
2. **Name the warrant** (which RVT-XX apply).
3. **Ask what fails first if we do nothing consolidating today** — usually durability/forgetting on exam-critical known material, or prerequisite fragility that endangers the next lawful job.
4. **Prefer hubs over leaves** when curriculum dependency and interdependence make one consolidating pass protect many known topics.
5. **Intensify exam-facing form** of already eligible topics as the sitting approaches — without expanding the field to unlearned content.
6. **Defer** when Learning Coach diagnosis or Recovery Coach disruption is primary.
7. **Explain** the ordering in plain language (see `REVISION_EXPLAINABILITY.md`).

Illustrative combination patterns (not scores):

| Pattern | Typical priority posture |
|---------|--------------------------|
| Fragile durability + high exam relevance on a known hub | Strong consolidating priority now |
| Planned spaced review + strong recent retrieval on a leaf topic | Lower priority; maintain light return or skip for frailer hubs |
| Prerequisite importance for tomorrow’s authorised first-pass | Raise reinforcement of the known foundation before new learning |
| High exam relevance but no prior exposure | **Out of field** — first learning, not revision priority |
| Any pattern during meaningful disruption primary | **Defer** to Recovery Coach |

---

## 6. Relationship to Daily Coach Selection

Revision priority produces **emphasis meaning** among consolidating candidates.  
Daily Coach still decides **whether revision is today’s primary objective** under the Canonical Study Plan and day gates.

| Layer | Question |
|-------|----------|
| Revision Coach priorities | Among lawful revision candidates, what deserves consolidating attention and why? |
| Daily Coach | Given plan, capacity, protections, and sibling inputs, what is most educationally valuable *today*? |

Revision Coach must not silently replace Daily Coach’s day-priority selection with an internal priority list.

---

## 7. Anti-Patterns

Forbidden prioritisation behaviours:

- Ranking by engagement metrics, streaks, or content popularity
- Always revising the most recent topic regardless of durability evidence
- Always revising the oldest topic regardless of exam relevance and plan windows
- Preferring easy wins that invent false readiness
- Preferring hardest topics as punishment
- Numeric leaderboards of “revision urgency”
- Using priority speech to override Learning Coach or replace Recovery Coach
