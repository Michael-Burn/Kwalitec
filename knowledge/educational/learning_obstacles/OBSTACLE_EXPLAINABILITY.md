## 1. Purpose

Students should never have to guess why Kwalitec thinks their learning is stuck — or feel blamed for it.

Obstacle explainability exists so every material diagnosis answers — in plain educational language — what barrier is believed, what trail supports it, how sure we are, and what to do next to improve.

Without obstacle explainability:

- stalls feel like unexplained judgement;
- interventions feel arbitrary or punitive;
- symptoms are narrated as character flaws;
- thin history is dressed as confident blame.

With obstacle explainability:

- the student trusts the diagnostic tutor posture;
- causes are separated from surface struggle;
- confidence postures stay honest;
- next improvement steps are actionable and hopeful without false certainty.

## 2. Traceability Obligation (Architectural)

Every material Learning Obstacle diagnosis must be traceable through:

| Trace link | Student-facing role |
|------------|---------------------|
| **Syllabus scope** | “For [topic / area]…” |
| **Symptoms (plain)** | “What’s showing up is…” |
| **Obstacle (cause)** | “The educational barrier looks like…” |
| **Learning objective(s) blocked** | “That’s mainly holding back [understanding / retrieval / application / retention / exam technique]…” |
| **Accumulated evidence** | “Because across recent study / practice / return…” |
| **Confidence posture** | “This is provisional / well supported / still unclear…” |
| **Next improvement step** | “So the next step is…” (intervention only if diagnosis gate is open) |

Internal IDs (LOB-XX, LO-XX, LI-XX, LPS-XX, EV-XX, EC-X) may exist for algorithms and audits. They must not appear as student-facing jargon.

A diagnosis with no evidence trail (or no explicit insufficient-warrant statement) is invalid — even if the explanation sounds motivating.

**Architectural requirement restated:**

> No intervention may be recommended without an explicit educational diagnosis.  
> Evidence, diagnosis, and intervention remain separate layers in speech as in doctrine.

---

## 3. Explainability Principles

1. **Improvement over judgement.** Name barriers as solvable educational problems.
2. **Symptom ≠ cause in speech.** Say what was observed, then what it likely means.
3. **Three answers every time.** What barrier; what evidence; what next improvement step.
4. **Facts and estimates stay distinct.** Completions and recorded outcomes are facts of observation; obstacle diagnoses are estimates / provisional judgements.
5. **Prefer one primary barrier.** Avoid multi-LOB dumps; add secondary only for honesty.
6. **Speak confidence honestly.** Provisional and insufficient-warrant language is dignified.
7. **Conflict is spoken.** Do not hide rival explanations when posture is Conflicting.
8. **Interventions labelled as guidance.** Only after diagnosis; not Mission law unless Daily Coach / plan authority makes them so.
9. **No score theatre.** No obstacle severity percentages or diagnostic scores.
10. **EIP-003 hierarchy.** Advice / estimate / fact claim types remain visible.
11. **Align with progression explainability.** Obstacle speech should not contradict Learning Coach progression narration on the same trail.

---

## 4. Four-Question Framework (Obstacle Specialisation)

Aligned to EIP-003’s four-question explainability framework:

| Question | Obstacle obligation |
|----------|---------------------|
| **What do we know?** | Observable symptoms and recorded evidence facts (coverage, practice outcomes, returns, cadence) |
| **What do we estimate?** | The educational barrier (LOB meaning in plain words) and confidence posture |
| **Why this diagnosis?** | Plain link from evidence pattern → cause (and rivals if Conflicting) |
| **What next?** | Improvement step — evidence gathering if under-warranted; otherwise primary intervention in plain language |

---

## 5. Required Narrative Elements

Every material student-facing obstacle explanation should include:

1. **Scope** — which syllabus area the diagnosis concerns.
2. **Constructive framing** — learning hard professional material often hits barriers; this is normal coaching.
3. **Symptom in plain words** — what is showing up.
4. **Barrier (diagnosis)** — the educational cause in plain words — or explicit “too early to diagnose.”
5. **Evidence support** — at least one concrete trail reference.
6. **Claim-type honesty** — estimate / provisional wording for the diagnosis.
7. **Next improvement step** — one clear action (gather evidence or diagnosed intervention).

Optional when useful:

- which kind of learning is blocked (understanding vs retrieval vs exam form);
- explicit non-claims (“this doesn’t mean you can’t do actuarial work…”);
- rival explanation when Conflicting;
- link to overall Profile only when it honestly colours the story.

## 6. Language Rules

### 6.1 Preferred vocabulary

| Prefer | Avoid |
|--------|-------|
| “The barrier looks like missing foundations on earlier ideas” | “You’re behind because you’re lazy” |
| “You recognise this with support, but it isn’t retrieving cleanly” | “You don’t know anything” |
| “There’s a specific misunderstanding to fix” | “You keep failing for no reason” |
| “Your study rhythm is too uneven for learning to settle” | “You lack discipline” |
| “We’re asking for too much at once” | “You can’t cope” |
| “Your effort is there — the method isn’t matching the exam” | “You’re studying wrong / stupidly” |
| “Your confidence and results don’t match yet” | “You’re delusional” / “You’re hopeless” |
| “We don’t yet have enough practice evidence” | “You refuse to work” |
| “We need to return to earlier topics so they hold” | “You forgot everything — typical” |
| “Exam technique still needs deliberate practice” | “You’ll never pass timed papers” |
| “Too early to diagnose the barrier — let’s gather clearer evidence” | Invented cause for coaching energy |
| “So the next step is…” | Opaque “optimise your deficit profile” |

### 6.2 Forbidden speech patterns

- Character judgements, shame, or punishment framing.
- Inferring understanding, durability, or exam-readiness obstacles from completion or streaks alone.
- Presenting a single session as longitudinal diagnostic proof.
- Soft confidence narrated as proven content incapacity — or as mastery.
- Numeric obstacle scores or severity theatre as educational truth.
- Recommending interventions without stating the diagnosis (or insufficient-warrant gathering).
- Collapsing evidence, diagnosis, and intervention into one unexplained tip.
- Engineering or optimiser jargon as the reason for the barrier belief.
- Silent contradiction of Progression or Profile speech on the same trail.

### 6.3 Constructive tone requirements

- **Specific** — name the educational barrier, not a vague “struggle.”
- **Forward-looking** — always include an improvement step.
- **Proportionate** — match confidence posture; do not overclaim.
- **Respectful of effort** — acknowledge work when sessions occurred, even if growth is thin.
- **Hopeful without theatre** — barriers are normal and addressable; do not promise a pass.

## 7. Speech Patterns by Confidence Posture

| Posture | Speech pattern |
|---------|----------------|
| **Insufficient warrant** | “For [scope], we can see [symptom], but it’s too early to name the educational barrier confidently. Next step: [specific evidence to gather].” |
| **Provisional** | “For [scope], the leading explanation is [barrier] — still provisional because [limit]. Next step: [matched improvement / further check].” |
| **Supported** | “For [scope], evidence across [trail] consistently points to [barrier]. That mainly holds back [objective]. Next step: [matched intervention in plain words].” |
| **Conflicting** | “For [scope], two explanations still fit: [A] and [B]. We won’t guess. Next step: [distinguishing evidence].” |

## 8. Good and Bad Examples

### 8.1 Prerequisite gap (Supported)

**Good:**  
“For contingent probabilities, practice keeps failing on steps that use earlier set-theory ideas. Across recent sessions, that pattern points to a foundation gap — so understanding of the new topic can’t settle yet. Next step: rebuild those earlier ideas, then return to the current questions.”

**Bad:**  
“You’re bad at probability. Do more questions.”  
*(Judgement; skips diagnosis honesty; intervention without named cause.)*

---

### 8.2 Weak retrieval (Provisional)

**Good:**  
“For formula work on this unit, you do better when notes are open than when you reconstruct from memory. That suggests a retrieval barrier — still provisional until we try a few more closed-book recalls. Next step: short unaided reconstruction practice.”

**Bad:**  
“Memory score: 32%. Critical deficit.”  
*(Score theatre; non-educational language.)*

---

### 8.3 Misconception

**Good:**  
“On annuity-factor questions, the same incorrect setup keeps appearing even when the numbers change. That looks like a specific misunderstanding to correct before more volume will help. Next step: repair that setup, then try mixed practice.”

**Bad:**  
“Stop getting these wrong.”  
*(Symptom as command; no cause; shaming.)*

---

### 8.4 Insufficient warrant

**Good:**  
“You’ve covered the early chapters, and a couple of questions went poorly, but we don’t yet have enough practice history to diagnose why. Next step: a short focused practice set so we can see whether this is foundations, method, or simply needing more attempts.”

**Bad:**  
“Clearly a prerequisite crisis — rewrite your whole plan.”  
*(Overclaim; collapses layers; escalates without warrant.)*

---

### 8.5 Intervention without diagnosis (unlawful)

**Bad:**  
“Do more spaced repetition this week.”  
*(May be a fine activity — but as Learning Coach obstacle speech it is unlawful without stating the barrier it responds to, e.g. revision neglect or fragile durability.)*

**Good:**  
“We’re moving through topics without returning, so durable knowledge can’t form — that’s revision neglect on this trail. Next step: schedule returns to the units you covered last month.”

## 9. Relationship to Intervention Speech

When the diagnosis gate is open:

1. State the **obstacle** first.
2. State the **intervention** as the improvement response second.
3. Keep wording that shows sequence: barrier → response.
4. Do not claim the obstacle has cleared because the intervention was *recommended*.

When the gate is closed (insufficient warrant / conflicting):

1. State that diagnosis is not ready.
2. Recommend **evidence gathering** only.
3. Do not dress gathering as a pretend cure.

## 10. Integrity Boundaries

| Boundary | Lawful | Unlawful |
|----------|--------|----------|
| Tone | Constructive, specific, hopeful | Shame, character judgement |
| Layers | Evidence / diagnosis / intervention visible in narrative | Single opaque tip |
| Certainty | Match confidence posture | Supported theatre from thin history |
| Scores | Refuse | Obstacle severity % |
| Progression alignment | Consistent with Learning Explainability | Contradict LPS speech silently |
| Clearing | Require new evidence before “barrier gone” speech | Declare cured because advice was accepted |
