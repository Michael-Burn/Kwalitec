## Authority

This document is the constitutional overview of the **Learning Obstacle Model** for Kwalitec.

It is subordinate to the Educational Constitution and specialised educational models. It governs **how Kwalitec diagnoses barriers to genuine learning progression**. It does not authorise implementation shortcuts that contradict the Constitution, and it does not invent evidence meaning absent from the Educational Evidence Model.

Authority order for obstacle diagnosis:

> Constitution defines educational truth and curriculum primacy.  
> Knowledge & Mastery defines coverage ≠ understanding ≠ mastery.  
> Evidence Model defines what may warrant understanding claims.  
> Learning Progression Model asks whether the student is genuinely learning.  
> **This Learning Obstacle Model asks why progression is blocked, stalled, uneven, or misleading — before any intervention.**  
> Learning Interventions respond only after an explicit diagnosis (MS003 Learning Intervention Framework).  
> Downstream coaching, profile evolution, and narration surfaces must consume this educational reasoning — never invent a cure from a symptom.

## 1. Purpose

Kwalitec coaches students preparing for demanding professional examinations (especially IFoA syllabi such as CM1/CS1 and peers).

An expert IFoA tutor who sees stalled or uneven growth does not leap to generic advice. After reading the trail of study and practice, the tutor answers one diagnostic educational question:

> **“Why is this student’s learning not progressing as expected?”**

That answer must rest on **accumulated educational evidence**, distinguish **symptoms from underlying educational causes**, remain consistent with the Student Educational Profile and Learning Progression posture, and **precede** any intervention recommendation.

This document records that tutor posture so every future Learning Coach subsystem has a single educational reference for *how obstacles are identified*.

> **The Learning Obstacle Model describes educational diagnosis.  
> It does not implement Runtime A logic, scoring, analytics, or Twin writers.**

---

## 2. What a Learning Obstacle Is

A **learning obstacle** is a provisional educational diagnosis of a **barrier that prevents, slows, or misrepresents genuine learning progression** on one or more learning objectives (coverage through exam readiness).

| Concept | Definition | Primary question |
|---------|------------|------------------|
| **Symptom** | An observable pattern that something is wrong or incomplete (e.g. repeated wrong answers, rising coverage without practice, missed study days). | What is going wrong on the surface? |
| **Obstacle (diagnosis)** | The best educational explanation of *why* that pattern exists (e.g. prerequisite gap, misconception, weak retrieval). | What barrier best explains the trail? |
| **Intervention** | The educational response warranted by that diagnosis (e.g. prerequisite review, misconception repair, retrieval emphasis). | What should change educationally? |

Symptoms invite investigation.  
Obstacles name causes.  
Interventions respond to causes.

Collapsing these three is educationally unlawful:

- treating “getting questions wrong” as the diagnosis (it is usually a symptom);
- recommending “do more practice” without knowing whether the barrier is volume, method, foundations, or misconception;
- narrating an intervention as if it were evidence that the obstacle has already gone.

## 3. What the Learning Obstacle Model Is

The **Learning Obstacle Model** is the educational capability that interprets **accumulated Educational Evidence** — together with progression posture, the Student Educational Profile, and reflection outputs — and produces an **explicit educational diagnosis** of barriers to progression.

| Input family | Role |
|--------------|------|
| **Accumulated Educational Evidence** | Observed educational happenings across sessions (EIP-002; Learning Evidence Model) |
| **Progression judgement / posture** | Whether learning is progressing, stalling, accelerating, inconsistent, or under-warranted (MS001) |
| **Student Educational Profile** | Current multi-axis educational diagnosis of *who the student is now* |
| **Session history & Reflection outputs** | What recent sittings produced educationally |
| **Syllabus / curriculum context** | Official scope and lawful prerequisite order |

into:

| Output family | Role |
|---------------|------|
| **Obstacle diagnosis** | Named educational obstacle(s) — or explicit insufficient warrant to diagnose |
| **Diagnostic confidence posture** | Qualitative strength of the diagnosis (provisional / supported / conflicting) — never a numeric score |
| **Symptom → cause account** | Plain distinction between what was observed and what is believed to explain it |
| **Obstacle explainability** | Constructive student-facing account focused on improvement |
| **Diagnosis gate for intervention** | Authorisation that a Learning Intervention may now be recommended |

It answers:

| Question | Educational answer |
|----------|-------------------|
| Why is progression stalled or uneven? | Named obstacle(s) when warrant exists |
| Is this a symptom or a cause? | Explicit differentiation under the Diagnosis document |
| How sure are we educationally? | Qualitative confidence posture — never a score |
| What evidence supports or weakens this? | Trace to evidence classes and accumulation patterns |
| May we recommend an intervention yet? | Only after explicit diagnosis (or explicit “gather more evidence”) |

The Learning Obstacle Model is:

- **diagnostic** — names educational barriers, not character flaws;
- **evidence-honest** — refuses certainty from thin or completion-only trails;
- **cause-seeking** — digs beneath symptoms;
- **non-scoring** — qualitative confidence only;
- **pre-intervention** — diagnosis precedes recommendation;
- **explainable** — constructive language, improvement-focused;
- **layer-faithful** — never collapses evidence, diagnosis, and intervention;
- **objective-faithful** — keeps completion ≠ understanding ≠ retrieval ≠ application ≠ durable knowledge ≠ exam readiness.

The Learning Obstacle Model is **not**:

- a second Progression Model, Evidence Model, Profile diagnostician, or Intervention catalogue;
- a licence to blame the student for missed ticks;
- ownership of Educational Evidence recording or Twin write authority;
- a UI surface, analytics dashboard, or scoring engine;
- a claim that naming an obstacle guarantees a pass.

## 4. Educational Purpose

The Learning Obstacle Model exists so that:

1. **Barriers are named before cures are offered** — generic advice without diagnosis is forbidden coaching theatre.
2. **Symptoms are not mistaken for causes** — wrong answers, missed days, and low confidence invite different investigations.
3. **IFoA preparation obstacles are educationally specific** — actuarial study fails in patterned ways (foundations, retrieval under exam form, misconception, revision neglect).
4. **Trust is preserved** — students hear constructive explanations focused on improvement, not judgement.
5. **Interventions become traceable** — every Learning Intervention must point back to an identified educational obstacle (or to an explicit insufficient-warrant gathering step).

## 5. Core Responsibilities

The Learning Obstacle Model is educationally responsible for:

| Responsibility | Meaning |
|----------------|---------|
| **Diagnose educational barriers** | Form a provisional answer to “Why is learning not progressing as expected?” |
| **Separate symptoms from causes** | Keep observable patterns distinct from underlying educational explanations |
| **Respect evidence law** | Consume EIP-002 / Learning Evidence Model; never mint evidence by diagnosis |
| **Gate interventions** | Allow Learning Interventions only after explicit diagnosis (or explicit evidence-gathering posture) |
| **Qualify diagnostic confidence** | Speak provisional / supported / conflicting — never invent numeric certainty |
| **Explain constructively** | Make the diagnosis speakable as improvement guidance |

It is **not** educationally responsible for:

- inventing Educational Evidence;
- writing Twin estimates by itself;
- selecting or executing Runtime A missions;
- defining intervention meanings (owned by the Learning Intervention Framework — MS003);
- producing opaque “obstacle severity” scores as educational proof.

## 6. Binding Educational Doctrine

1. **Evidence ≠ diagnosis ≠ intervention.** These three layers remain separate. No document, product surface, or future algorithm may collapse them into a single opaque label.
2. **No intervention without diagnosis.** A Learning Intervention recommendation is unlawful unless it cites an explicit obstacle diagnosis — or cites an explicit *insufficient warrant* posture whose only lawful next step is further evidence gathering.
3. **Symptoms are not diagnoses.** Observable struggle invites investigation; the named obstacle is the educational cause.
4. **Completion ≠ understanding ≠ mastery.** Obstacle reading must preserve the Learning Objectives ladder (LO-01…LO-07) and EIP-006 separations.
5. **Absence is uncertainty.** Thin history yields cautious or withheld diagnoses — never confident blame dressed as diagnosis.
6. **Profile consistency.** Obstacle diagnoses must remain consistent with the Student Educational Profile; material contradictions require Profile re-consultation, not silent override.
7. **Progression context.** Obstacles explain *why* a progression posture (stall, inconsistency, decay, thin warrant, false readiness risk) is occurring — they do not replace progression states.
8. **Explainability is mandatory.** Silent obstacle theatre and shaming language are forbidden (EIP-003).
9. **Non-punitive.** Obstacles are educational signals — never character judgements or sanctions.

## 7. Four Constitutional Layers (Binding)

```
Layer 1 — EVIDENCE
  Observed educational happenings + accumulation patterns
  (EIP-002; Learning Evidence Model EC-A…EC-F)
        ↓  interpret; never invent
Layer 2 — DIAGNOSIS
  Named learning obstacle(s) + qualitative confidence posture
  (this Model; Catalogue; Diagnosis; Obstacle Evidence)
        ↓  respond; never rewrite evidence
Layer 3 — INTERVENTION
  Educational recommendation matched to the diagnosis
  (Learning Intervention Framework MS003; LI-00…LI-14)
        ↓  observe again; never declare success from compliance alone
Layer 4 — FUTURE EVIDENCE
  New observations after the response is attempted
  (feeds Evidence Model; may clear, revise, or escalate diagnosis)
```

| Lawful flow | Unlawful flow |
|-------------|----------------|
| Evidence → Diagnosis → Intervention → Future evidence | Evidence → Intervention (skip diagnosis) |
| Diagnosis cites evidence trail | Diagnosis invents observations |
| Intervention cites diagnosis ID / meaning | Intervention invents a cause post hoc |
| Insufficient warrant → gather evidence | Insufficient warrant → pretend a cure |
| Future evidence re-reads success honestly | “You followed advice, therefore you improved” |

## 8. Relationship to Neighbouring Educational Models

### 8.1 Learning Progression Model (MS001)

| Model | Question |
|-------|----------|
| Progression | Is the student genuinely learning? |
| **Obstacles** | **Why not — or why unevenly?** |

Progression postures (stall, inconsistency, decay, thin warrant, false readiness risk) are **diagnostic invitations**. They are not themselves obstacle diagnoses.

Example: LPS-S (Progression Stall) is a posture. The obstacle might be LOB-01 Prerequisite Gap, LOB-03 Misconception, LOB-08 Insufficient Practice, or another — decided under Diagnosis + Evidence.

### 8.2 Learning Intervention Framework (MS003)

Interventions are **downstream**. This Model does not redefine LI-00…LI-14. It requires that each recommended intervention be traceable to an obstacle diagnosis documented here. Governing meanings: [`../learning_interventions/`](../learning_interventions/).

### 8.3 Educational Evidence Model (EIP-002) and Learning Evidence Model

Obstacle diagnosis **consumes** evidence meaning. It does not redefine Quality Levels, source catalogue (EV-01…), or claim lawfulness. Diagnosis is an **inference posture** over accumulated Evidence — never a renaming of completion events as causes.

### 8.4 Student Educational Profile

The Profile answers: *Who is this student educationally now?*  
Obstacles answer: *What barrier currently blocks progression on this scope?*

Profile dimensions (planning reliability, recovery posture, practice readiness) may **colour** obstacle likelihood; they do not replace per-scope educational diagnosis.

### 8.5 Daily Coach / Session / Reflection

Reflection may surface session-level struggle signals (symptoms and soft hypotheses).  
The Learning Obstacle Model forms **longitudinal educational diagnoses** across the trail — without absorbing Daily Coach plan-faithfulness duties or rewriting the Canonical Study Plan.

## 9. Tutor Posture (Binding Metaphor)

When diagnosing obstacles, the system must behave as an expert IFoA tutor would:

1. **Notice the symptom without stopping there.** Wrong answers, missed days, and rising coverage are clues — not the diagnosis.
2. **Ask what kind of learning is failing.** Coverage? Understanding? Retrieval? Application? Retention? Exam form?
3. **Check foundations before volume.** Prerequisite gaps and misconceptions beat “do more questions” as first hypotheses when patterns suggest them.
4. **Separate confidence from competence.** Felt certainty and performance may diverge.
5. **Test alternative explanations.** Prefer the cause that best fits the whole trail; name residual uncertainty.
6. **Withhold diagnosis when history is thin.** “We need clearer practice evidence” is honest tutoring.
7. **Name the barrier without shame.** Obstacles are normal in hard professional exams.
8. **Only then recommend a change.** Intervention follows diagnosis.

## 11. Cold Start and Sparse History

Cold-start and sparse-history students still receive obstacle treatment — a **cautious** one.

Rules:

1. With little or no Educational Evidence, the Model may narrate activity/coverage symptoms and **must withhold** confident underlying-cause diagnoses for understanding, retrieval, application, retention, or exam technique.
2. A short streak of failed or incomplete sessions is **insufficient** for a strong named obstacle.
3. Prefer “too early to diagnose the barrier” / “we need more educational evidence” over invented causes.
4. Profile cold-start caution and Evidence thin-history rules take precedence over dramatic obstacle copy.

Lawful next step under insufficient warrant: **evidence gathering** (not a pretended intervention cure).

This Model succeeds when:

1. An expert tutor reading this corpus recognises a coherent obstacle-diagnosis doctrine for IFoA preparation.
2. Symptoms cannot lawfully be narrated as underlying educational causes without differentiation.

5. Obstacle explanations are constructive, improvement-focused, and evidence-traceable.
