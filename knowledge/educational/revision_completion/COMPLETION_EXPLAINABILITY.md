> why revision is considered successful (or not yet),  
> what evidence supports that conclusion,

> Explanation is supportive — never judgement.  
> Explainability never invents educational certainty.  
> Completion never implies first learning or mastery.**

## 1. Purpose

Students should never have to guess whether consolidating revision is over — or feel that “you’re done revising” was invented from a session counter or checklist.

Completion explainability specialises MS001 revision explainability and MS002 strategy explainability so every material completion judgement answers — in plain educational language — why success (or not), what evidence, what changes next, and what maintenance or monitoring remains.

Without completion explainability:

- ending intensive revision feels arbitrary or premature;
- continuing revision feels like punishment or busywork;
- students confuse strengthened existing knowledge with mastery or first learning;
- maintenance feels unexplained or like distrust;
- strategy change or Learning Coach escalation feels like failure;
- Daily Coach return feels unexplained.

With completion explainability:

- the student trusts the consolidating tutor posture;
- warrant → strategy → completion → next step stays visible;
- first-learning and mastery honesty are preserved;
- confidence in existing learning is strengthened without false certainty.

## 3. Traceability Obligation (Architectural)

Every material completion judgement must be traceable through:

| Trace link | Student-facing role |
|------------|---------------------|
| **Prior exposure / consolidating context** | “We’ve been strengthening material you already studied when…” |
| **Why success / not yet** | “We can lighten revision because…” / “We’re not ready to call this complete because…” |
| **Evidence basis (brief)** | “Looking at your recent returns / retrieval / how these topics work together…” |
| **Explicit non-grounds (when useful)** | “This isn’t just because a week passed / sessions were ticked / you finished a checklist…” |
| **What happens next** | “From here, a light keep-alive return…” / “Ordinary daily guidance resumes…” / “We’ll change the revision approach…” / “We need learning-focused coaching…” |
| **Maintenance / monitoring remaining** | “We’ll keep a light return so it stays with you…” / “No special intensive revision watch beyond ordinary coaching…” |
| **First-learning / mastery honesty** | “This means your existing knowledge is stronger — not that everything is mastered, and not that new topics were learned as revision.” |

Internal IDs (RvCC-XX, RvCE-XX, RvCT-XX, RVS-XX) may exist for algorithms and audits. They must not appear as student-facing jargon.

Architectural requirement restated:

> **Revision completion must be justified by accumulated educational evidence.  
> It strengthens confidence in existing learning and must never be interpreted as first learning or mastery.**

A completion message with no evidence link — or one that cites only attendance, ticks, repetition counts, or elapsed time — is invalid.

---

## 4. Four-Question Framework (Completion Specialisation)

Every material completion judgement must answer EIP-003’s four questions, specialised for completion:

| EIP-003 question | Completion specialisation |
|------------------|---------------------------|
| **What is known?** | What consolidating retrieval / durability / integration / prerequisite / exam-usable observations accumulated on previously learned material |
| **What is estimated?** | Provisional judgements (e.g. residual fragility; provisional readiness) — labelled as provisional |
| **Why this guidance?** | Why completion is affirmed or withheld; why this RvCT transition |
| **What next?** | Concrete next coaching posture + maintenance / monitoring statement |

Students must hear all four — especially **why successful / not yet**, **what happens next**, and **whether maintenance or monitoring remains** — not only “back to normal.”

---

## 5. Required Student Understandings

Material completion speech must make the following understandable:

### 5.1 Why revision is considered successful (or not yet)

**When successful (RvCT-01 / RvCT-02):**

- Name strengthened educational conditions in plain language (retrieval, durability, integration, foundation stability, exam-usable strength as relevant).
- Tie success to consolidating *existing* knowledge — never to inventing new coverage.
- State clearly that intensive revision can lighten, maintenance can begin, or ordinary guidance can resume.

**When not yet:**

- Name which consolidating conditions are still missing, without moral framing.
- Prefer concrete educational gaps (“retrieval is still fragile on return”) over vague “not ready.”
- If Learning Coach escalation is required, say that revision alone is not enough — without shame.

### 5.2 What evidence supports that conclusion

- Brief, plain account of the trail (recent returns, retrieval improvement, joint use of known topics, foundation refresh where relevant).
- Distinguish known observations from provisional estimates.
- Where useful, say what was *not* treated as proof (time alone, ticks alone, question count alone).
- Never invent observations to make a tidy story.

### 5.3 What happens next

| Transition | Student-facing cue (illustrative posture, not copy-deck) |
|------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| **RvCT-01** | Light keep-alive / maintenance revision so this stays with you |
| **RvCT-02** | Ordinary daily guidance resumes under your plan |
| **RvCT-03** | We change the revision approach to better fit what we’re seeing |
| **RvCT-04** | Revision alone isn’t enough — learning-focused coaching takes the lead |
| **RvCT-05** | We continue with light watch / keep gathering a clearer picture |

### 5.4 Whether continued monitoring or maintenance revision is recommended

- If maintenance (RvCT-01) is recommended, name it as protective keep-alive of *existing* strength — not as unfinished crisis.
- If residual risk exists under RvCT-05, name it specifically and supportively (e.g. durability after fragile return).
- If no special revision watch remains, say that ordinary coaching vigilance is enough — do not invent probation.
- Monitoring / maintenance must not sound like distrust or punishment.

### 5.5 What completion does *not* mean

Material success speech must not allow the student to reasonably conclude that:

- topics are mastered because revision ended;
- new syllabus content was learned under a revision label;
- the exam is now assured;
- fragility history was erased;
- intensive revision is still owed “to be safe” after declaring success;
- Learning Coach problems were solved by finishing revision sittings.

## 6. Tone and Language Rules

1. **Supportive, never judgement.** Completion is coaching wisdom, not a moral verdict.  
2. **Hopeful and specific.** “Retrieval on this known chapter is stronger across returns” beats “good job surviving revision mode.”  
3. **Strengthen confidence in existing learning.** Affirm consolidating progress without inventing mastery.  
4. **No mastery ceremony.** Forbid “fully mastered,” “exam ready for sure,” or “learned this for the first time via revision.”  
5. **No timer or quota speech.** Forbid “your ten revision sessions are up” or “your revision week expired.”  
6. **No streak / checklist speech as proof.** Streaks and checklists may be UX chrome; they are not educational evidence in student explanation.  
7. **Honest Learning Coach escalation.** RvCT-04 is care for genuine learning problems, not abandonment or failure theatre.  
8. **Honest strategy change.** RvCT-03 is clearer consolidating route, not demotion.  
9. **Close with next step.** Every completion message names what happens next educationally.  
10. **Protect consolidating honesty** especially when withholding RvCT-01 / RvCT-02.

## 7. Worked Explainability Sketches (Non-Normative Copy)

These illustrate posture. Product copy may differ; educational obligations may not.

### 7.1 RvCT-01 maintenance after successful retrieval strengthening

> You’ve been returning to material you already studied, and your retrieval is stronger across those returns — not just on a single day. We can move to a lighter keep-alive approach so it stays with you. That doesn’t mean it’s mastered; it means the intensive consolidation job has done enough for now.

### 7.2 RvCT-02 return to ordinary Daily Coach guidance

> The consolidating work on those known topics has done its job: durability looks better across spaced returns, and we no longer need intensive revision as the lead story. Ordinary daily guidance under your plan can resume. We’ll still coach lawfully, including any protected revision windows your plan already includes — without treating this as unfinished first learning or mastery.

### 7.3 RvCT-01 with light RvCT-05 monitoring

> Your return on that fragile chapter has steadied enough for light maintenance rather than intensive drills. We’ll keep a gentle eye on durability over the next stretch — not as a test, just to make sure the strengthen holds.

### 7.4 Not yet — RvCT-05 gather

> You’re consolidating again, which matters. We’re not calling this complete yet because the trail after such fragile returns is still thin — especially for the foundation topics we refreshed. Next we keep the current approach and gather clearer evidence before we change posture.

### 7.5 RvCT-03 alternative revision strategy

> Retrieval practice helped, but what we’re seeing now points more to connecting related topics you’ve already studied than to single-topic recall. We’ll shift the revision approach to practise how those known ideas work together. Same destination; clearer consolidating route. This isn’t a demotion.

### 7.6 RvCT-04 Learning Coach escalation

> We’ve given consolidating revision a fair attempt on this covered topic, and the pattern still looks like a deeper learning problem rather than ordinary forgetting. The honest next step is learning-focused coaching — not another loop of the same revision. Finishing more revision sittings would not honestly fix this.

### 7.7 Forbidden sketches

> ~~Your revision period has expired.~~  
> ~~You completed eight revision sessions — objective achieved.~~  
> ~~You’re fully revised and back to mastery.~~  
> ~~You learned this chapter for the first time through revision — well done.~~  
> ~~We’ll keep intensive revision indefinitely to be safe.~~  
> ~~Others have revised more, so you’re not done.~~  
> ~~Checklist finished — exam ready.~~

## 8. Surface Obligations

Any student-facing surface that presents revision completion must:

1. Answer the four EIP-003 questions as specialised above.  
2. Make §5 understandings available without requiring the student to infer them.  
3. Keep internal IDs invisible.  
4. Refuse mastery / first-learning / exam-certainty speech from completion.  
5. Refuse time-only / attendance-only / tick-only / repetition-count-only justifications.  
6. Align with Daily Coach explainability after RvCT-02 — do not keep a parallel intensive revision narrator.  
7. Remain consistent with MS001 / MS002 prior-exposure, warrant, and strategy stories already told.  
8. When recommending maintenance or monitoring, make that recommendation explicit and supportive.

---

## 9. Anti-Patterns (Forbidden)

- Silent completion with no evidence account  
- Completion celebrated as mastery graduation or first-learning success  
- “Monitoring” framed as probation or distrust  
- Learning Coach escalation framed as personal failure  
- Strategy change framed as the student “failing revision”  
- Dual messaging: “complete” while still demanding intensive crisis volume  
- Maintenance spoken as unfinished punishment  
- Jargon dumps (RvCC, RvCE, RvCT, RVS, Twin, scores)
