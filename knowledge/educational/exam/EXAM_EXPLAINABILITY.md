## 1. Purpose

Students should never have to guess why Kwalitec is shifting toward examination preparation — or feel that “exam prep” is a euphemism for unfinished first-pass, unfinished revision, or punishment catch-up.

Examination explainability exists so every material Exam Coach recommendation answers — in plain educational language — why preparation is changing, what evidence supports it, what assessment-facing outcome is expected, and how that contributes to examination performance.

Without examination explainability:

- exam prep feels arbitrary or like panic theatre;
- new content or unfinished revision disguised as exam prep erodes trust;
- readiness speech becomes certainty or shame;
- priority among emphases feels like an opaque optimiser;
- contribution to examination performance is unspeakable.

With examination explainability:

- the student trusts the assessment-facing tutor posture;
- warrant → evidence → priority → expected outcome stays visible;
- wrong-mode handoffs (first learning / revision / recovery) are honest;
- examination performance purpose remains speakable without pass promises.

## 2. Traceability Obligation (Architectural)

Every material examination preparation recommendation must be traceable through:

| Trace link | Student-facing role |
|------------|---------------------|
| **Why preparation is changing** | “We’re shifting toward examination preparation because…” |
| **Evidence basis** | “Looking at your learning / revision / plan window / sitting timing…” |
| **Examination objectives** | “The educational goal is to strengthen [demonstration / exam planning / conditions / confidence / sitting decisions]…” |
| **Priority rationale** | “This comes before other exam-prep emphases because…” |
| **Expected outcome** | “If this goes well, you should be better able to…” |
| **Examination performance link** | “This helps your examination performance by…” |
| **Boundary honesty** | “This is assessment preparation of material you’ve already learned and revised…” / “This isn’t exam prep yet — it’s first learning / revision / recovery…” |

Internal IDs (EPT-XX, EXO-XX, EXP-XX) may exist for algorithms and audits. They must not appear as student-facing jargon.

An examination preparation recommendation with no prior-preparation honesty and no evidence / plan-window warrant is invalid — even if the tip sounds motivating.

Architectural requirement restated:

> **The Exam Coach prepares learners for assessment using accumulated educational evidence.  
> Recommendations must remain consistent with the Learning Coach, Recovery Coach, Revision Coach, and Canonical Study Plan — without redefining their educational meaning.**

## 3. Four-Question Framework (Examination Specialisation)

Every material Exam Coach recommendation must answer EIP-003’s four questions, specialised for examination preparation:

| EIP-003 question | Examination specialisation |
|------------------|----------------------------|
| **What is known?** | What learning / revision / continuity evidence exists; what sitting context is bound |
| **What is estimated?** | Provisional judgements (e.g. likely conditions fragility) — labelled as provisional |
| **Why this guidance?** | Why preparation is changing now; why this priority among assessment-facing emphases; how it serves examination performance |
| **What next?** | Concrete assessment-facing next step + expected educational outcome |

Students must hear all four — especially **why preparation is changing** and **expected outcome** — not only a mock or topic name.

## 4. Mandatory Student Understandings

Each material recommendation must make the following understandable (not necessarily as four labelled headings in UI, but present in substance):

### 4.1 Why preparation is changing

Explain the assessment-facing reason in educational language:

- approaching examination window;
- completion of planned revision;
- examination registration / sitting binding;
- readiness review findings;
- final preparation period.

Refuse vague speech such as “the algorithm selected this” or “you should just do more past papers.”

### 4.2 What evidence supports it

Cite educational evidence classes honestly:

- Learning Coach longitudinal meaning where present;
- Revision Coach warrant / strategy / completion meaning where present;
- Recovery Coach posture when relevant (including why exam prep is *not* primary);
- plan-authorised examination-facing window as educational intent (not packing leftover);
- sitting timing / registration as context — not as proof of readiness.

Label estimates as estimates. Do not invent observations.

### 4.3 What educational outcome is expected

Name the assessment-facing good in student language:

- better able to show what you already know under exam conditions (EXO-01);
- a clearer plan for the examination period and sitting (EXO-02);
- steadier performance under timed / format constraints (EXO-03);
- more justified confidence — not false certainty (EXO-04);
- better decisions inside the paper (EXO-05).

Do not promise a pass, a mark, or redefine mastery.

### 4.4 How it contributes to examination performance

Link today’s assessment-facing job to sitting performance:

- demonstration practice makes accumulated understanding usable under pressure;
- examination planning protects time and attention for known strengths;
- conditions management reduces avoidable performance loss on known material;
- sitting decision-making helps apply what is already prepared wisely;
- confidence grounded in evidence supports calm execution without inventing coverage.

## 5. Tone and Integrity Rules

1. **Supportive, not shaming.** Exam nerves and ordinary gaps are speakable; speech must not moralise anxiety or decay.
2. **Assessment-facing, not demoting.** Examination preparation is approach coaching — not a verdict that earlier learning “failed.”
3. **Honest about mode.** If the need is first learning, revision, or recovery, say so clearly.
4. **No numeric theatre.** Do not present readiness scores, predicted marks, or pass probabilities as educational proof.
5. **No certainty inflation.** Readiness and examination performance contribution remain provisional where evidence is incomplete.
6. **Consistent with siblings.** Do not narrate exam advice that contradicts Learning Coach evidence, Revision Coach completion meaning, or Recovery Coach restorative posture.
7. **Plan-faithful.** Do not imply the Exam Coach rewrote the student’s authorised plan or educational strategy.

## 6. Speech Patterns (Lawful vs Unlawful)

| Situation | Lawful speech | Unlawful speech |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|
| Revision complete; exam window opens | “Your plan set aside this period to prepare for the sitting. We’ll focus on demonstrating [known topics] under timed conditions because revision has already strengthened them.” | “Random exam grind — do every past paper.” |
| Unread chapter requested as exam prep | “That chapter is still first learning for you. We’ll study it as new material, not as exam preparation.” | “Let’s exam-prep Chapter 12” (never studied) |
| Fragile known hubs still consolidating | “Those topics still need consolidating revision before exam approach is the main job. We’ll keep revision primary there.” | “Skip revision — just mock everything.” |
| Exam approaching | “As the sitting nears, we’ll practise how you approach the paper with material you’ve already learned and revised — starting with [emphasis] because…” | “You’re exam ready if you grind papers all week.” |
| Disruption primary | “After the break, we need to rebuild a sustainable study rhythm first; examination preparation comes once continuity is stable.” | “Just exam-prep hard to catch up.” |
| Student wants a predicted mark | “I won’t invent a mark from one mock. What we can say is which known areas are ready to practise under conditions, and which still need learning or revision.” | “You’re on track for a 65.” |

## 7. Explaining Priority Without Scores

When multiple assessment-facing emphases compete, explain priority using EXP factors in plain language:

- educational readiness honesty;
- syllabus coverage honesty (known vs unlearned);
- durable understanding;
- revision evidence / completion;
- assessment timing;
- strength of evidence and plan-window honesty.

Example pattern:

> “Both timed practice and sitting-order planning would help. We’re prioritising timed practice on [known topic] today because revision evidence shows it’s durable enough to rehearse under conditions, and the sitting is close enough that conditions management matters more than another planning discussion.”

Never:

> “Readiness scored 87; conditions urgency scored 64.”

---

## 8. Explaining Refusals and Handoffs

When Exam Coach refuses an examination-preparation frame, explainability still applies:

| Refusal / handoff | Student-facing substance |
|-------------------|--------------------------|
| No prior learning | Why this is first learning, not exam prep |
| Revision still primary | Why consolidating revision comes before assessment approach |
| Recovery primary | Why restorative continuity comes first |
| Learning Coach diagnosis primary | Why exam tips wait on understanding the barrier |
| Protection / plan conflict | Why examination-facing windows cannot be stolen for first-pass without plan change |
| Thin evidence | Why Kwalitec will not invent readiness; what warrant would look like |
| Certainty request | Why marks / pass promises are refused |

---

## 10. Anti-Patterns

Forbidden explainability behaviours:

- “Because the system said so”
- Exam prep labelled without stating why preparation is changing
- Shame for ordinary exam nerves or incomplete coverage honesty
- Mastery, mark, or pass promises from preparation activity
- Numeric readiness / predicted-mark scores as educational proof
- Hiding that advice is actually first learning, revision, or recovery
- Contradicting Learning Coach, Revision Coach, Recovery Coach, or Canonical Study Plan protections in narration
- Redefining sibling coach meanings in student speech
