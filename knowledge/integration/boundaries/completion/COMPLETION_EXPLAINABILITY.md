## 1. Purpose

An expert IFoA tutor who cannot say *which boundaries* were assessed — which governance constraints were validated, which responsibility separations were confirmed, which findings were published, and which limits remained intact — is not closing honestly; they are performing.

After Programmes VI–X have made meaning, ownership, tips, context, contracts, evidence, governance, and execution speech speakable, and after WS3 / MS001–MS002 have made boundary and lifecycle speech speakable, Programme XI still needs one boundary-assessment-completion-speech answer:

> **“Which boundaries were assessed, which governance constraints were validated, which responsibility separations were confirmed, which findings were published, and which constitutional boundaries remained intact?”**

That answer must cover every material affirmed (or refused) completion without inventing educational success, creating constitutional authority, redefining constitutional responsibilities, reinterpreting constitutional meaning, amending constitutional specifications, transferring authority, minting tips, inventing state, rewriting specifications, or freezing a stack as law.

## 2. What Boundary Integrity Completion Explainability Is

**Constitutional Boundary Integrity Completion Explainability** is the constitutional obligation to narrate **fulfilment (or lawful refusal of fulfilment) of the required constitutional boundary integrity assessment lifecycle** — not to invent a second tutor voice from CI complete badges, and not to grade the student, create constitutional authority, rewrite responsibilities, or amend constitutional specifications.

| Concept | Definition | Primary question |
|---------|------------|------------------|
| **Boundaries assessed** | Named constitutional boundary statements evaluated under CBI law | Which boundaries were assessed? |
| **Governance constraints validated** | Named authority limits and behavioural may / must / must-not outcomes | Which governance constraints were validated? |
| **Responsibility separations confirmed** | Named duty allocations that remained separated as published | Which responsibility separations were confirmed? |
| **Findings published** | Named boundary integrity findings with lawful dispositions | Which findings were published? |
| **Boundaries intact** | Explicit confirmation that forbidden overclaims / mutations / reinterpretations did not occur | Which constitutional boundaries remained intact? |

Boundary Integrity Completion Explainability is:

- **fulfilment-centred** — it narrates why CBIC criteria hold (or why close is refused);
- **catalogue-bound** — every justification cites published CBI / CBIL / CBIC identities where applicable;
- **corpus-bound** — every justification cites published specifications, named boundaries, governance constraints, responsibility separations, and named findings;
- **non-legislative** — explanation never becomes a back door to create authority or amend corpora;
- **non-mutating** — explanation never narrates completion as responsibility rewrite, constraint softening, or layer absorption;
- **finding-honest** — explanation never silently upgrades published findings;
- **quality-honest** — complete is never narrated as mastery, readiness, or educational quality;
- **technology-honest** — explanation never presents a stack preference as constitutional law;
- **audience-aware** — student speech and developer/auditor traces differ in vocabulary, not in truth;
- **layer-honest** — it does not replace Programme VI tip warrant speech, Programme VII ownership / workflow / state speech, EIP-002 evidence meaning speech, Programme VIII contract / evidence / validation speech, Programme IX evaluative speech, Programme X execution speech, MS001 boundary speech, or MS002 lifecycle speech;
- **audit-aligned** — explanation components match CBIC reconstruction needs;
- **authority-honest** — explanations must not paper over authority inference or stack canonisation.

Boundary Integrity Completion Explainability is **not**:

- a rewrite of Constitution / EIP / Programme VI–X / WS3 meaning;
- a quality report on learning, coaching, or exam readiness;
- a constitutional authority instrument or amendment warrant;
- a silent responsibility-rewrite, constraint-softening, or finding-upgrade mechanism;
- a Programme IX evaluative-finish report as constitutional law;
- a substitute for WS3 / MS001 boundary explainability or WS3 / MS002 lifecycle explainability (compose with; do not replace);
- a substitute for Programme XI WS1 CDIC or WS2 CCIC completion explainability (compose with; do not collapse);
- a substitute for Programme IX CTC / CVC / CCC / CRTC completion explainability (compose with; do not collapse);
- a rendering system, UI template, CI badge, graph visualiser, or analytics dashboard;
- a claim that clear completion speech guarantees learning, a pass, conformity, constitutional amendment, or responsibility rewrite;
- a licence to present string scanners, lint scores, ticket graphs, or pipeline greens as constitutional proof or educational quality.

## 3. Mandatory Explanation Questions

Every material completion judgement (affirm or refuse close) should answer all five questions:

### CBICQ-01 — Which boundaries were assessed?

Name the primary constitutional boundary statement identities evaluated under the assessment concern — including applicable CBI-01…CBI-07 categories and authority / responsibility / behavioural / consumption / production / layer outcomes — or name which required boundaries remain unassessed when refusing close. Never narrate invented boundaries as lawful assessment subjects. “The corpus was scanned somewhere” is not a constitutional boundary identity.

### CBICQ-02 — Which governance constraints were validated?

Name the primary authority-limit and behavioural may / must / must-not identities and outcomes validated under the concern — or name which required governance constraints remain unvalidated when refusing close. Include lawful failed / deferred / escalated governance outcomes honestly. Never narrate invented authority as validated governance, and never narrate silently softened “must not” as lawful constraint validation. “Looks governed enough” is not a constitutional governance-constraint identity.

### CBICQ-03 — Which responsibility separations were confirmed?

Name the primary responsibility-boundary identities and duty-owner outcomes confirmed under the concern — or name which required separations remain unconfirmed when refusing close. Include lawful failed / deferred / escalated responsibility outcomes honestly. Never narrate absorbed, transferred, or dual-claimed duties as confirmed separations. “Looks separated enough” is not a constitutional responsibility-separation identity.

### CBICQ-04 — Which findings were published?

Name the primary boundary integrity finding identities and dispositions published under the concern (intact / violated / deferred / escalated) — or name which required findings remain unpublished when refusing close. Never narrate a violated / deferred / escalated finding as upgraded by completion, and never narrate a silent replacement of a prior lawful finding. “Looks intact enough” is not a constitutional finding identity.

### CBICQ-05 — Which constitutional boundaries remained intact?

Confirm the hard stops in `COMPLETION_BOUNDARIES.md` that were checked — especially non-creation of constitutional authority, non-redefinition of constitutional responsibilities, non-alteration of constitutional constraints, non-reinterpretation of constitutional meaning, non-amendment of constitutional specifications, implementation neutrality, repeatability honesty, and non-equation of completion with educational success, Programme IX evaluative completion, WS1 / WS2 assessment completion, or constitutional truth.

## 5. Audience Rules

| Audience | May hear | Must not hear as educational law |
|----------|----------|-----------------------------------|
| **Student** | Plain speech that a boundary check finished (or did not), without syllabus rewrite claims | “You’re exam-ready,” “the law changed,” “responsibilities moved,” “this stack is now the syllabus,” “everything is permanently intact forever” |
| **Developer** | Named corpus identities, CBI / CBIL / CBIC IDs, boundary / governance / responsibility / finding refs, refuse reasons | “Ship because assessment-complete,” “rewrite responsibilities in main to match the badge,” “freeze this framework as constitutional” |
| **Auditor** | Reconstructable CBIC-01…CBIC-05 outcomes, CBICQ answers, boundary preservation, continuity honesty | Mute “complete” badges, invented boundaries, invented authority, silent finding upgrades, silent responsibility rewrites |

Student-facing and auditor-facing projections **must share one constitutional truth**. Vocabulary may differ; facts may not.

## 6. Forbidden Explanations

The following explanation patterns are constitutionally defective:

- “Assessment complete, therefore constitutional authority now resides in the scanner / pipeline / completion badge.”
- “Assessment complete, therefore we redefined responsibilities to clear collisions.”
- “Assessment complete, therefore must-not became may.”
- “Assessment complete, therefore Programme VI / EIP meaning now means X.”
- “Assessment complete, therefore constitutional specifications are amended.”
- “Assessment complete, therefore the student is exam-ready / mastered / succeeded.”
- “Assessment complete, therefore the implementation is conformant / verified / compliant / certified.”
- “Assessment complete because CI was green / the folder looked bounded / the ticket closed.”
- “Governance was validated somehow” without named constraint identity.
- “Responsibility separation was confirmed somehow” without named separation identity.
- “Findings were published somehow” without named finding identity and disposition.
- Silent upgrade of violated / deferred / escalated findings into intact under close narration.
- Narrating a preferred stack, language, framework, or vendor as constitutional law because assessment closed.
- Collapsing CBIC completion into CDIC / CCIC / CTC / CVC / CCC / CRTC completion (or vice versa) without distinction.
