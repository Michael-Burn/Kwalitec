## 1. Purpose

Boundary integrity without a closed catalogue invents law by proximity: whichever folder adjacency, “common sense limit,” or pipeline annotation happens to exist becomes the tutor’s “proof the constitution is bounded.”

This catalogue names the only lawful constitutional boundary categories an assessment may apply — and binds each category to purpose, scope, inputs, outputs, validation rules, and permitted / prohibited behaviours.

**Hard constraint:** This document never creates constitutional authority, never redefines responsibilities, never alters published constraints, never amends constitutional specifications, and never invents new domain boundary statements under validation pretext.

**Relation to CT / CC / CV / CCM / CRT / CDI / CCI:** Those catalogues remain defined solely by their owners. CBI categories *validate constitutional boundary consistency*; they do not replace lineage preservation, fidelity evaluation, verification, compliance determination, certification recognition, dependency validation, or catalogue validation.

**Relation to educational quality:** No CBI type judges whether learning was good, whether a tip was wise, or whether the student is ready. Intact ≠ quality. Intact ≠ conformant. Intact ≠ certified.

## 3. Global Validation Rules

These rules apply to every CBI category:

| Rule ID | Validation rule |
|---------|-----------------|
| **CBIR-01 Authority fidelity** | Every material authority boundary must remain reconstructable to its published owning corpus. Silent authority absorption by a subordinate horizon is a defect. |
| **CBIR-02 Responsibility fidelity** | Every material responsibility boundary must retain its published duty owner. Silent duty transfer or dual exclusive ownership without published warrant is a defect. |
| **CBIR-03 Constraint fidelity** | Published “may”, “must”, and “must not” statements remain as published. Silent softening, contradiction without published precedence, or selective ignoring is a defect. |
| **CBIR-04 Consumption honesty** | A corpus may consume only artefacts / meanings its published consumption boundaries permit. Inventing consumption rights under integrity pretext is unlawful. |
| **CBIR-05 Production honesty** | A corpus may produce only artefacts / findings its published production boundaries permit. Inventing production rights under integrity pretext is unlawful. |
| **CBIR-06 Layer / isolation fidelity** | Boundaries must honour Constitution / EIP → VI → VII → VIII → IX → X → XI and lawful workstream / milestone subordination. Layer absorption is a defect. |
| **CBIR-07 Catalogue closure** | Only CBI-01…CBI-07 may classify constitutional boundary integrity assessments. Unpublished customs are unlawful. |
| **CBIR-08 Non-mutation** | Validation never creates, softens, invents, deletes, or soft-amends constitutional boundaries, responsibilities, constraints, or specifications. Findings report; they do not rewrite. |
| **CBIR-09 Neutrality** | Validation criteria speak to constitutional identities and published boundaries — never “must use Flask / SQLAlchemy / grep / GitHub Actions.” |
| **CBIR-10 Finding honesty** | Dispositions are intact / violated / deferred / escalated only. “Close enough,” “temporary may,” or “demo exception” are not constitutional dispositions. |

## 4. CBI-01 — Authority Boundaries

### Constitutional purpose

Validate lawful **limits on who may define, own, or decide** under published constitutional law — so no subordinate corpus silently becomes foundational or peer authority without published warrant.

### Constitutional scope

Published authority statements across Constitution / EIP and Programme VI–XI corpora (including `*_BOUNDARIES.md` authority maps and ownership corpora). Does **not** create new authority or elevate architecture / Version 2 preference docs to constitutional authority.

### Constitutional inputs

- Published authority / ownership / defining-right statements.
- Named corpus identities claiming or citing authority.
- Published CBIO-01 / CBIO-04 expectations.

### Constitutional outputs

- Authority-boundary finding under CBI-01.
- Named authority-owner note (who may define / own / decide).
- Audit-speakable confirmation that authority was not absorbed (true/false).

### Validation rules (category-specific)

- Every material constitutional corpus under Programme VI–XI must reconstruct authority claims to published superior law (Constitution / EIP and/or published programme owners).
- Edges or statements that treat architecture, product roadmap, or Runtime A docs as Constitution / EIP peers are defects when used as foundational authority.
- Findings may note missing authority citation; they may not invent an authority amendment.

### Permitted behaviours

- Confirm Programme VI meaning corpora retain educational defining authority as published.
- Confirm Programme VII ownership corpora retain ownership disposition authority as published.
- Confirm Programme XI assurance corpora remain validators — never foundational authors — as published.

### Prohibited behaviours

- Treat a Programme XI finding as a Constitution / EIP amendment or new defining authority.
- Soft-elevate Version 2 / Adaptive / Twin design docs to constitutional authority via boundary prose.
- Invent unpublished authority customs as peer law.
- Use CBI-01 to create or transfer constitutional authority.

## 5. CBI-02 — Responsibility Boundaries

### Constitutional purpose

Validate lawful **allocation and separation of constitutional responsibilities** — so published duties are not absorbed by foreign horizons under integration, readiness, or “helpfulness” pretext.

### Constitutional scope

Published responsibility statements (what a corpus must / must not do; duty maps; non-responsibility clauses). Does **not** reassign duties or invent dual exclusive owners.

### Constitutional inputs

- Published responsibility / non-responsibility statements.
- Horizon / workstream / milestone owning identities.
- Published CBIO-02 expectations.

### Constitutional outputs

- Responsibility-boundary finding under CBI-02.
- Named duty-owner and non-absorption notes.
- Collision notes when two corpora claim exclusive responsibility for the same duty without published composition warrant.

### Validation rules (category-specific)

- Exclusive responsibilities remain with their published owners.
- Composition of responsibilities is lawful only when published as composition — never as silent takeover.
- Assurance horizons (Programme XI) may validate responsibility structure; they may not absorb the duties they validate.

### Permitted behaviours

- Confirm Programme VIII runtime corpora do not absorb Programme VI educational meaning responsibility.
- Confirm Programme IX conformance corpora do not absorb Programme X decision responsibility.
- Confirm Programme XI / WS3 boundary integrity does not absorb Constitution / EIP or Programme VI–X authorship responsibilities.

### Prohibited behaviours

- Reassign Programme VI tip warrant responsibility to Programme XI findings.
- Let completion / lifecycle successors absorb the meaning responsibility of their MS001 predecessors.
- Invent dual exclusive ownership to paper over a collision.
- Use CBI-02 to rewrite responsibility maps in situ.

## 6. CBI-03 — Behavioural Constraints

### Constitutional purpose

Validate lawful **“may”, “must”, and “must not” constraints** across the published corpus — so constraint statements remain consistent, reconstructable, and unsoftened.

### Constitutional scope

Published behavioural constraint statements in Constitution / EIP and Programme VI–XI corpora. Does **not** invent new constraints or soften existing ones for delivery convenience.

### Constitutional inputs

- Named may / must / must-not statements and their owning corpora.
- Published precedence / composition rules where they exist.
- Published CBIO-03 expectations.

### Constitutional outputs

- Constraint-boundary finding under CBI-03.
- Constraint-consistency notes (consistent / contradicted / incomplete).
- Softening / contradiction disclosures when detected.

### Validation rules (category-specific)

- A “must not” remains a hard prohibition unless a published amendment or published precedence rule lawfully qualifies it.
- Contradictory may / must / must-not statements on the same behaviour without published resolution are defects.
- Assessment may report missing constraint identity; it may not invent a temporary “demo may.”

### Permitted behaviours

- Compare “must never amend specifications” statements across sibling assurance corpora for consistency.
- Confirm a Programme VIII “must not invent educational meaning” constraint survives alongside Programme XI validation rights.
- Report deferred when constraint statements are incomplete for the assessment scope.

### Prohibited behaviours

- Soften “must not” into “may” so a release ships.
- Treat operational practice (“we sometimes skip this”) as a constraint amendment.
- Invent unpublished precedence to force intact status.
- Use CBI-03 to rewrite constraint text in situ.

## 7. CBI-04 — Consumption Boundaries

### Constitutional purpose

Validate lawful **limits on which artefacts, meanings, or findings a corpus may consume** — so consumption remains consumption, never reverse authorship.

### Constitutional scope

Published consumption rights and limits (what a corpus may read / cite / use as input). Does **not** invent new consumption rights or convert consumption into authorship.

### Constitutional inputs

- Consumer corpus identity + claimed consumed artefact / meaning identities.
- Published consumption boundary statements.
- Published CBIO-04 / CBIO-01 expectations.

### Constitutional outputs

- Consumption-boundary finding under CBI-04.
- Lawful-consumption confirmation or violation note.
- Authorship-reversal disclosures when consumption is used as defining power.

### Validation rules (category-specific)

- Consumers may use published superior / peer artefacts only as published consumption boundaries allow.
- Consumption of findings never converts the consumer into the producer’s authority.
- Missing published consumption right → deferred / violated — never invent-in-place permission.

### Permitted behaviours

- Confirm Programme IX compliance may consume Programme VIII verification / evidence artefacts as published.
- Confirm Programme XI boundary integrity may consume Programme VI–X boundary statements as assessment subjects.
- Confirm Programme X explainability may consume audit artefacts as published.

### Prohibited behaviours

- Treat consumption of a superior corpus as licence to redefine that corpus.
- Invent consumption of unpublished tribal artefacts as constitutional input.
- Convert “we read their docs” into “we now own their meaning.”
- Use CBI-04 to grant new consumption rights.

## 8. CBI-05 — Production Boundaries

### Constitutional purpose

Validate lawful **limits on which artefacts, findings, or dispositions a corpus may produce** — so production remains within published warrants and never becomes soft legislation.

### Constitutional scope

Published production rights and limits (what a corpus may emit: findings, determinations, explanations, warrants). Does **not** invent new production rights or elevate findings into law.

### Constitutional inputs

- Producer corpus identity + claimed produced artefact / finding identities.
- Published production boundary statements.
- Published CBIO-01 / CBIO-05 expectations.

### Constitutional outputs

- Production-boundary finding under CBI-05.
- Lawful-production confirmation or violation note.
- Over-production disclosures (for example producing educational warrants from an assurance horizon).

### Validation rules (category-specific)

- Producers may emit only artefact kinds their published production boundaries permit.
- Findings dispositions (intact / violated / deferred / escalated) never become constitutional amendments.
- Missing published production right → deferred / violated — never invent-in-place warrant.

### Permitted behaviours

- Confirm Programme XI / WS3 may produce boundary integrity findings as published.
- Confirm Programme IX certification may produce recognition dispositions as published.
- Confirm Programme VI coaches may produce educational warrants as published.

### Prohibited behaviours

- Produce tip warrants from Programme XI assurance machinery.
- Produce Constitution / EIP amendments as “findings.”
- Produce stack-freeze declarations as constitutional law.
- Use CBI-05 to invent new production rights.

## 9. CBI-06 — Layer Boundaries

### Constitutional purpose

Validate lawful **separation of concerns across constitutional layers** — Constitution / EIP, Programmes VI–XI, and lawful workstream / milestone subordination — so horizons remain isolated as published.

### Constitutional scope

Published layer / horizon / separation-of-concerns statements and stack order. Does **not** invent new layers or collapse published layers for delivery convenience.

### Constitutional inputs

- Published stack / layer order and separation statements.
- Named horizon identities under assessment.
- Published CBIO-04 expectations.

### Constitutional outputs

- Layer-boundary finding under CBI-06.
- Separation-intact / absorbed / inverted notes.
- Workstream / milestone subordination notes when in scope.

### Validation rules (category-specific)

- Lawful order: Constitution / EIP → VI → VII → VIII → IX → X → XI (validator; never author of upstream meaning).
- A lower layer may *consume* published superior law; it may not *author* or *reinterpret* that law via boundary assessment.
- Sibling horizons within a programme may compose as published; they may not silently absorb each other’s exclusive concerns.

### Permitted behaviours

- Confirm Programme VIII remains execution of published law — not educational meaning authorship.
- Confirm Programme IX remains governance — not Programme VI coaching authorship.
- Confirm Programme XI / WS1–WS3 remain assurance validators — not foundational law.

### Prohibited behaviours

- Collapse VI–X into one mega-programme via boundary theatre.
- Let Programme XI readiness prose rewrite Programme VI meaning.
- Invert layer order so a completion model becomes the meaning model it completes.
- Use CBI-06 to invent a new constitutional layer.

## 10. CBI-07 — Assessment Integrity

### Constitutional purpose

Preserve **reconstructable records of boundary integrity assessments** — so every material finding remains auditable without becoming a second constitution.

### Constitutional scope

Boundary integrity assessment records, their linkage to assessed corpora / boundary statements, and explainability / continuity obligations. Does **not** invent educational state or freeze a preferred assessment tool as law.

### Constitutional inputs

- Assessment record identities and related corpus / CBI category citations.
- Published CBIO-05 / explainability expectations.
- Continuity obligations (EIP-005).

### Constitutional outputs

- Assessment-integrity / lineage records under CBI-07.
- Reconstructability confirmation (true/false).
- Continuity notes relative to prior lawful assessments where applicable.

### Validation rules (category-specific)

- Every material boundary finding must be reconstructable to named corpora, CBI categories, constraints evaluated, separations validated, and disposition.
- Erasing prior lawful assessment history to simplify the next report is a defect.
- Assessment tooling identity is never constitutional law.

### Permitted behaviours

- Record which CBI-01…CBI-06 categories bound a finding.
- Preserve auditor-facing trails that answer CBIEQ-01…CBIEQ-05.
- Note deferred / escalated when records are incomplete.

### Prohibited behaviours

- Treat assessment logs as constitutional amendments.
- Privilege a scanner / CI vendor as the only lawful assessment producer.
- Discard contradictory prior findings without published continuity handling.
- Use CBI-07 to invent missing boundary statements.

## 11. Composition Rules

| Situation | Lawful composition |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Authority absorption suspected | Apply CBI-01 with CBI-06; report — do not reassign authority |
| Duty collision across horizons | Apply CBI-02; disclose collision; propose upstream amendment |
| Contradictory may / must / must-not | Apply CBI-03; prefer violated / deferred over softening |
| Cross-horizon use of artefacts | Apply CBI-04 and/or CBI-05; confirm consumption ≠ authorship and production ≠ legislation |
| Programme XI validating VI–X | CBI-01…CBI-06 may assess subjects; Programme XI never becomes their author |
| Incomplete published inputs | Prefer deferred / escalated under CBIR-10; never invent boundaries |
