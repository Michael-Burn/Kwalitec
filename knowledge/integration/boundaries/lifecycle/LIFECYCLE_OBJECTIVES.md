## 1. Purpose

An expert IFoA tutor does not optimise “scanner finished,” “stages skipped for speed,” or “boundary badge shown.” The tutor optimises **honest organisation of boundary assessment work**: only a repeatable, consistent, implementation-neutral, explainable, and auditable sequence — discovering published boundary statements and validating authority, responsibility, behavioural / consumption / production constraints, and layering under CBI-01…CBI-07 — may support lawful boundary integrity findings.

These objectives bind every constitutional boundary integrity lifecycle and every successor coordination machinery that claims constitutional boundary integrity lifecycle meaning.

---

## 2. Primary Objective

### CBILO-01 — Preserve constitutional authority

**Definition.** Ensure that every material boundary integrity lifecycle organises assessments through recognised CBIL-01…CBIL-08 stages — initiating against identifiable published corpora, discovering named boundary statements, validating authority boundaries, and constructing / publishing findings under published CBI-01…CBI-07 categories — so that findings occur only after lawful initiation and discovery, and that findings are published without becoming law, authority creation, responsibility rewrites, or replacements for constitutional authority.

**Includes:**

- Binding every material lifecycle to identifiable published Constitution / EIP / Programme VI–XI specifications and subordinate workstream / milestone corpora.
- Discovering only published boundary statements as inputs.
- Applying only published CBI-01…CBI-07 categories and CBIR validation rules across stages.
- Preferring lawful violated / deferred / escalated / refuse-progress outcomes over rewriting law, inventing boundaries, inventing authority, softening constraints, or mutating corpora so the lifecycle “completes.”
- Keeping lifecycle progress distinct from educational quality, mastery, readiness theatre, product success, boundary-scanner greenery, or constitutional amendment.
- Preserving the Model’s posture: stages coordinate assessment; they do not become law or constitutional authority.

**Excludes:**

- Treating green CI, coverage percentages, linter scores, Twin estimates, Adaptive scores, UI ticks, or string scanners as lifecycle fulfilment.
- Soft-amending specifications, inventing unpublished customs, inventing or rewriting boundaries, inventing authority, softening constraints, or privileging “how main behaves” to force progress.
- Optimising for throughput, latency, or demo continuity as a substitute for lawful coordination.
- Declaring “close enough” publication without documenting a corpus amendment.
- Freezing an implementation as permanently lawful because the lifecycle published findings.
- Creating constitutional authority under a lifecycle badge.

**Tutor rationale.** Professional exam preparation fails when software pretends unfinished or dishonest boundary assessment is fine because integration needs a finished green boundary report. Coordination honesty is care; lifecycle theatre is harm.

## 3. Supporting Objectives

### CBILO-02 — Preserve responsibility separation

**Definition.** Ensure that every material lifecycle validates — and leaves reconstructable — published responsibility boundaries so duty allocations remain separated across horizons and no foreign corpus silently absorbs exclusive responsibilities through stage progress.

**Tutor rationale.** A coach who lets yesterday’s worksheet mark today’s exam as if it owned the syllabus has abandoned institutional order for convenience.

**Manifestations:**

- CBIL-04 requires reconstructable published responsibility boundaries; silent duty transfer or dual exclusive ownership without published warrant is a defect.
- Findings cite owning corpora and responsibility identities, not only ticket IDs or commit SHAs.
- Continuity (EIP-005) forbids erasing prior lawful assessment history to simplify the next report.
- Responsibility-separation preservation never authorises inventing missing duty statements or redefining published responsibilities.

---

### CBILO-03 — Preserve boundary consistency

**Definition.** Ensure that lifecycle organisation preserves consistency among published authority, responsibility, behavioural, consumption, production, and layer boundaries — so contradictions, silent softens, and invented rights are reported honestly rather than papered over for progress.

**Tutor rationale.** A tutor who softens “must not” into “may” so a progress chart stays green has abandoned care for theatre.

**Manifestations:**

- CBIL-03…CBIL-06 apply published CBI / CBIR rules for authority, responsibility, behavioural, consumption, production, and layer boundaries.
- Constraint contradictions without published precedence are hard defects, not soft variables.
- Forbidden actions in `LIFECYCLE_BOUNDARIES.md` are hard stops.
- Boundary-consistency preservation never authorises inventing, softening, or deleting published boundary statements.

---

### CBILO-04 — Preserve assessment repeatability

**Definition.** Ensure that the same published corpus identities, the same discovered boundary statements, the same published CBI / CBIR criteria, and the same CBIL stage sequence yield the same boundary disposition (intact / violated / deferred / escalated) and the same lawful stage outcomes — absent a published non-deterministic exception (none are granted by this Model for constitutional meaning or integrity status).

**Tutor rationale.** A coach who treats the same syllabus boundary as lawful or not depending on which reviewer woke first — or skips stages randomly — is not coaching; they are gambling.

**Manifestations:**

- Only CBIL-01…CBIL-08 may organise constitutional boundary integrity lifecycles.
- Stage purpose, inputs, outputs, and permitted / prohibited activities remain as published in `LIFECYCLE_STAGES.md`.
- Stage transitions and outcomes are corpus-bound and reproducible.
- Parallel CI runners, sharding, and cache races must not change constitutional disposition or invent stage skips.
- Explicit multi-type CBI sets use published conjunction / published stop rules, not random reviewer races.
- “Eventually consistent lifecycle meaning” is a constitutional defect.
- Invented or unstable boundary inputs, and invented authority / responsibility allocations, are hard stops — not soft variables.

---

### CBILO-05 — Preserve governance integrity

**Definition.** Ensure that lifecycle findings support honest Programme XI integration and readiness judgements — so governance and readiness claims consume intact boundary structure produced through lawful stages rather than inventing readiness despite contradicted, softened, untraced, or incompletely assessed boundaries — and that every material lifecycle remains explainable and auditable under `LIFECYCLE_EXPLAINABILITY.md`.

**Tutor rationale.** A coach who declares a study plan “governed” while worksheets quietly claim each other’s exclusive marking rights is performing governance theatre, not care. A tutor who cannot later say *why* a boundary was accepted cannot be trusted.

**Manifestations:**

- Intact findings published after CBIL-01…CBIL-08 may inform downstream readiness / governance honesty; they never themselves certify educational readiness, mastery, or product success.
- Violated / deferred / escalated findings, and refuse-* dispositions, block honest governance claims that depend on the broken or incomplete boundary structure.
- Explainability obligations apply to all material lifecycles (boundaries assessed, authority / responsibility validated, constraints evaluated, findings produced, limits remaining intact).
- Readiness / governance proxies (CI greens, coverage, scanner scores) never redefine CBILO-01…CBILO-05.
- Governance integrity here means *constitutional corpus boundary honesty for lawful integration* — not student exam readiness and not Runtime A deployment theatre.
- Preserving governance integrity never authorises rewriting boundaries or skipping stages so a release can ship.

## 4. Objective Interactions

| If … | Then … |
|------|--------|
| CBILO-01 conflicts with “helping” contradicted boundaries look lifecycle-complete | Lawful coordination honesty wins — violated / deferred / escalated / refuse-progress |
| CBILO-02 conflicts with soft-absorbing a foreign horizon’s exclusive duties | Responsibility separation wins |
| CBILO-03 conflicts with softening constraints to force green | Boundary consistency wins |
| CBILO-04 conflicts with race-dependent reviewer outcomes or private stage shortcuts | Assessment repeatability wins |
| CBILO-05 conflicts with shipping despite broken or incompletely assessed boundary structure, or with opaque “trust us” badges | Governance integrity, explainability, and auditability win |

No supporting objective authorises violating CBILO-01.

These objectives specialise — and never weaken — Constitution / EIP integrity obligations, Programme VI–X authority preservation, and WS3 / MS001 boundary integrity objectives for the lifecycle horizon.

## 5. Non-Objectives

The constitutional boundary integrity lifecycle does **not** optimise for:

- educational quality scoring, mastery grading, or exam-readiness judgement;
- CI throughput, flaky-test suppression, or coverage theatre as educational success;
- inventing constitutional certainty when specifications or published boundaries are incomplete;
- collapsing CBIL stages into one mega-step that owns meaning;
- making a particular language, framework, store, or CI vendor irreplaceable;
- using Twin / Adaptive / UI / analytics / string-scanner signals as substitutes for published constitutional boundaries;
- freezing implementations, releases, or stacks as permanently lawful;
- treating documentation as optional commentary on whatever code already shipped;
- replacing MS001 boundary integrity meaning, Programme XI WS1 / WS2 dependency / catalogue integrity, Programme IX CT lineage preservation, or Programme IX–X evaluative / execution horizons;
- rewriting, inventing, softening, or deleting constitutional boundaries so reports look tidy;
- creating constitutional authority under a lifecycle badge;
- redefining constitutional responsibilities or altering constitutional constraints under coordination pretence.

## 6. Closing Statement

> **A lifecycle that becomes “complete” by creating constitutional authority, redefining responsibilities, reinterpreting meaning, or amending specifications is a failed educational system.
