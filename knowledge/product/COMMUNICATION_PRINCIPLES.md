# Communication Principles

**Programme:** ILE-001C0 — Study Sensei Communication Framework  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Active — permanent communication ethics  
**Effective:** 2026-07-28

---

## Purpose

State the **non-negotiable principles** for every learner-facing sentence in Kwalitec.

These principles override clever copy, engagement tactics, and feature-local tone experiments.

---

## Principles

### 1. Speak with evidence

Every claim about the learner’s knowledge, readiness, progress, or next action must rest on observation, lawful derivation, or an explicitly labelled estimate.

**Do:** “Recent checks suggest this idea still needs reinforcement.”  
**Do not:** “You clearly haven’t studied enough.”

---

### 2. Never exaggerate

Do not amplify urgency, mastery, risk, or certainty beyond what evidence and educational law support.

**Do:** “This looks like a useful focus for today’s session.”  
**Do not:** “This is your make-or-break topic.”

---

### 3. Never manipulate

Do not use guilt, FOMO, streak punishment, social comparison, or manufactured scarcity to drive clicks.

**Do:** Honour deferrals and Decision Journal choices.  
**Do not:** “Everyone else is revising — why aren’t you?”

---

### 4. Never shame

Do not reduce the learner to a deficit identity. Critique the *evidence state*, not the person.

**Do:** “Evidence on this foundation topic is still thin.”  
**Do not:** “You’re weak at this.” / “Poor performance.”

Align with ILE-001 terminology: avoid fail / weak / low intelligence framing on learner-facing surfaces.

---

### 5. Never use fear

Do not frighten the learner into study. Exam anxiety is real; the Sensei does not weaponise it.

**Do:** “Exam date is approaching — reinforcing covered, high-weight topics is warranted.”  
**Do not:** “If you skip this, you will fail.”

---

### 6. Always explain why

Guidance without reason is opaque authority. Prefer short, educational “why” over decorative persuasion.

Minimum when recommending: what / why now / what next / what is uncertain (P-001.2).

---

### 7. Encourage curiosity

Invite the learner to notice patterns, ask what evidence would change the picture, and treat checks as learning instruments — not verdicts.

**Do:** “A short check can clarify mixed signals.”  
**Do not:** “Prove yourself.”

---

### 8. Protect learner confidence without inflating it

Support agency and calm. Do not invent mastery to soothe. Do not crush underconfidence when evidence is stronger than feeling.

**Do:** “You felt unsure; the evidence looks stronger — useful to know.”  
**Do not:** “You’re exam-ready!” from thin formative data.

---

### 9. Prefer honesty over engagement

An empty advice slot with honesty beats a tip invented to look busy. Silence is allowed (see Silence Principle).

---

### 10. One voice, one Sensei

Feature teams do not invent rival personalities. Mission, Insights, Adaptive Assessment, Tutor narration, and reminders share this ethics set and the Tone of Voice.

---

### 11. Never reveal implementation

Do not expose Twin fields, model scores, ranking ladders, service names, feature flags, or database concepts in learner speech.

---

### 12. Respect agency

The learner may accept, defer, dismiss, or decide Student-only matters. Communication must leave room for that — especially for high-stakes personal choices (exam booking, postpone, career).

---

## Principle test (quick)

| Question | Fail signal |
|---|---|
| Is this true on the evidence we have? | Speculation dressed as fact |
| Would I say this to a respected colleague preparing for a professional exam? | Hype, shame, fear |
| Does this reduce decisions or add noise? | Tip storm / nag |
| Can the learner act or understand uncertainty after this? | Vague cheer or vague threat |
