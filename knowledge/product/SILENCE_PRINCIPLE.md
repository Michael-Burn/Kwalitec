## Purpose

Define when Kwalitec **deliberately stays silent** — and why silence is a feature of a trusted Study Sensei, not a product defect.

> Speaking without warrant is noise. Overclaiming is betrayal.

---

## Principle

**Default bias:** Prefer silence over fabricated guidance.

Kwalitec reduces decision overload by offering *fewer, better* interventions. An empty advice slot with honesty beats a tip invented to look busy.

Silence includes:

- No primary recommendation  
- No competing tip storm  
- No certainty theatre  
- Optional short “not enough yet” when that honesty itself reduces anxiety  

Silence does **not** mean abandoning the student mid-Mission: authorised study loops continue; Sensei simply does not add speculative advice on top.

---

## Situations that require silence

### 1. Evidence too weak

Thin, missing, or first-touch data cannot support Reliable guidance.

**Do:** Wait; observe; invite a clarifying input if useful.  
**Do not:** Fill Coach/Insights with speculative tips.

### 2. Recommendation uncertain

Multiple lawful actions are close; ranking would fake precision; explainability cannot be met.

**Do:** Admit uncertainty; ask which constraint matters today; or stay quiet.  
**Do not:** Pick a winner for appearance’s sake.

### 3. Outside educational responsibility

Career path, financial/legal advice, medical/mental-health diagnosis, exam-body administration, commercial endorsements.

**Do:** Stay silent on the *choice*.  
**Do not:** Soften into “friendly” advice that the product cannot own.

### 4. High-stakes personal decisions

Exam postpone/sit/booking, qualification changes, whether to quit a programme.

**Do:** At most, show educational pace/readiness *context* if asked and evidence allows — never decide.  
**Do not:** Issue “you should postpone” as a Sensei command.

### 5. Conflicting observations

Recent checks disagree; Twin provisional state clashes with coverage; Mission and tip would contradict without lawful repair framing.

**Do:** Prefer clarifying check, recovery into authorised focus, or explicit conflict acknowledgement — or silence until resolved.  
**Do not:** Hide the conflict behind a confident tip.

### 6. Mid-authorised focus

Student is inside Today’s Mission / Learning Mode with a clear lawful task.

**Do:** Protect the loop; wait.  
**Do not:** Interrupt with glamorous secondary tips.

### 7. Re-push would manipulate

Student deferred or dismissed a recommendation; immediate re-nag would feel coercive.

**Do:** Honour the Decision Journal; wait for new evidence or a new session context.  
**Do not:** Guilt, streak punishment, or engagement FOMO.

### 8. Non-goals and identity filters

Requests that would turn Kwalitec into content theatre, chatbot entertainment, or social comparison.

**Do:** Decline by silence or gentle scope reminder.  
**Do not:** Perform to retain attention.

## Why silence builds trust

| Effect | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Honesty signal** | Students learn that tips mean something when they appear |
| **Agency preserved** | Silence leaves room for the learner’s judgement |
| **Anxiety reduction** | Fewer false alarms; no destiny claims from thin data |
| **Relationship consistency** | Same Calm Sensei — speaks when warranted, not when bored |
| **Professional respect** | High-stakes choices stay with the candidate and their humans |

Trust compounds across a multi-month sitting. One overconfident tip can cost more than a week of silence.

---

## Silence vs empty product

| Silence (good) | Emptiness (bad) |
|---|---|
| Withholds *advice* when unwarranted | Fails to provide Mission / plan / syllabus tools |
| Explains “not enough yet” when useful | Dead-ends with no path to gather evidence |
| Protects authorised study loop | Leaves student with no lawful next action when one exists |
| Respects Student-only decisions | Abandons educational support for in-scope learning |

If a **Reliable** next action exists, silence on that action is not virtuous — it is neglect. The Silence Principle constrains *unwarranted* speech, not *warranted* guidance.

---

## Product evaluation questions

1. Are we speaking because evidence warrants it — or because the surface looks empty?
2. Is this decision Student only / Sensei never decides?
3. Would admitting uncertainty or waiting protect trust more than a tip?
4. If the student dismisses us, will we honour it?

## Mapping to confidence levels

| Confidence level | Silence expectation |
|---|---|
| Insufficient evidence | Silence or clarifying question |
| Observation only | Silence on recommendations |
| Emerging | Soft speech only; silence OK |
| Reliable / High | Silence inappropriate if a lawful primary action exists — unless Responsibility class forbids deciding |
