# AP-002 — UX Principles

**Programme:** AP-002 — Educational Assessment Engine  
**Status:** Design  

---

## 1. Emotional contract

Students should feel:

| Feeling | Design implication |
|---|---|
| **Supported** | Clear purpose; help available; next step always visible |
| **Curious** | Framed as discovery about their understanding |
| **Safe** | Exit allowed; mistakes are data; no public ranking |

Students must **never** feel punished for incorrect answers.

---

## 2. Avoid exam anxiety

| Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|
| Countdown punishment by default | Gentle time guidance only when pedagogically needed |
| Red “FAILED” stamps | “Useful signal for today’s plan” |
| Percentage as identity | Evidence + next action |
| Surprise pop quizzes without mission context | Framed mission steps |
| Locked progress until pass | Recovery path after evidence |
| Comparison to peers | Personal Twin uncertainty reduction |
| “Exam mode” chrome for formative checks | Calm study chrome |

High-stakes exam simulation, if ever productised, is a separate experience with explicit consent — not the default Assessment Engine UX.

---

## 3. Encourage learning

Every assessment screen should answer:

1. Why am I doing this?
2. What happens with my answers?
3. What do I do next?

Default closure pattern:

```
You completed a check on {objective}.
Here’s what we learned about your understanding.
Next: {mission / Tutor-suggested action from Twin decisions}.
```

---

## 4. Interaction principles

1. **One job per screen** — stem, response, optional confidence; no dashboard clutter.
2. **Hints without shame** — using a hint is allowed; it becomes evidence, not a moral failure.
3. **Pause / resume** — life interrupts study; abandonment is not delinquency.
4. **Plain language** — no Twin / pipeline / inference jargon.
5. **Confidence optional** — invite calibration; never force anxiety.
6. **Immediate educational feedback** — short, deterministic, non-theatrical.
7. **Tutor on request** — “Explain this result” without auto-lecturing.
8. **Accessibility** — readable stems, keyboard-friendly controls, no colour-only correctness.

---

## 5. Visual & tone guidance

Within existing Kwalitec student design system:

- Keep formative assessment visually continuous with study sessions (LXP), not a separate “exam skin”
- Prefer calm progress (“3 of 5 checks”) over score bars that imply judgement
- Correct/incorrect states should be clear but not aggressive
- Celebrate completion of the *check*, not superiority

Do not introduce purple-glow gamification, badge spam, or streak punishment for missed assessments.

---

## 6. Copy bank (illustrative)

**Entry**

- “Quick check — helps keep today’s plan accurate.”
- “No grades. Just clearer next steps.”

**During**

- “Take your time.”
- “Hint available if you want one.”

**After incorrect**

- “That mix-up is useful — we’ll use it to guide practice.”
- “Not solid yet — that’s exactly what this check is for.”

**After correct**

- “Nice — we’ll treat this as evidence, not a final verdict.”
- “If it still feels shaky, we can reinforce it next.”

**Exit**

- “Paused. Your progress is saved.”

---

## 7. Trust & honesty

- Never claim mastery the Twin does not support
- Never hide that answers inform the study plan
- Never imply human invigilation or credential value for formative checks
- Disclose when a check is diagnostic vs verification in plain words

---

## 8. Success criterion for UX

A student can complete an assessment and say:

> “That helped me know what to study next — and I didn’t feel tested.”

If they feel tested, the UX has failed the educational philosophy even if observations were perfect.
