# Blind Review SV-012

**Reviewer:** Nathan Cole  
**Age:** 25  
**Country:** United Kingdom  
**Exam:** IFoA CM1 (first sitting)  
**Weeks until exam:** 11  
**Occupation:** Graduate Actuarial Analyst  
**Date:** 24 July 2026  
**Context:** Morning after a major practice assessment went badly in one topic. Four weeks of near-daily use before that. CMP and question bank open. I am not looking for comfort. I am looking for evidence that yesterday changed today.

**Package confirmed:** Student-facing build in the review package — **Kwalitec v2.0.0 · Internal Alpha · Founding Cohort · Build RC2**. CM1 is supported. I reviewed only the educational experience a student can see. I ignored engineering documentation.

**Question I held the whole time:** *Did Kwalitec actually learn anything about me?*

---

## How I got here

I have been using Kwalitec for four weeks. Most weeknights: open the app, take the day’s session, work from the CMP and practice questions, tick the activity list, record attempted / correct at the end. Learning Workspace path most days — Dashboard → Session → study → Finish → Practice Outcome Capture. I also check Home for Readiness / Journey / Coach, and Analytics when I want a longer look.

Yesterday I sat a major practice assessment. Results were worse than I expected. One topic was a mess. Everywhere else was fine — which somehow made the weak spot feel worse. I logged what I could through Practice Outcome Capture and went to bed unsettled.

Today I opened Kwalitec again expecting a reaction.

---

## What today felt like

### Mission / Session

Today’s Study Session still gave me a named topic, a why, success criteria, and the familiar activity checklist: read, examples, practice, review mistakes. That part still works.

What I needed was a visible link to yesterday. Something like: *you struggled here — go back*.

What I got was closer to the usual “next step / highest-value based on recent learning evidence” framing. Even when the topic looked related to where I failed, the briefing did not say so clearly. I had to infer whether today’s focus was a response to the bad assessment or just the next item on the plan.

That is not the same as feeling adapted to.

### Home — Coach, Journey, Readiness

**Coach** still spoke in the same register I have heard for weeks: focus on this topic next; highest-value next step; studying it should strengthen exam readiness. It did not name my weak topic. It did not say “yesterday exposed a gap.” It repeated the mission rationale in coaching clothes. After a bad assessment, that felt like silence dressed as advice.

**Journey** showed path / overall progress language. It did not visibly flinch. No clear “this area is now exposed.” After yesterday, I wanted Journey to look different. It mostly looked like a map that had kept moving — or not moving in any way I could connect to the failure.

**Readiness** eventually shows a signal once there is history. After four weeks I am past the empty “not enough history” state. A single bad day did not produce a clear, readable response I could trust — nothing that said, in plain terms, *readiness dipped because of that topic*. It stayed a composite I cannot explain to myself.

### Dashboard / Analytics

This is where I came closest to an answer.

After practice outcomes accumulate, Dashboard and Analytics can surface **Topics needing more practice**, accuracy from what I logged, Estimated Knowledge once there is enough recorded practice, and weekly areas for improvement. When I open Analytics after a poor day, a weak topic can appear in the “needing more practice” list with an estimated knowledge figure. Accuracy trend can move if I logged the mess honestly.

That is the strongest evidence I have that yesterday entered the system at all.

It still feels like a ledger reacting, not a tutor reacting. The numbers notice. The day’s mission and the Coach do not explain the notice back to me.

---

## Answers

### 1. Did today’s mission reflect yesterday’s poor performance?

**Unclear. Possibly in topic choice. Not clearly in explanation.**

I could not tell, from the briefing alone, that today’s session existed *because* of yesterday’s failure. If the topic overlapped my weak area, I noticed — but I supplied that link myself. The product did not.

### 2. Did I understand why today’s recommendation changed?

**No — not in a way that settled me.**

“Highest-value next step based on your recent learning evidence” is not enough after a shock assessment. I need: *changed because you scored poorly on X while the rest held*. Without that, a changed topic still feels arbitrary.

### 3. Did Coach acknowledge my weak topic?

**No.**

Coach did not name the weak topic. It did not acknowledge the assessment. It restated the mission. After four weeks, I already knew Coach and Mission say the same thing. Yesterday I needed Coach to say something Mission would not.

### 4. Did Journey visibly change?

**Not in a way I could connect to yesterday.**

Journey still reads as progress along a path. It did not show the scar from the bad topic. For my purpose today — *did yesterday change the story?* — Journey was quiet.

### 5. Did Readiness respond appropriately?

**Not appropriately enough for my confidence.**

I accept that readiness should not swing wildly on one sitting. I still expected a readable reaction: a dip, a flag, a plain-language note tied to the weak area. What I get is a composite that is hard to interpret the morning after a failure. It did not help me decide what to do with the panic.

### 6. Did I feel the application had learned something about me?

**Only a little — and mostly in Analytics, not in today’s guidance.**

The practice log and “topics needing more practice” suggest my numbers were kept. That is learning of a sort.

I did **not** feel the application had learned *me* — the uneven profile of “strong everywhere except this one hole.” That is what yesterday taught *me*. Kwalitec did not show it back clearly on the surfaces that decide my day.

### 7. Did I trust that tomorrow’s recommendations would continue adapting?

**Weakly. Not enough to calm me.**

I trust that if I keep logging practice, charts and weak-topic lists will update. I do **not** yet trust that tomorrow’s mission and Coach will intelligently chase the hole. Direction feels plan-led with a thin evidence layer, not assessment-reactive coaching.

### 8. Which feature best demonstrated personalisation?

**Analytics / Dashboard “Topics needing more practice,” fed by Practice Outcome Capture.**

That loop — record attempted / correct, later see weak topics and accuracy — is the clearest personal signal I found after a bad day. Estimated Knowledge’s honesty (completion ≠ understanding) also still matters.

### 9. Which feature still felt generic?

**Coach — and Mission’s “why” copy.**

Same highest-value / recent learning evidence language whether I am coasting or crashing. Journey’s progress story also felt generic relative to yesterday’s specific failure.

### 10. If Kwalitec never adapted after assessments, would I continue using it?

**As a daily director and log — maybe. As the system that owns my preparation — no.**

Why:

I already have a CMP and a question bank. What I need from Kwalitec is intelligent reaction when my learning changes. If assessments never move tomorrow’s study in a way I can see and trust, the product becomes a polite schedule with a checklist. After yesterday, that is not enough for 11 weeks out. I would keep a notebook of weak topics myself and stop waiting for the app to notice.

If it *does* adapt — clearly, by name, in mission and coach — I would stay. That is the difference between a tool that manages my evenings and a tool that manages my risk.

## Bottom line

Yesterday told me something important about my CM1 preparation: one topic is exposed; the rest is not.

Today I opened Kwalitec asking whether it had learned the same thing.

It stored my practice numbers. It can list topics that need more practice. It still speaks about “highest-value next steps” without naming the failure. Coach did not acknowledge the weak topic. Journey did not show the scar. Readiness did not talk to me in plain language about what changed overnight.

So: the application noticed something in the data. It did not convince me it understood *me*.

Until the morning after a bad assessment feels different from any other morning — in the mission, in the Coach, in language I can trust — I will keep opening Kwalitec for direction, and I will keep trusting my own notes for the truth.
