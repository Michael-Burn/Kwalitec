# Blind Review SV-011

**Reviewer:** Oliver Hughes  
**Age:** 24  
**Country:** United Kingdom  
**Exam:** IFoA CM1 (first sitting)  
**Weeks until exam:** 14  
**Occupation:** Graduate Actuarial Consultant  
**Date:** 24 July 2026  
**Context:** Weekday evening after three weeks of near-daily use. CMP and question bank open beside me. I am not looking for encouragement. I want to know whether I am improving.

**Package confirmed:** Student-facing build in the review package — **Kwalitec v2.0.0 · Internal Alpha · Founding Cohort · Build RC2**. CM1 is supported. I reviewed only the educational experience a student can see. I ignored engineering documentation.

**Question I held the whole time:** *Does this application genuinely help me understand whether I am improving?*

---

## How I have been using it

I study most weekdays. I rarely skip. For three weeks I have opened Kwalitec, taken the day’s session, worked from the CMP and practice questions, ticked the activity list, and recorded attempted / correct at the end. I write my own notes separately, as I always do. I review mistakes carefully in those notes.

I use the Learning Workspace path most evenings: Dashboard → Session → study → Finish → Practice Outcome Capture. I also check Home for Readiness / Journey / Coach, and Analytics when I want a longer look.

I am not judging polish. I am judging whether the feedback tells me the truth about my learning.

---

## What a normal session feels like after three weeks

### Mission / Session

Today’s Study Session still gives me a named topic, a why, success criteria, and the same activity checklist: read, examples, practice, review mistakes. That matches how I already study. After three weeks it still answers “what should I do tonight?” cleanly.

When I finish, Practice Outcome Capture asks for questions attempted / correct, optional time, optional notes. It says clearly that this is what I reported — not Estimated Knowledge. I respect that honesty. I enter the numbers carefully. I put a short note when something went wrong.

What I do **not** get after the session is a diagnosis of *what* I improved in that hour. No “you are stronger on X; still weak on Y.” The session feedback confirms the session was recorded. It does not interpret my understanding.

### Dashboard

Progress through the study plan moves when I complete topics. Syllabus coverage is visible. Estimated Knowledge only appears once enough practice is recorded, and the product is blunt that finishing a topic is not the same as understanding it. That distinction matters to me. It is one of the few places that speaks to my actual concern.

Recommendations still push me toward the next high-priority topic. Useful for direction. Thin as feedback on skill.

### Home — Readiness, Journey, Coach

**Readiness** eventually stops saying there is no history, and Analytics explains that estimated readiness leans on syllabus coverage, Estimated Knowledge from recorded practice, and recent review habits. After three weeks I can see a signal starting. I still struggle to translate it into “am I on track for my CM1 sitting in 14 weeks?” It feels like a composite, not a judgement I could explain to myself in plain language.

**Journey** still reads as a path story and overall progress. It tells me I am moving through a plan. It does not help me interpret *how well* I am learning. After three weeks of careful work, Journey still feels more like a map of movement than a report on improvement.

**Coach** keeps saying, in substance: focus on this topic next; highest-value next step based on recent learning evidence; studying it should strengthen exam readiness. It is consistent with the mission. It does not explain what I personally need to fix — which method, which mistake pattern, which gap versus last week. For someone who already completes what he plans, “do the next topic” is not the feedback I am missing.

### Analytics / History

This is where the product comes closest to answering me.

When sessions and practice outcomes accumulate, I can see study time, session completion, accuracy trend from what I logged, weekly hours, and lists that can surface topics needing more practice versus stronger estimated knowledge. The weekly report names areas for improvement in effort language (completion rate, days studied, duration tracking).

That is useful. It is also mostly **effort and self-reported accuracy**, not deep educational insight. If I am diligent and my logged accuracy drifts up, the charts look better. If I am diligent but still confused on a method I keep mis-applying, the app may not notice unless my counts or topic flags show it. My careful mistake review still lives in my notebook, not in Kwalitec.

## Answers

### 1. Did I understand what I had improved?

**Only partly.**

I understand that I have covered more of the plan, completed more sessions, and that my logged practice accuracy can trend. When Estimated Knowledge appears, I understand the product is trying to separate “I studied it” from “I can do it.”

I do **not** clearly understand what I have *improved at* in CM1 terms — which ideas are sticking, which techniques are safer under time pressure. After a good evening, I know I worked. I do not leave with a crisp “you got better at X.”

### 2. Did I understand where I remained weak?

**Somewhat, at topic level. Not deeply.**

Analytics can point at topics needing more practice. Recommendations can flag attention. That is a start.

It does not replace how I review mistakes. It does not name the specific weakness inside a topic. After three weeks I still trust my own notes more than Kwalitec for “where am I actually weak?”

### 3. Did Journey help me interpret my progress?

**Not in the way I need.**

Journey helps me see that I am on a path and that overall progress exists as a story toward exam readiness. It does not help me interpret whether that progress is real learning or just completed sessions. For my question — am I getting better? — Journey is quiet.

### 4. Did Readiness help me judge exam preparation?

**A little, once history exists. Not enough to settle me.**

I like that Readiness does not invent a trend from nothing. I like that Analytics is explicit about what goes into estimated readiness.

I still cannot comfortably answer “how ready am I for CM1 in 14 weeks?” from Readiness alone. It feels like an early signal, not a preparation judgement I would trust in a conversation with myself.

### 5. Did Coach explain what to improve next?

**It explained what to study next. Not what to improve.**

Those are different for me. Coach and Mission agree on the next topic and call it highest-value. That reduces decision load. It does not tell me which skill or mistake pattern to repair. As someone who already shows up and finishes the plan, I already know how to “do the next thing.” I need help seeing the next *weakness*.

### 6. After finishing today’s session, did I know whether I had a good study day?

**I knew whether I had a complete study day. Not always whether I had a good learning day.**

If I finished the session, ticked the activities, and recorded practice, the product treats the day as done. That matches diligence. It does not tell me if the hour actually moved my understanding.

The honest line — completing a topic is not understanding — is the closest thing to a check on false confidence. I appreciate it. I still walk away relying on my own sense of the practice, and on my notes, to judge quality.

### 7. If someone asked me, “How is your CM1 preparation going?”, would Kwalitec help me answer?

**Help a little. Not enough to answer well.**

I could say: I have a plan, I am studying most weekdays, coverage has moved, I have logged practice, readiness/analytics are starting to show a trend.

I could **not** confidently say, from Kwalitec alone: I am stronger on these CM1 areas, still exposed on those, and on track for the sitting. That sentence still comes from my notes, my practice papers, and my own judgement.

### 8. Which feature provided the clearest educational feedback?

**Analytics (with Practice Outcome Capture feeding it), plus the Dashboard’s Estimated Knowledge honesty.**

The combination of recording attempted/correct and later seeing accuracy / topics needing practice / estimated knowledge is the clearest educational feedback loop I found. It is still thin, but it is the most concrete.

### 9. Which feature felt least informative?

**Journey — and Coach, for my purpose.**

Journey: progress language without interpretation of learning quality.  
Coach: next-step wording that mostly restates the mission.  
For someone who wants feedback on improvement, both feel polite and under-informative.

### 10. Would I trust Kwalitec to track my improvement over the next three months? Why?

**Partially — for diligence and topic direction. Not fully for improvement.**

Why:

I trust it to keep me on a CM1 plan, to give me tonight’s focus, and to store a session/practice history I can look back on. I trust its honesty when it refuses to invent readiness or equate completion with understanding. That matters to me.

I do **not** yet trust it as the system that will tell me whether I am getting better at CM1. The feedback is mostly coverage, effort, and self-reported counts. My real improvement signal still comes from careful mistake review in my notes and from how I perform on practice under time. Over the next three months I would keep using Kwalitec as the daily director and log. I would not hand it the job of answering my biggest question.

## Bottom line

I work hard. That is not my problem.

My problem is knowing whether the hard work is turning into CM1 ability. After three weeks of consistent use, Kwalitec is excellent at telling me what to study next and whether I completed the day. It is only beginning to show trends from what I log. It is still weak at interpreting improvement and weakness in a way I could trust.

I would keep opening it on weeknights. I would not yet trust it to answer the only question I really brought with me: *am I getting better?*
