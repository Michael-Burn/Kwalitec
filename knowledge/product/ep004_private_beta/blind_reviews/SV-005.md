## How I used it tonight

Got credentials from the coordinator. Signed in after work.

Confirmed CM1 is a supported paper in the study-plan flow. The shared beta account I was given already had an active plan pointing at IFoA CS1 with a concrete daily topic — fine for judging *how* recommendations are explained. For my own prep I would run this on CM1; tonight I cared about whether the “why” holds up.

I kept one question open the whole time: if this app tells me to study something, do I understand why?

### Learning Workspace Dashboard

Default home after sign-in. Clear card for today’s study session with a named topic (in this account: *Review CS1-A: Descriptive statistics foundations*). Progress at 0% syllabus coverage with an explicit note that this is Learning Progress, not Estimated Knowledge. Estimated Knowledge said it needs recorded practice — that honesty I respect.

There was also a recommendation needing attention (*Study 1.1…*, High / New Topic) and a collapsed “Why this session” / “Details” link. Exam pace said “Comfortable” with a long runway — not my 10-week CM1 reality, so I treated that as sample plan data, not personal advice.

### Student Home (the other “home”)

Opened Home via the student top nav. Subtitle promises: “What you should do next, and why.”

Today’s Mission: **30 minutes**, no topic name on the card, copy saying this is the “highest-value next step… based on your recent learning evidence.”

Readiness: “Not enough history for a trend yet.”  
Learning journey: empty until sessions accumulate.  
Coach insight: same “highest-value / learning evidence / strengthen exam readiness” language as the mission line.

That is the first trust break. The page claims evidence while Readiness admits there is none yet. I do not ignore contradictions like that.

### Today’s Study Session (Session / missions path)

Fuller briefing. Named topic. Estimated **90 min** (not 30). Syllabus coverage 0%.

Box titled **“Why you are studying this”** — but the text was an instruction: resume the session and finish when planned study is done. That answers *what to click next*, not *why this topic over another*. Success criteria and the activity checklist (read, examples, practice, review mistakes) are sensible study hygiene; they are not a selection rationale.

### Active Study Session

This was the clearest explanation I found all evening. The learning-objective copy said today’s mission follows the Current Learning Topic in the study plan — essentially the next syllabus topic not yet completed studying, one clear topic at a time. That is a rule I can inspect. I understand it. I can agree or disagree with “syllabus order first,” but it is not opaque.

Session plan suggested reading notes, attempting practice questions, marking weak areas. Still my CMP and question bank doing the real work — which is fine if the app is honest that it is a director, not a tutor.

### Session Overview (from Home → Start Session)

Thin. “your topic.” Topic list: **Core methods**. Activities: **No activities listed.** About 30 minutes. No “why” at all. If this were the only briefing I saw, I would not start studying from it.

### Journey / Analytics / Readiness

Journey: 0% complete. Expected on day one; not yet useful as evidence.

Analytics was more careful. Estimated Readiness unavailable, with an explicit statement of what it will use later (syllabus coverage, Estimated Knowledge from recorded practice, recent review habits). Empty charts explained what data is missing. The line that completing a topic alone is not understanding matches how I already think about CM1.

So: when the product *refuses* to invent a readiness score, it feels more evidence-based than when the Coach *claims* learning evidence with an empty history.

## Answers

### 1. Did I understand why today’s mission was selected?

Only once I reached the active Study Session objective text.

There, the selection rule is readable: next unfinished Current Learning Topic on the plan / official syllabus path.

Elsewhere, no. Home never named the topic. The Session page’s “Why you are studying this” did not explain selection. Coach repeated “highest-value next step” without showing alternatives or inputs. Session Overview gave almost no justification.

So: partially, and only on one path — not within the first screen that promised “and why.”

### 2. Did I trust the recommendation?

Not yet — not enough to let it override my own plan.

I trust a **named syllabus next-topic** rule more than I trust marketing language about “highest-value” and “learning evidence.” The concrete Learning Workspace topic felt plausible as a syllabus starting point. The evidence claims on Home did not.

### 3. If I didn’t trust it, why not?

- **Claim vs evidence mismatch.** Coach and Today’s Mission say “based on your recent learning evidence” while Readiness says there is not enough history. That reads like confident copy, not analysis.
- **“Why” labels that don’t answer why.** Resume instructions are not reasons.
- **Same day, two durations.** 30 minutes on Home vs 90 minutes on Session for what is presented as today’s focus. I will not take time advice seriously until those agree.
- **Thin Overview path.** “Core methods” / “your topic” / no activities does not earn influence over a CM1 weekday.
- **No inspectable trade-off.** Nothing showed why not another chapter, a weak area, or revision. For me that is mandatory before I change what I study.

### 4. Did the Coach explain enough?

No.

Coach restated the mission in slightly longer form. It did not cite practice results, syllabus position, time-to-exam pressure, or anything I could check against my handwritten notes. For someone who reads the CMP carefully, “studying this topic now is expected to strengthen your exam readiness” is a claim without a derivation. Useful coaching would show working. This did not.

### 5. Did Readiness appear evidence-based?

The empty state did. Saying “not enough history” is honest.

Analytics’ description of future readiness inputs also felt evidence-oriented.

What did *not* feel evidence-based was using “readiness” / “learning evidence” language in Coach while Readiness itself was empty. Evidence-based systems either show the data or withhold the conclusion. Mixing both on one page undermines the metric.

### 6. Would I follow the app’s advice without checking my own notes?

No.

I would open the CMP index and my notes before accepting any daily focus — especially with 10 weeks to CM1. The app has not earned unilateral authority. At best it could propose a candidate topic that I verify.

### 7. Did I ever feel manipulated?

Not emotionally manipulated — no guilt, streaks-as-pressure, or panic language tonight.

I did feel **epistemically pushed**: authoritative phrases (“highest-value,” “expected to strengthen readiness,” “Reduce decisions”) ask me to accept conclusions without showing inputs. That is the form of persuasion I distrust most. I would rather a blunt “next unfinished syllabus topic” than a polished claim of optimisation.

### 8. Which recommendation felt the most credible?

The active Study Session explanation that today’s work is the next Current Learning Topic on the official syllabus path in Learning Mode — plus the Analytics honesty that practice (not mere completion) feeds Estimated Knowledge / readiness later.

That is a rule I can reason about.

### 9. Which recommendation felt the least credible?

Home / Coach: “highest-value next step… based on your recent learning evidence” while Readiness and Journey are empty.

Runner-up: Session Overview with “Core methods,” “your topic,” and no activities — a recommendation without a reason or a plan.

### 10. If this app became part of my daily study routine, would it increase or decrease my confidence in my preparation?

**Slightly decrease**, if I treated its advice as authoritative.

Why: opaque or contradictory “why” statements would make me second-guess both the app *and* my own plan. Confidence in CM1 prep, for me, comes from knowing the syllabus spine, working examples until the method is clear, and tracking weak spots in my notes. An app that reduces decisions without showing its working competes with that process.

**If** it consistently exposed a simple, checkable rule (e.g. next unfinished topic; or revise X because accuracy on recorded practice was Y) and kept durations/topic names consistent across screens, it could *increase* confidence by removing evening planning friction — while I still verify against the CMP. Tonight it is closer to the first case than the second.

## Bottom line

I want an app that shows its working. Tonight Kwalitec sometimes *labels* a why and sometimes *states a simple syllabus rule* — but too often it asserts “highest-value” and “learning evidence” without evidence I can see. Until the recommendation and the explanation match, I will keep deciding from my notes and the CMP, and treat Kwalitec as optional structure, not authority.
