# Blind Review SV-008

**Reviewer:** Rachel Evans  
**Age:** 27  
**Country:** United Kingdom  
**Exam:** IFoA CM1 (preparing for a possible second sitting)  
**Occupation:** Graduate Actuarial Consultant  
**Date:** 24 July 2026  
**Context:** 8 PM on results day. Mark: 56%. Failed. Spent six months believing I would pass. Have not decided whether to attempt again.

**Package confirmed:** Student-facing build in the review package — **Kwalitec v2.0.0 · Internal Alpha · Founding Cohort · Build RC2**. CM1 is supported. I reviewed only what a student can see and do.

---

## What I brought into the room

I do not want motivation.

I want evidence that another six months would not be the same six months.

Tonight I opened Kwalitec reluctantly. Not hopeful. Not curious. Just one question:

*Could this help me avoid failing again?*

---

## How I used it tonight

Signed in with the credentials I was given. The login line said “Know exactly what to study next.” That is quieter than the messages I have been getting all day. I stayed.

### Home

Landed somewhere that said what I should do next, and why. **Today’s Mission — 30 minutes.** Start Session.

I read that number twice. Thirty minutes. Not a timetable. Not a six-month plan. Just tonight.

Underneath: Readiness with no history yet. Journey with no story yet. Coach telling me to focus on this topic next because it is the “highest-value next step” based on “recent learning evidence,” and that studying it should strengthen exam readiness.

I have six months of learning evidence. None of it is here. The panels are empty because I am new to *this* system — not because I am new to CM1. On results day, empty progress feels like being reset to zero. I already feel reset to zero.

I did not feel cheered up. I also did not feel shouted at. The tone is calm. Clinical. That suited me tonight better than pep talk would have.

### Journey

Opened Journey. “Where you are on the path to exam readiness.”

**0% complete.** Empty bar.

I sat with that for a while. Part of me thought: at least it is honest. Part of me thought: I cannot look at another empty path after today. Restarting *looked* possible in the mechanical sense — there is a button, there is a next step — but emotionally, 0% after a fail is hard to swallow. It made restarting feel like starting from nothing, which is exactly what I am afraid of.

### Coach

I read the Coach panel carefully. I wanted something that said: *this attempt can be different because we will catch the gaps that a mark of 56% usually hides.*

What I got was confident language about highest-value next steps and learning evidence I do not yet have in the app. It did not make me feel more hopeful. It made me feel directed. Direction is useful. Hope is something else. Tonight I needed the second as much as the first, and I did not get it.

### Mission / Session

I started a session the way the product wanted me to.

On the fuller session path I got a named topic, a reason for studying it, what success looks like, and a short checklist: read, examples, practice, review mistakes. Estimated time disagreed with the Home card (30 vs 90). I chose the smaller number in my head and stopped when I was done. I am too tired to argue with the product about minutes.

I worked from my own materials. Tick boxes. Finish. Then: how did practice go? Questions attempted. Questions correct.

Recording “questions correct” on the evening I learned I failed felt sharp. Not because the screen was cruel — it was not — but because numbers are how I measure myself, and today a number already hurt me. I filled it in anyway. There was a way to close without practice results. I noticed that. It felt like a small mercy.

I did not fill in product feedback forms. I am not here to rate an app tonight.

Then I closed the laptop.

## Answers

### 1. Did Kwalitec make me feel understood?

Partially.

Understood as a tired student who needs one clear next step: yes. The thirty-minute mission and the “reduce decisions” line met me where I actually am at 8 PM.

Understood as someone who failed CM1 this morning with 56% after six months of disciplined work: no. Nothing in what I saw spoke to that situation. The product treated me like any other learner with an empty history. I am not any other learner tonight.

### 2. At any point did I feel judged?

Not by the product’s tone.

No scolding. No “you should have…”. No motivational shame. The language is impersonal in a way that protected me.

The only place judgment crept in was internal: typing how many I got right after a fail, and staring at 0% on Journey. Those moments activated my own self-criticism. The app did not add to it. It also did not cushion it.

### 3. Did the application acknowledge that students sometimes fail?

No.

Not in copy. Not in Journey. Not in Coach. Not after the session.

There is no sense that resitting is a normal part of this exam path, or that a near-miss mark is a different kind of restart than a first attempt. For someone deciding whether to sit again, that silence is loud.

### 4. Did today’s mission feel achievable after such a disappointing day?

Yes — the thirty-minute framing did.

I could do thirty minutes. I could not have faced a rebuilt six-month plan tonight. Naming one objective and letting me stop was the kindest structural choice I encountered. That is what got me through a session at all.

### 5. Did the Coach make me feel more hopeful?

No.

More oriented, maybe. Not more hopeful.

Hope, for me, would sound like: *here is how the next attempt will not repeat the last one.* The Coach repeated the mission in softer words. On results night, that is not enough.

### 6. Did Readiness increase or reduce my anxiety?

It increased it slightly.

“Not enough history for a trend yet” is fair for a new account. Emotionally, after failing an exam I prepared for half a year, being told there is no readiness signal yet feels like standing in the dark again. I came looking for evidence I would not fail the same way. An empty Readiness panel cannot give that. Waiting for a trend to appear requires trust I do not have tonight.

### 7. Did Journey make restarting feel possible?

Mechanically, yes. Emotionally, barely.

There is a path. There is a button. There is language about exam readiness ahead of me.

But **0% complete** on the night of a fail made restarting feel like erasure, not recovery. I need restarting to feel like *continuing differently*, not like wiping the board. Journey did not give me that distinction.

### 8. At what moment did I feel most encouraged?

When I saw **30 minutes** on Today’s Mission and a single Start button.

Not because it promised a pass. Because it asked for something I could still do on the worst day of my exam year. Achievable is the only form of encouragement I can accept tonight.

### 9. At what moment did I think, “This won’t change anything”?

Two moments, honestly.

First: Coach and mission copy talking about “recent learning evidence” while every evidence panel was empty. That felt like the same kind of confident study narrative I already lived through — structure without proof it would catch what I missed.

Second: Journey at 0%. If the story of my preparation starts again from nothing, I cannot see yet how this prevents another 56%.

### 10. After one session, would I commit to another CM1 attempt using Kwalitec?

**Not yet.**

I would not commit to another sitting based on one evening. Commitment, for me, needs evidence that the *method* is different — that weak topics get found earlier, that readiness means something before results day, that six months will not dissolve into the same blind spots.

What I will say is narrower:

I would open Kwalitec again tomorrow for another short session. The daily loop is gentle enough for someone who is emptied out. If, over a few weeks, Readiness and Journey start showing a story that feels true — and if Coach begins to explain *why this, not that* in a way that maps to real gaps — then I could imagine committing to a second attempt *with* this beside me.

Tonight: I finished thirty minutes. That is all I can promise. It is more than I expected of myself at 8 PM on results day. It is not yet a plan for six more months.

## What I needed tonight

- Something that asked for less than a rebuilt life.
- A tone that did not celebrate or scold.
- A reason to believe the next six months would not be a rerun.

## What I got

- A thirty-minute mission I could finish.
- Calm direction without judgment.
- Empty readiness, empty journey, and coaching language that assumes evidence I do not have here yet.

## Bottom line (results night)

Kwalitec did not hurt me. That matters more than it sounds.

It also did not answer the only question I brought: whether another attempt would be different. It helped me study for half an hour when I thought I could not. It did not help me decide to sit CM1 again.

I will come back tomorrow if I can face the screen. I will not call that a commitment. Commitment requires proof. Tonight I only have a finished session and a mark of 56% still sitting in my chest.
