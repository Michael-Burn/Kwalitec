# Blind Review SV-009

**Reviewer:** Alex Morgan  
**Age:** 29  
**Country:** United Kingdom  
**Exam:** IFoA CS1 (second sitting)  
**Occupation:** Senior Actuarial Analyst  
**Date:** 24 July 2026  
**Context:** One reluctant evening evaluation after a colleague insisted I try it. Laptop after work. Existing stack left open beside me: CMP, past papers, Anki, Notion, Excel tracker, OneNote, notebook, Google Calendar.

**Package confirmed:** Student-facing build in the review package — **Kwalitec v2.0.0 · Internal Alpha · Founding Cohort · Build RC2**. CS1 is supported. I reviewed only what a student can see and do. I ignored engineering documentation.

**Question I held all evening:** *What would I stop using if I adopted this?*

---

## What I brought into the room

I am not looking for another study app.

I already have a workflow I have refined for years. It survives busy weeks, fails, and second sittings. Changing it has a real cost.

My colleague said I needed to try Kwalitec. I gave it one evening. Not to be impressed. To see whether anything in my stack becomes redundant.

---

## How I used it tonight

Signed in with the credentials I was given. Login line: “Know exactly what to study next.” That is the only claim that interested me. Everything else I already own.

### Dashboard / Home

Landed on a Learning Workspace Dashboard with **Today’s Study Session** — a named CS1 topic and a Start button. Also found a second Home with **Today’s Mission (30 minutes)**, Readiness, Journey, Coach.

Two “what to do next” surfaces. My Calendar already answers when. My Excel tracker already answers what is overdue. Having two homes inside one product does not replace either of those tools; it just adds a third place to reconcile.

I stayed with the fuller Session path from the Dashboard.

### Study Plan

Opened Study Plan long enough to confirm CS1 and a sitting. Fine as a container for exam + weekly minutes. I already hold sitting date, weekly hours, and topic order in Notion and Google Calendar. This did not make those feel optional.

### Mission / Session

Session briefing: topic, why this session, success checklist, recommended activities (read, examples, practice, review mistakes). Estimated time disagreed with the Home card (30 vs 90). I ignored the conflict and studied for the block I had booked.

I worked from the **CMP** and a past paper set, same as any Tuesday. Ticked Kwalitec’s activity boxes while I did it. The app directed; it did not supply notes, worked examples, or questions. That is important for substitution: a director is not a library.

Finished. Recorded questions attempted / correct. Useful as a log entry. My Excel sheet already stores attempt counts by topic with more columns I care about (paper source, timing band, error type). Tonight’s capture was thinner.

### Journey / History / Analytics / Revision / Coach

**Journey:** path language and early progress. Looks like a progress story. My Notion syllabus map already does that with more control.

**History / Analytics:** charts and summaries that will fill with sessions. Overlaps the job of my Excel revision tracker and weekly review tab. Not richer than what I already build. Not linked to Anki ease or past-paper codes.

**Revision:** adaptive revision options once there is enough progress. Empty / thin for a fresh account tonight. Even when populated, I do not see spaced-repetition scheduling, card content, or failure-mode tagging. That is Anki’s job in my stack. This does not threaten Anki.

**Coach:** “highest-value next step” based on “learning evidence.” On night one the evidence is thin, so the advice mostly restates the mission. I trust explanations with working. Tonight I got direction without a clear competing-topic rationale I could audit against my tracker.

I did not migrate anything. I closed the laptop with every existing tool still open.

## Answers

### 1. Did Kwalitec replace anything in my existing workflow?

**No — not in a way that lets me delete or stop maintaining anything.**

After one evening, the only slot it *competes* for is “decide tonight’s topic.” I already solve that with Calendar + Excel + Notion. Kwalitec offered a third answer, not a replacement answer I would trust enough to retire the others.

It did not replace CMP, past papers, Anki, Notion, Excel, OneNote, the notebook, or Calendar.

### 2. If not, why not?

Because mature workflows fail at the *content and evidence* layer, not at the *button* layer.

| Tool | What it does for me | What Kwalitec did tonight |
|---|---|---|
| CMP | Official learning content | Pointed at a topic; did not teach it |
| Past papers | Exam-shaped practice | Asked me to record counts after I practiced elsewhere |
| Anki | Spaced recall of definitions / methods | No cards, no intervals, no review queue |
| Notion | Notes, syllabus map, sitting wiki | Progress story / plan shell — not my notes |
| Excel | Custom topic scores, paper logs, error taxonomy | Basic attempted/correct + charts later |
| OneNote / notebook | Working and thinking space | Session checklist only |
| Google Calendar | When I study | Assumed I would show up; did not schedule me |

Kwalitec reduces a decision. It does not absorb the artefacts those tools hold. I will not abandon tools that still hold irreplaceable data.

Also: two homes and disagreeing session durations made me less willing to treat it as the single source of truth. A replacement has to be authoritative. Tonight it was advisory and slightly inconsistent.

### 3. Which feature had the highest practical value?

**Today’s Study Session / Mission — the daily “do this next” briefing with activity checklist.**

That is the only feature that touched a real friction in second-sitting life: opening the evening without debating the syllabus again. The checklist matched how I already use the CMP (read → examples → practice → mistakes). Practical. Contained. Still dependent on materials I bring myself.

Everything else was either empty, duplicative, or not yet earning trust.

### 4. Which feature duplicated tools I already own?

Several, clearly:

- **Study Plan** ↔ Notion sitting page + Google Calendar hours  
- **Journey** ↔ Notion / notebook syllabus progress map  
- **History / Analytics** ↔ Excel revision tracker and weekly review  
- **Practice outcome capture** ↔ the log columns I already type into Excel  
- **Revision** (as framed) ↔ a weaker cousin of what Anki already owns for recall  
- **Coach “what next”** ↔ the priority sort I already run in Excel when I am honest with myself  

Duplication without displacement is noise. Tonight that was the pattern.

### 5. Would I remove Anki because of Kwalitec?

**No.**

Anki is recall infrastructure. Kwalitec is session direction. Different jobs. Nothing I saw generates or schedules flashcards, or preserves years of CS1 card history. Removing Anki for this would be malpractice for my second sitting.

### 6. Would I remove Notion?

**No.**

Notion holds notes, links, sitting logistics, and the personal wiki I actually search. Kwalitec’s plan/journey views do not store my explanations or CMP page references. I would at most paste “tonight’s Kwalitec topic” into Notion — which is integration theatre, not replacement.

### 7. Would I remove Excel?

**No.**

Excel is where I keep the evidence trail I trust for a resit: topic heat, paper performance, timing, error types. Kwalitec’s attempted/correct capture and future charts are a subset. I will not trade a model I control for charts I cannot reshape.

### 8. Would I stop maintaining my own revision tracker?

**Not yet. Probably not at all on this evidence.**

If Kwalitec’s History / Analytics / Readiness eventually showed topic-level weakness with explanations I could audit — and stayed consistent across surfaces — I might *stop updating the “what next” column* in Excel and let Kwalitec propose the nightly focus.

I would still maintain the detailed tracker. The nightly decision layer is the only piece that looked replaceable in principle. Tonight it was not replaceable in practice.

### 9. If I adopted Kwalitec, what would become simpler?

Only one thing, honestly:

**The first five minutes of an evening study block** — less syllabus dithering, a named topic, a short activity list.

Session logging might become slightly simpler if I accepted thinner practice capture. That would also make my evidence *worse*, so I would not call that a net win.

Calendar, Anki reviews, CMP reading, past-paper blocks, Notion notes — none of those get simpler. They get an extra login beside them.

### 10. If I ignored Kwalitec completely, what would I genuinely lose?

**Almost nothing I cannot already do.**

I would lose an automated nightly topic suggestion and a packaged session checklist. I already produce both manually with lower glamour and higher control.

I would not lose content, recall scheduling, notes, calendar integrity, or my resit evidence model.

For someone with a mature stack, ignoring Kwalitec tonight has a near-zero educational cost. That is the substitution verdict.

### 11. Six months from now, do I believe I would still be using it?

**Unlikely — unless it proves it finds gaps my tracker misses.**

Why:

I only keep tools that either (a) hold unique artefacts, or (b) beat my current decision process on evidence. Tonight Kwalitec does neither. It is a polite director sitting on top of materials I already own.

I am patient. I would give it two or three weeks of parallel use: Kwalitec proposes the night; I compare against Excel priority; I watch whether Coach / Readiness explanations become auditable. If after that it is still “mission restated as insight,” and Journey/Analytics remain a thinner mirror of my sheet, I will stop opening it without drama.

Habit resistance is not stubbornness. It is cost control. Second sittings do not have room for redundant systems.

## Bottom line

Kwalitec did not earn a deletion in my stack.

It answered “what should I study next?” in a tidy way. That is real, and it is the highest practical value I saw. It did not answer “what becomes obsolete?” with anything I would actually remove.

For a second-sitting CS1 student with CMP, past papers, Anki, Notion, Excel, and Calendar already working: adopting Kwalitec adds a director. It does not simplify the system enough to justify the habit change — not on one evening, and not on the substitution evidence available in this student-facing build.

I will tell my colleague: interesting nightly pointer; keep your tracker.
