**Reviewer:** Sophie Turner  
**Age:** 26  
**Country:** United Kingdom  
**Exam:** IFoA CM1 (first sitting)  
**Weeks until exam:** 9  
**Occupation:** Graduate Actuarial Consultant  
**Date:** 24 July 2026  
**Context:** Evening after work. Roughly one focused study block on the laptop with the CMP open beside me. I was not looking for bugs. I was asking, the whole time: *did this help me make a better study decision than I would have made alone?*

**Package confirmed:** Student-facing build in the review package — **Kwalitec v2.0.0 · Internal Alpha · Founding Cohort · Build RC2**. CM1 is listed as supported (with CS1 and CB2). I reviewed only the educational experience a student can see and use. I ignored engineering documentation.

**Belief I brought in:** Most students don’t fail because they don’t work hard. They fail because they spend time on the wrong things. My problem is not motivation. It is deciding: stay on this topic, move on, practise, or reread the CMP.

## How I used it

Signed in with the credentials from the coordinator.

Landed on the Learning Workspace **Student Dashboard**. Today’s Study Session was already pointed at one topic — in this workspace, the first syllabus block for the active plan — marked **In Progress**, with **Resume Study Session**. The dashboard framed the day as “what to study today, and whether you are on track.”

I also opened the student **Home / Dashboard** (“What you should do next, and why”). That surface showed:

- **Today’s Mission** — 30 minutes; “highest-value next step… based on your recent learning evidence”
- **Readiness** — not enough history for a trend yet
- **Learning journey** — empty until sessions complete
- **Coach insight** — focus on this topic next; same “highest-value / learning evidence / exam readiness” language
- **Quick actions** — Start Session, Review Reflection, Open Schedule

Then I did a normal study path:

1. Confirmed **Study Plan** (exam, sitting, roadmap of topics with weights and estimated hours).  
2. Opened **Session** → Today’s Study Session (one focused objective; estimated **90 min**; success criteria; recommended activities: read → examples → practice → review mistakes).  
3. **Resumed** into the active Study Session (timer, activity checklist, session plan including “Attempt 8 practice questions”).  
4. Worked from official materials outside the app, ticking prompts as I went.  
5. **Finished** and reached **Practice Outcome Capture** (questions attempted / correct, optional notes) — the natural end of the decision loop for the day.  
6. Checked **Journey** (0% complete), **Revision** (no revision focus; keep today’s session), and **History** (not enough sessions / readiness trend yet).

I did not redesign anything. I asked only whether my decisions got better.

## Answers

### 1. Did today’s mission change what I would normally have studied?

**Yes.**

Left alone after work, I usually open the CMP at whichever chapter still feels uncomfortable, or I jump to practice questions I already halfway recognise because it feels productive. Today the mission forced a single topic and a single session. I did not spend the first twenty minutes negotiating with myself about which chapter “deserved” the evening.

That is a real behaviour change for me. The content of the pick mattered less than the fact that there *was* a pick I was willing to follow.

### 2. Did the application help me stop studying the wrong thing?

**Partly — at the topic level. Not inside the topic.**

It helped me stop **topic-hopping**. One objective for the day. Revision also told me not to divert into revision yet (“No revision is recommended right now. Keep going with today’s session.”). With nine weeks to go, that is useful. Premature revision is one of my usual traps.

It did **not** help me stop the *within-topic* mistakes I actually make:

- staying too long in the notes because they feel safer than questions  
- re-reading a section I already understand  
- practising questions that flatter me instead of exposing weak spots  
- not knowing when “enough” has been done on this chapter to move on

The activity list (read / examples / practice / review mistakes) is a sensible order. It is not a stop rule. “Finish when today’s planned study is done” still leaves *me* to decide what “done” means.

### 3. Did Coach improve any of my study decisions?

**No meaningful ones.**

Coach restated the mission in softer language: focus on this topic; highest-value next step; based on recent learning evidence; expected to strengthen exam readiness.

I already had the topic from the mission. Coach did not tell me whether to keep reading, switch to questions, or leave this topic for tomorrow. And “learning evidence” sat beside Readiness saying there is not enough history for a trend. That combination does not improve my decisions. It makes me less sure the advice is earned.

I treated Coach as colour, not counsel.

### 4. Did Journey influence my priorities?

**Not today.**

Journey showed overall progress at **0% complete** and invited me to “Explore your journey.” That is orientation, not prioritisation. It did not change what I opened, how long I stayed, or whether I practised versus reread.

With more history it might matter. On this session it did not.

### 5. Did Readiness affect today’s choices?

**No.**

Readiness was honest: not enough history yet; detail will appear as I study. I started the mission anyway. Readiness neither pushed me toward a harder topic nor told me I was behind. For a first sitting with nine weeks left, an empty readiness gauge is fine — it just means readiness is not yet a decision tool.

### 6. Did I spend less time deciding what to do?

**Yes — for “what topic tonight.”**  
**No — for “what to do *inside* the topic.”**

Opening the app removed the syllabus-browsing argument with myself. Start / Resume was obvious within seconds.

Once I was in the CMP, I still spent time on my usual micro-decisions: another example or a question bank; one more read-through or move on; eight questions enough or stay longer. The session plan’s “Attempt 8 practice questions” helped a little — a number is better than a vibe — but the harder judgement calls remained mine.

### 7. Which recommendation actually changed my behaviour?

**Today’s Mission / Start (or Resume) Study Session on a single topic.**

That is the one I obeyed. I studied the nominated topic instead of grazing. The recommended activities nudged me to include practice rather than only notes, which I often skip when tired. Recording practice at the end is also a behaviour I would not always enforce alone.

### 8. Which recommendation did I ignore? Why?

I ignored or sidelined:

- **Coach insight** — redundant with the mission; evidence language felt unearned while Readiness was empty.  
- **Journey / Explore your journey** — no priority signal today.  
- **Review Reflection / Open Schedule** (quick actions) — not useful mid-evening when the decision I needed was already “start the mission.”  
- **Home’s 30-minute mission duration** when Session said **90 minutes** for the same day — I followed the Learning Workspace session path and treated 30 minutes as a soft prompt I did not trust as the real plan.

Why: I only change behaviour when the recommendation answers a decision I am actually stuck on. Tonight I was stuck on *what to study*. Coach, Journey, and the thinner Home clock did not answer *how long to stay* or *when to move on* any better than I already could.

### 9. After today’s session, do I believe I made better study decisions than yesterday?

**Slightly better — on topic choice. Not clearly better — on time allocation inside the topic.**

Yesterday (without this): more wandering, more comfort practice.  
Today (with this): one topic, structured prompts, intention to record practice.

I still finished unsure whether I should have moved on earlier or stayed longer in the notes. That is the decision that actually costs me marks over a sitting. Kwalitec has not owned that decision yet.

### 10. Would I trust Kwalitec to help me make better decisions over the next three months? Why?

**Conditional trust — yes for daily focus; not yet for sitting strategy.**

I would trust it to:

- remove the nightly “which chapter?” argument  
- keep me on one objective instead of hopping  
- push me toward practice prompts and recording outcomes  
- hold me back from revision too early while the syllabus is still mostly untouched

I would **not** yet trust it to:

- tell me when a topic is “done enough” with nine weeks left  
- trade off CMP re-read versus question practice on a weak area  
- use Readiness / Coach as real guidance until those panels have history that matches my results  
- reconcile conflicting signals (30 vs 90 minutes; “learning evidence” vs empty readiness)

Over three months I would keep using the mission as my default start. I would still make the hard continue / move-on / practise decisions myself until the product can answer those with something more specific than “highest-value next step.”

## Central verdict

**Does Kwalitec improve the quality of students’ study decisions?**

**Yes for the decision “what should I study tonight?”**  
**Not yet for the decisions I fail on most: should I continue, move on, practise, or reread?**

That is still the gap between a useful planner and a decision partner. I would use it again tomorrow morning for the mission. I would not yet hand it my CM1 sitting strategy.
