**Date:** 24 July 2026  
**Context:** Evening after work. About an hour on the laptop with the CMP beside me. I was not hunting for bugs. I was trying to work out how the system decides.

**Package confirmed:** Student-facing build in the review package — **Kwalitec v2.0.0 · Internal Alpha · Founding Cohort · Build RC2**. CS1 is supported. I reviewed only the current student experience. I ignored engineering documents.

**Question I held the whole time:** *If I understand how the system thinks, I know when to trust it — can I form that model from what the app actually says?*

---

## How I used it

Signed in with the credentials from the coordinator. Landed on the Learning Workspace **Student Dashboard**.

Today’s Study Session was **Review CS1-A: Descriptive statistics foundations** for IFoA CS1, marked **In Progress**, with **Resume Study Session**. That matched the start of the syllabus, which was reassuring — but I still wanted the *rule*, not just a sensible first topic.

### Looking for the rule

I opened **Why this session**. That is where the product finally spoke plainly:

> In Learning Mode, today’s mission follows your **Current Learning Topic** in this study plan — the **next syllabus topic you have not yet completed studying**.

That is a model I can hold. Ordered syllabus. One current topic. Mission = that topic until it is completed studying.

I opened **Recommendations needing attention**. It pointed at **Study 1.1 The Purpose And Function Of Data Analysis** (High / New Topic), with **Details**:

- **Observed Facts:** next syllabus topic is 1.1; 0% of the curriculum covered  
- **Educational Advice:** continue syllabus order to advance Study Progress  

Same rule, restated with facts. That is the kind of working I want.

The Dashboard also separated **Learning Progress / Study Progress** (syllabus coverage weighted by the official syllabus) from **Estimated Knowledge** (needs recorded practice; completing a topic alone is not understanding). That is a clean systems distinction: *coverage* versus *demonstrated practice*.

### Study Plan

Opened **Study Plan**. Curriculum Roadmap: 14 topics in syllabus sections with weights and estimated hours, all Not Started. Topic 1 matches the spirit of what today’s session and the recommendation are pointing at. The roadmap makes the “next unfinished topic” story feel grounded. Learning Outcomes still say “Not available yet,” but the sequence is visible.

### Session briefing

**Session** → Today’s Study Session. Same CS1-A topic. Estimated time **90 min**. **Why you are studying this** mostly said to resume and finish — operational, not causal — until I opened **Session guidance**. That expanded into the same Learning Mode explanation, plus:

- Learning objective: advance Study Progress along the official syllabus, one topic at a time  
- Observed facts about today’s topic, active plan, 0 of 14 completed  
- Reminder that Estimated Knowledge is separate  

Inside the active session, the learning objective and Learning Mode line appear again without needing a click. Recommended activities are labelled as prompts, not scored practice. Practice results come when you finish. That fits: the session advances study progress; practice feeds a different estimate later.

### The other Home / Coach

I also opened the student **Home / Dashboard** (“What you should do next, and why”).

**Today’s Mission:** 30 minutes. Copy: *highest-value next step for this topic based on your recent learning evidence.*

**Coach insight:** same register — focus on this topic next; highest-value next step based on recent learning evidence; expected to strengthen exam readiness.

That is a **different story**. “Learning evidence” sounds adaptive: something about my performance choosing the topic. The Learning Workspace said: next unfinished syllabus topic in Learning Mode. I barely have evidence yet. Readiness even says not enough history for a trend. So Coach is using confident evidence-language when the clearest rule I found does not need evidence at all.

Home also said **30 minutes** while Session said **90 min** for the same day. Same decision, two clocks. That does not break the topic rule, but it does make me less sure which surface is authoritative.

I tried **Start Session** from Home once. **Session Overview** showed “Core methods,” about 30 minutes, **No activities listed**. That did not look like CS1-A / 1.1. For someone trying to predict the system, a third topic label on a third surface is noise. I went back to the Learning Workspace path.

### Journey, Revision, History, Analytics

**Journey:** path to exam readiness, 0% complete. Does not explain *why today’s topic*. Progress theatre, not selection logic.

**Revision:** no revision recommended; keep going with today’s session. Consistent with “learn first, revise later,” but thin.

**History:** empty sessions / readiness trend. Fair for early use. Does not teach the decision rule.

**Analytics:** more useful for the *measurement* model than the *mission* model. Estimated readiness uses syllabus coverage, Estimated Knowledge from recorded practice, and recent review habits. Charts warn that readiness / accuracy depend on what I record. That helps me understand what the gauges mean. It still does not show a scored shortlist of why topic A beat topic B today — and in Learning Mode, that may not be how selection works anyway.

**Help** was release info and feedback links. It did not explain how missions are chosen.

## My working model (in my own words)

From what the application **communicates** when I dig into the Learning Workspace:

1. I have a study plan for IFoA CS1 with an ordered syllabus roadmap.  
2. In **Learning Mode**, today’s mission is the **Current Learning Topic**: the next syllabus topic I have not yet **completed studying**.  
3. Doing the session is meant to advance **Study Progress** along that official order.  
4. **Estimated Knowledge** and readiness are fed by **recorded practice** later. They are explicitly not the same as finishing a topic.  
5. Until I complete studying the current topic, I should expect the same focus to keep coming back (resume / continue), not a random jump.

What I **cannot** cleanly reconcile yet:

- Coach / Home “highest-value next step based on **recent learning evidence**” versus Learning Mode “next unfinished syllabus topic.”  
- Topic naming: CS1-A descriptive statistics vs 1.1 purpose of data analysis vs “Core methods” on Session Overview.  
- Whether a bad practice day would **change tomorrow’s mission**, or only change Estimated Knowledge / readiness / later revision — the screens disagree in tone.

I am not inventing a neural ranking. I am reporting the rule the product *stated*, and the places it *sounded* like a different rule.

## Answers

### 1. By the end of the session, could I explain how Kwalitec chooses today’s mission?

**Mostly yes — if I stay with the Learning Workspace explanation.**

Today’s mission is the Current Learning Topic: the next syllabus topic not yet completed studying, in Learning Mode, from my CS1 study plan.

**Only partly** if I only read Home and Coach. Those surfaces say “highest-value” and “learning evidence” without showing the evidence or the ranking. I would not trust that wording as the real rule.

### 2. Which parts of the application helped me understand that process?

- **Why this session** on the Student Dashboard (Learning Mode / Current Learning Topic)  
- **Recommendations → Details** (Observed Facts + Educational Advice: syllabus order advances Study Progress)  
- **Session guidance** on Today’s Study Session (same Learning Mode rule + observed facts)  
- Active session learning-objective copy (same rule visible while studying)  
- **Study Plan** roadmap (ordered topics; makes “next” concrete)  
- Progress vs Estimated Knowledge copy on Dashboard and Analytics (what the system is and is not optimising for day-to-day)

### 3. Which parts made it harder?

- **Coach** and Home mission copy (“highest-value… recent learning evidence”) without showing working  
- **Two homes** with different duration (30 vs 90) for the same day  
- **Session Overview** from Home Start Session (“Core methods,” no activities) — breaks the topic story  
- Topic labels that do not match across Dashboard / Recommendation / Overview  
- **Journey** framed as exam readiness progress without explaining selection  
- **Help** not explaining the educational model  
- Having to **expand** Why / Details / Session guidance to find the real rule — the default visible line is often “resume and finish,” which is instruction, not reasoning

### 4. Did different screens tell a consistent story?

**No — not fully.**

Learning Workspace tells a **syllabus-order / Current Learning Topic** story.  
Home / Coach tell an **evidence-weighted highest-value** story.  
Session Overview can tell a **thin / differently labelled** story.

The measurement story (Study Progress ≠ Estimated Knowledge) is more consistent than the selection story.

### 5. Could I predict what tomorrow’s recommendation would probably be?

**Yes, under the Learning Mode rule:** the same Current Learning Topic until I have completed studying it; then the next topic on the Study Plan roadmap.

**Less confidently** from Coach language alone — that wording makes me wonder if weak practice could reshuffle the queue tomorrow. The product never showed me that reshuffle happening. It only asserted “learning evidence.”

### 6. If my performance changed dramatically tomorrow, would I expect the recommendation to change? Why?

**Under the clearest Learning Mode copy: not immediately.**

Why: the mission is defined as the next topic not yet **completed studying**. Poor or strong practice updates practice outcomes / Estimated Knowledge / readiness ingredients. Completing studying is what advances the Current Learning Topic. So a dramatic accuracy swing should change the **gauges**, not necessarily **today’s syllabus slot**, until the topic is marked completed studying or revision mode kicks in later.

**Under Coach wording: maybe.** “Recent learning evidence” implies performance can change the next step. That is why I only half-trust the Coach as an explanation of selection.

I would expect Revision to matter after more syllabus progress — Revision itself said there is no revision focus yet and to keep the day’s session.

### 7. Which feature best explained the application’s reasoning?

**Why this session / Session guidance** — especially the Learning Mode paragraph — together with **Recommendations → Details**.

That is the only place that states a predictive rule in plain language and ties it to observed facts on my plan.

### 8. Which feature felt like a “black box”?

**Coach insight** on Home.

It repeats “highest-value” and “learning evidence” and “exam readiness” without listing alternatives considered, scores, or the unfinished-topic rule. It sounds like a conclusion without a derivation. For me, that is the opposite of trust.

### 9. If a colleague asked, “How does Kwalitec decide what you study?”, could I answer confidently?

**Confidently about Learning Mode on the Learning Workspace path:**

“It puts you on the next unfinished syllabus topic in your study plan and keeps you there until you’ve completed studying it. Practice results feed a separate knowledge/readiness estimate — they are not treated as the same thing as finishing a topic.”

**Not confidently about the whole product:**

I would have to add: “Unless you only look at Coach, which talks as if recent evidence picks the highest-value step — and I never saw that evidence.”

### 10. Did understanding the system increase my trust?

**Yes — once I found Learning Mode.**

Trust rose when the rule became falsifiable: I can look at the roadmap, see what is incomplete, and predict the mission.

Trust fell when Coach used evidence-language that does not match that rule, and when Home / Session Overview disagreed on time and topic labelling. I trust systems that keep one story. Two stories make me double-check everything.

I would use Kwalitec as a syllabus sequencer with an honest practice log — and I would treat Coach’s “highest-value evidence” line as marketing until it shows its working like the Recommendations Details panel does.

## Bottom line

I came in wanting one thing: a mental model I can use to predict the next recommendation.

Kwalitec *does* give me one, in Learning Mode: today’s mission is the Current Learning Topic — the next syllabus topic I have not completed studying — and practice feeds a separate knowledge estimate.

That model increased my trust. It is simple, syllabus-first, and checkable against the Study Plan.

The product also *says* something else on Home and Coach: highest-value next step from recent learning evidence. That sounds smarter and shows less. For someone like me, that is not sophistication. It is a black box standing next to a clear rule.

So: can an ordinary student form an accurate mental model of how Kwalitec works? **Yes, if they find Why this session / Session guidance / Recommendation Details.** **Not reliably if they only follow the Coach.**

I will trust the Learning Mode rule until the app shows me evidence that overrides it. Until Coach shows that working, I will treat it as a paraphrase, not an explanation.
