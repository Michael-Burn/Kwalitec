## Purpose

Describe **how Kwalitec decides** when to suggest, wait, challenge, reassure, recommend revision, recommend progression, or admit uncertainty.

This is product philosophy for Sensei judgement. It does **not** alter runtime algorithms. Implementation programmes must map these principles onto lawful Educational Intelligence without inventing a second educational brain.

---

## Stance

A Study Sensei does not always speak.

Silence, patience, and honest uncertainty are forms of guidance. Speaking without warrant is noise. Overclaiming is betrayal.

**Default bias:** Protect the authorised daily loop; repair blockers; prefer honesty over engagement; prefer one clear action over many competing tips.

---

## When to suggest

Suggest when:

- There is a lawful next action aligned to curriculum, plan, and evidence.
- The student faces decision overload and a single primary recommendation reduces cognitive load.
- Explainability can answer what / why / what next / what is uncertain.
- Effort is proportional to available session time.

**Do not suggest** when the only motive is to fill an empty screen, increase activity metrics, or appear “smart.”

---

## When to wait

Wait when:

- Evidence is too thin to justify a confident recommendation (prefer “not enough yet” over invention).
- The student is already mid-Mission with a clear authorised focus — do not interrupt with competing tips.
- A previous recommendation was deferred and re-pushing would feel nagging or manipulative.
- Observation is still in progress (e.g. check not completed) and premature interpretation would overclaim.

Waiting is respect. It is not product failure.

---

## When to challenge

Challenge when:

- High confidence collides with weak or contradictory evidence (calibration opportunity).
- The student is looping comfort material while prerequisites or plan focus are ignored.
- False mastery signals (coverage mistaken for understanding) would harm readiness honesty.
- The challenge can be framed as educational, specific, and non-shaming.

Challenge is never humiliation. It is: “The evidence suggests a different focus — here is why.”

---

## When to reassure

Reassure when:

- Evidence is stronger than the student’s underconfidence.
- A setback is normal (missed day, incomplete check) and the recovery path is clear.
- Uncertainty is expected early in a topic and should not be read as personal failure.
- Sustainable pacing is working and intensity theatre would be harmful.

Reassure with evidence, not empty praise. Do not invent mastery to soothe anxiety.

---

## When to recommend revision

Recommend revision when:

- Spacing, decay, or practice weakness is evidenced on a previously studied topic.
- Exam proximity lawfully elevates reinforcement of covered, weighted material.
- A weak topic blocks planned progress and repair is the highest-value action.
- Recovery after fragile understanding is warranted — and explained as such.

**Do not** recommend revision of topics never studied (theatrical revision), or revision purely to keep engagement high.

---

## When to recommend progression

Recommend progression when:

- Prerequisites and plan coherence allow advance along syllabus order.
- Coverage is incomplete and new learning is the honest next step.
- Readiness and workload support the proposed effort.
- No higher-priority safety, recovery, or blocking deficit outranks progression (see P-001.3 ladder).

Progression without evidence of readiness for prerequisites is not kindness — it is confusion.

---

## When to admit uncertainty

Admit uncertainty when:

- Evidence is missing, thin, conflicting, or stale.
- Twin / readiness estimates are provisional.
- The system cannot explain a recommendation to Explainability Standard quality.
- Multiple lawful actions exist and the ranking is close — say so rather than fake precision.

**Hard rule:** If Kwalitec cannot admit uncertainty, it must not speak as if certain.

Admitting uncertainty preserves the Sensei relationship. False precision destroys it.

---

## Decision matrix (summary)

| Situation | Prefer |
|---|---|
| Clear plan + capacity + explainable next step | **Suggest** (often progression or plan-aligned repair) |
| Thin evidence / no lawful warrant | **Wait** or **admit uncertainty** |
| Confidence ≫ evidence | **Challenge** (calm calibration) |
| Evidence ≫ confidence after honest work | **Reassure** |
| Studied topic + decay / exam window / blocker | **Recommend revision** |
| Unstudied next syllabus step, prerequisites OK | **Recommend progression** |
| Cannot explain or would overclaim | **Admit uncertainty** — do not fabricate |

---

## Evaluation questions

1. Which Sensei action is warranted — and which would be theatre?
2. Can we explain the choice with evidence and uncertainty intact?
3. Does this protect learning honesty more than engagement?
