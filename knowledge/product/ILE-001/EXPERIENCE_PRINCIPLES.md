# Experience Principles — Adaptive Assessment

**Programme:** ILE-001 — Adaptive Assessment Experience  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Design  
**Effective:** 2026-07-28  

---

## Purpose

Decision filters for Adaptive Assessment UX, copy, and product behaviour. If a proposed interaction conflicts with these principles, change the interaction — or escalate to amend a higher authority (ILE-000 / Constitution).

These extend `USER_EXPERIENCE_PHILOSOPHY.md` and `AP-002/UX_PRINCIPLES.md` for the Adaptive Assessment surface.

---

## 1. Never surprise the learner

Checks appear only with visible framing and lawful Mission / recommendation context.

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Announce intent before the first item | Pop a quiz mid-flow without explanation |
| Show estimated effort (e.g. ~5 minutes) | Hide length until the student is trapped |
| Allow decline / defer when Mission policy allows | Force “you must pass to continue” |

Surprise creates exam anxiety and destroys trust faster than thin evidence does.

---

## 2. Always explain why this learning check exists

Before starting, the student must see a plain answer to:

- Why this check, now?
- What will answers be used for?
- What happens after?

During and after, keep “why” adjacent to the experience — not buried in Help.

If the system cannot explain the check, it must not present it as authoritative guidance.

---

## 3. Respect learner confidence

Self-reported confidence is educational signal, not vanity.

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Invite confidence optionally when useful | Force anxiety-inducing confidence rituals every item |
| Treat high confidence + weak evidence as a calibration opportunity | Mock the student for being wrong while “sure” |
| Treat low confidence + strong evidence as fragility / underconfidence support | Inflate praise that invents mastery |

Confidence UX detail: `CONFIDENCE_AND_UNCERTAINTY_UX.md`.

---

## 4. Avoid repetitive questioning

Repetition without educational reason feels punitive and reduces completion quality.

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Skip items recently answered with strong evidence | Loop the same stem for engagement metrics |
| Space revisits according to revision / uncertainty intent | Re-ask identical items immediately after success “to be sure” without framing |
| Vary confirmation vs exploration within policy | Grind volume as a substitute for teaching |

Policy detail: `QUESTION_SELECTION_POLICY.md`.

---

## 5. Reward consistency

Value showing up and completing honest checks — not perfect accuracy theatre.

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Acknowledge completed checks as useful evidence | Celebrate “100%” as identity |
| Support pause/resume and return-after-gap journeys | Punish broken streaks or missed days |
| Prefer sustainable daily loops | Heroic multi-hour assessment batteries |

Consistency beats intensity (Educational Philosophy).

---

## 6. Protect motivation

Mistakes are data. Incomplete evidence is honesty. Neither is moral failure.

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Use constructive, specific recovery language | Red “FAILED” stamps, shame, or public comparison |
| Keep formative chrome continuous with study sessions | Switch into invigilation / exam skin by default |
| Offer Tutor explanation on request | Auto-lecture after every miss |
| Preserve exit paths | Trap the student until a “pass” |

---

## 7. Be transparent about uncertainty

Unknown, thin, conflicting, or stale evidence must remain visible as uncertainty.

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Say what is still unsure and how to reduce it | Fake precision with decorative scores |
| Label provisional estimates | Speak as if one check settled mastery |
| Separate “we collected evidence” from “we conclude competence” | Overclaim Twin belief in marketing copy |

---

## 8. One job per screen

Stem → response → optional confidence. No dashboard clutter, competing CTAs, or analytics chrome during the check.

---

## 9. Time is sacred

Respect the Mission’s published duration and the student’s stated availability.

Prefer a shorter, complete Quick Check over an abandoned Deep Check.

---

## 10. One educational truth

Assessment outcomes must not contradict Home’s primary next action or invent a shadow recommendation path.

Post-check “what next” must reflect Twin / Mission authority already decided — Tutor only narrates.

---

## 11. Continuity with study, not exam culture

Adaptive Assessment is part of the Education Operating System companion: calm, professional, syllabus-honest.

High-stakes exam simulation — if ever productised — is a **separate**, explicitly consented experience. It is not the default Adaptive Assessment skin.

---

## Evaluation questions (design / review)

Before shipping an Adaptive Assessment interaction, answer yes to all:

1. Would I feel surprised or tested if this appeared in my study session?
2. Can a tired candidate explain why this check exists in one sentence?
3. Does incomplete or weak evidence stay honest?
4. Does this reduce decisions and increase learning — or only increase questions?

If any answer fails, redesign.
