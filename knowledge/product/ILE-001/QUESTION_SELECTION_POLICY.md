# Question Selection Policy

**Programme:** ILE-001 — Adaptive Assessment Experience  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Design  
**Effective:** 2026-07-28  

---

## Purpose

Product rules for *which kinds of questions* Adaptive Assessment may present, given a session type and educational intent.

This is **policy for learner trust and educational usefulness** — not an item-response algorithm specification.

---

## Policy goals

1. Collect observations that serve the stated intent.  
2. Preserve trust (no trap exams, no pointless repetition).  
3. Fit available study time.  
4. Stay within lawful curriculum scope.  
5. Prefer quality of evidence over quantity of items.

---

## 1. Avoid unnecessary repetition

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Recent strong evidence** | Do not re-ask the same or near-identical item soon after a clear, unassisted success unless the intent is explicit durability (Revision Check) and spaced by design. |
| **Immediate re-probe** | After a miss, prefer teaching / recovery or a *different* angle — not the identical stem as punishment. |
| **Student perception** | If the student would reasonably say “I just did this,” the policy failed unless the frame explains why a revisit is intentional. |

---

## 2. Mix confirmation and exploration

Within a session, balance:

| Mode | Role |
|---|---|
| **Confirmation** | Items that verify whether a believed-understood idea still holds |
| **Exploration** | Items that probe thin, adjacent, or prerequisite uncertainty |

| Session type | Default mix |
|---|---|
| Quick Check | Mostly confirmation of today’s focus; light exploration only if thin evidence |
| Deep Check | Heavier confirmation; limited exploration of known fragile edges |
| Recovery Check | Soft confirmation of foundations; minimal harsh exploration |
| Confidence Check | Mix with confidence prompts; exploration secondary |
| Revision Check | Confirmation of due topics; exploration only for linked prerequisites if decay suspected |
| Readiness Check | Syllabus-weighted confirmation; exploration only where Twin flags critical unknowns |

Never fill time with random exploration when the frame promised a focused confirmation.

---

## 3. Respect available study time

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Budget first** | Item count and richness must fit Mission duration and stated effort estimate |
| **Prefer complete short** | A finished Quick Check beats an abandoned Deep Check |
| **Early stop** | Allow ending when intent is met or time is up — with honest incomplete-evidence messaging if needed |
| **No bait-and-switch** | Do not advertise “3 minutes” then expand silently |

---

## 4. Balance breadth and depth

| Context | Preference |
|---|---|
| Daily learning check | Depth on today’s objective |
| Weak-topic reinforcement | Depth on the weak objective; avoid multi-topic anxiety piles |
| Revision Check | Controlled breadth across due items; cap topics per sitting |
| Recovery after inactivity | Narrow breadth; protect cognitive load |
| Readiness Check | Weighted breadth across sitting-critical areas within time |

Curriculum-first: do not invent off-syllabus “interesting” items for engagement.

---

## 5. Preserve learner trust

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Intent match** | Items must visibly serve the framed purpose |
| **No trick culture** | Ambiguous “gotchas” that teach nothing reduce trust |
| **Hint honesty** | Hints allowed; using a hint is evidence, not moral failure |
| **Fairness** | Same educational inputs → same policy outcome; no random punitive difficulty |
| **Transparency** | If an item is diagnostic vs verification, framing may say so in plain words |
| **No shadow bank** | Do not use secret “test” items that never produce Twin-usable observations |

---

## 6. Prerequisite and safety gates

Before selecting items on an objective:

1. Curriculum entity is supported and retrievable.  
2. Prerequisites respected — or the frame explicitly marks a boundary diagnostic.  
3. Content quality sufficient for formative use (supported subject integrity).  
4. Assessment density / burnout gates already passed at Mission level.

If gates fail: do not select; Mission should schedule study/practice instead.

---

## 7. Evidence quality preferences

Prefer items that:

- Map cleanly to syllabus topics / learning objectives  
- Yield interpretable observations (including misconception tags where available)  
- Distinguish understanding from recognition when the intent requires it (especially Deep / Readiness)

Deprioritise items that only inflate activity without Twin benefit.

---

## 8. Confidence prompts (when used)

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Optional by default | Especially under anxiety-sensitive journeys |
| Sparse | Not every item unless Confidence Check explicitly centres calibration |
| Non-blocking | Skipping confidence does not block submission of the answer |
| Evidence, not grade | Confidence is a soft signal for calibration narrative |

---

## 9. Anti-patterns (reject)

- Maximising questions answered as a KPI  
- Always escalating difficulty after errors  
- Repeating the same weak-topic item set daily without teaching between  
- Mixing unrelated topics to “keep it interesting”  
- Selecting items the pipeline cannot turn into lawful observations  

---

## Policy → student explanation (examples)

| Internal reason | Student-facing why |
|---|---|
| Thin evidence on objective X | “We don’t have much evidence yet on X.” |
| Revision due | “It’s time to see what still holds from earlier work.” |
| Conflicting signals | “Recent checks disagree — this clarifies.” |
| Time budget short | “A short set so it fits today’s session.” |
| Density gate | (Session suppressed) Mission offers study instead |
