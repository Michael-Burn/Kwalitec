## 1. Student problem

**Student problem:**

> Students often cannot see why Coach, Insights, readiness, or plan guidance recommended a particular action. Constitutional explainability exists, but day-to-day speech still feels opaque (“highest-value / learning evidence”), which weakens trust and recommendation follow-through.

**Evidence:**

> KSI baseline K8 = 55 (Partial), below Version 1 floor ≥ 70 (V1-K3). EP-004 blind-review Coach opacity themes; `BASELINE_KSI_ASSESSMENT.md` priority #1 for K8; Architecture Constitution Art. IV vs student-perceived gap noted in P-001.1.

| Design question | Helped? | How |

**Student benefit summary:**

> No immediate student-visible change. Establishes the permanent contract so future EP/P work must ship evidence-backed, confidence-honest, action-clear explanations — the prerequisite for raising perceived trust.

**Final Test:** Does this help students become better professionals? **Yes** — professional study depends on justified priorities; governance that forbids opaque guidance serves that outcome. Benefit is enabling, not yet experiential.

## 10. Lessons learned for student value

> Constitutional explainability without a product schema, length discipline, and review gate does not move student-perceived trust. K8 is blocked less by missing philosophy than by missing enforceable presentation contracts. Mandating evidence, confidence, next action, and Runtime A consistency is the governance lever; live K8 gains still wait on implementation.
