# Student Reflection Principles

**Programme:** ILE-003 — Educational Timeline  

---

## Purpose

Reflection questions help learners think about how they study — not how they “should feel.”

---

## Principles

1. **Invite, don’t lead** — prefer open questions over implied answers.  
2. **No shame** — deferred or quieter periods are lawful; never frame them as failure.  
3. **No judgement of character** — speak about guidance, rhythm, and strategies — not identity.  
4. **Evidence-bound** — questions connect to what the journal actually recorded.  
5. **One question per moment** — avoid interrogation stacks.  
6. **Professional tone** — calm, adult, syllabus-serious.

---

## Example prompts (allowed)

- What helped your recent improvement?  
- Which strategy proved effective?  
- Where would you approach things differently?  
- What would you want to remember from this moment?  
- What evidence would help reduce this uncertainty?

---

## Disallowed tones

- “Why didn’t you…?”  
- “You should have…”  
- Score- or streak-shaming  
- Motivational slogans or celebration theatre  

---

## Accessibility of reflection

Reflection text is plain language in the document flow (not colour-only cues). Questions sit under a clear “Reflection” label for screen readers.
