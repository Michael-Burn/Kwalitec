## 1. Purpose

Change without a closed evolution catalogue invents law by proximity: whichever rewrite, merge, archive, or “clarification” happens to ship becomes the tutor’s “proof the constitution evolved.”

This catalogue names the only lawful constitutional evolution types a governance activity may apply — and binds each type to purpose, scope, inputs, outputs, and permitted / prohibited governance actions.

---

## 2. Catalogue Overview

| ID | Evolution type | Constitutional purpose (short) | Primary inputs | Primary outputs |
|----|----------------|--------------------------------|----------------|-----------------|
| **CEG-01** | Constitutional Amendment | Govern lawful modification of existing published constitutional meaning | Named published artefact(s) + justification + continuity / integrity constraints | Amendment disposition + provenance of prior/successor meaning |
| **CEG-02** | Constitutional Extension | Govern lawful addition of new published constitutional meaning without erasing prior law | Named parent corpus + proposed extension scope + authority map constraints | Extension disposition + additive provenance |
| **CEG-03** | Constitutional Deprecation | Govern lawful marking of published meaning as no longer forward-operative | Named artefact(s) + deprecation rationale + successor pointer if any | Deprecation disposition + preserved historical operability record |
| **CEG-04** | Constitutional Supersession | Govern lawful replacement of published meaning by successor artefacts | Named predecessor + successor + relationship justification | Supersession disposition + predecessor→successor provenance |
| **CEG-05** | Constitutional Consolidation | Govern lawful unification of related published artefacts without silent meaning loss | Named source artefacts + consolidation map + integrity constraints | Consolidation disposition + source→unified provenance |
| **CEG-06** | Constitutional Archival | Govern lawful preservation of non-forward-operative artefacts as historical law | Named artefact(s) + archival rationale + access/continuity constraints | Archival disposition + archival provenance |
| **CEG-07** | Constitutional Restoration | Govern lawful return of previously deprecated / archived / superseded meaning to forward operation | Named restored artefact(s) + restoration rationale + conflict checks | Restoration disposition + restoration provenance |

Material evolution must map to one or more of these types as published law requires. Cross-cutting situations may bind multiple CEG types; none may invent a type outside this catalogue.

**Relation to CC / CT / CV / CCM / CRT / RC / EC / EV:** Those catalogues remain defined solely by their owners. CEG types *govern lawful corpus change*; they do not replace, reclassify, re-verify, re-determine, invent, certify, or amend those catalogues by proximity.

**Relation to educational quality:** No CEG type judges whether learning was good, whether a tip was wise, or whether the student is ready. Evolved ≠ quality. Evolved ≠ silent soft amendment by governance prose. Evolved ≠ frozen stack authority. Evolved ≠ waiver of WS1–WS4.

## 3. CEG-01 — Constitutional Amendment

### Constitutional purpose

Govern the lawful **modification of existing published constitutional meaning** — so educational and execution law may change deliberately while continuity, integrity, authority, and historical provenance remain intact.

### Constitutional scope

Governance of proposed modifications to identifiable published Constitution / EIP / Programme VI / VII / VIII / IX artefacts (or named clauses within them). Does **not** itself perform the textual amendment. Does **not** authorise silent soft amendment in assessment prose, release notes, or product copy. Does **not** freeze a preferred implementation as the amendment standard.

### Constitutional inputs

- Named published constitutional artefact(s) / clause(s) proposed for modification.
- Constitutional justification for amendment (integrity / continuity / authority / educational care — never mere product urgency).
- Continuity and integrity constraints from EIP-005 / EIP-001 / this Model.
- Published CEGO-01 / CEGO-02 / CEGO-03 / CEGO-06 expectations.
- Supporting historical identity of the prior published version.

### Constitutional outputs

- Amendment governance disposition: **permitted** / **refused** / **deferred** / **escalated**.
- Provenance note binding prior published meaning to proposed successor meaning.
- Explicit non-enactment note: governance disposition is not itself the corpus rewrite.
- Boundary preservation note (authority, continuity, non-history-rewrite).

### Permitted governance actions

- Classify a proposed modification as CEG-01 when published artefacts and justification are identifiable.
- Require preservation of prior version identity and successor relationship.
- Refuse amendment theatre that would redefine educational meaning without naming the artefact under change.
- Escalate when published law requires human / corpus stewardship beyond automated disposition.
- Compose with CEG-04 / CEG-05 when amendment is part of a larger supersession or consolidation map.

### Prohibited governance actions

- Directly amend constitutional specification text under the CEG-01 label.
- Treat product urgency, green CI, or certification seals as amendment authority.
- Erase prior published meaning so the amendment looks like “what we always meant.”
- Privilege a language, framework, or reference stack as constitutional amendment law.
- Bypass Constitution / EIP / Programme VI–VIII authorship rules.
- Replace WS1–WS4 assessment meaning by amending “whatever failed” without published justification.
- Narrate governance prose as the completed amendment.

## 4. CEG-02 — Constitutional Extension

### Constitutional purpose

Govern the lawful **addition of new published constitutional meaning** — so the corpus may grow without erasing, silently rewriting, or collapsing prior law.

### Constitutional scope

Governance of additive constitutional artefacts or additive clauses that extend published corpora without replacing them. Does **not** absorb adjacent Programme authority. Does **not** invent educational meaning outside the extension’s published parent authority. Does **not** treat “new feature docs” as automatic constitutional extensions.

### Constitutional inputs

- Named parent corpus / authority map under which the extension would publish.
- Proposed extension identity and scope.
- Integrity constraints ensuring additive publication does not collapse layering.
- Published CEGO-01 / CEGO-02 / CEGO-05 / CEGO-06 expectations.
- Confirmation that prior artefacts remain historically intact.

### Constitutional outputs

- Extension governance disposition: **permitted** / **refused** / **deferred** / **escalated**.
- Additive provenance note (parent corpus → new artefact identity).
- Explicit non-replacement note: extension does not supersede unnamed neighbours by proximity.
- Boundary preservation note.

### Permitted governance actions

- Classify additive publication as CEG-02 when parent authority and scope are identifiable.
- Require that prior law remain speakable after extension.
- Refuse extension theatre that silently redefines existing meaning under “clarification.”
- Require explainability of what is new versus what remains unchanged.
- Compose with CEG-01 only when a true modification accompanies the addition and both are named.

### Prohibited governance actions

- Smuggle amendments into “extensions” without CEG-01 classification.
- Directly publish specification text under the governance label without lawful authorship process.
- Use extension to absorb Programme VI / VII / VIII / IX neighbour authority.
- Treat architecture ADRs, PRDs, or Version 2 design notes as automatic constitutional extensions.
- Privilege a delivery stack as the reason an extension “must” become law.
- Erase or deprecate prior artefacts under an extension label.

## 5. CEG-03 — Constitutional Deprecation

### Constitutional purpose

Govern the lawful **marking of published constitutional meaning as no longer forward-operative** — so obsolete law can retire without pretending it never existed.

### Constitutional scope

Governance of deprecation designations for identifiable published artefacts or clauses. Deprecation preserves historical meaning for continuity and audit; it does not erase provenance. Does **not** require immediate deletion. Does **not** rewrite history to make deprecated meaning look never-lawful.

### Constitutional inputs

- Named published artefact(s) / clause(s) proposed for deprecation.
- Deprecation rationale bound to constitutional integrity / continuity / authority care.
- Successor pointer if forward operation moves elsewhere (may compose with CEG-04).
- Published CEGO-01 / CEGO-03 / CEGO-04 / CEGO-05 expectations.
- Confirmation that historical operability remains reconstructable.

### Constitutional outputs

- Deprecation governance disposition: **permitted** / **refused** / **deferred** / **escalated**.
- Deprecation provenance note (artefact identity, effective boundary, successor if any).
- Historical operability record (what the artefact meant while forward-operative).
- Boundary preservation note (non-erasure, non-retrospective invalidation of prior lawful acts under that meaning).

### Permitted governance actions

- Mark named artefacts as no longer forward-operative under published justification.
- Preserve historical meaning for audit, continuity, and restoration eligibility.
- Require successor clarity when students / developers would otherwise face silent gaps.
- Compose with CEG-04 when deprecation accompanies supersession.
- Compose with CEG-06 when deprecated artefacts move to archival preservation.

### Prohibited governance actions

- Delete or erase deprecated artefacts’ historical identity under the deprecation label.
- Retrospectively declare prior lawful acts under the artefact unconstitutional without published restoration/conflict law.
- Deprecate by vibe, roadmap pressure, or “no one reads this file.”
- Use deprecation to hide failed assessment findings.
- Directly rewrite neighbouring artefacts under deprecation cover.
- Treat deprecation as certification, compliance waiver, or verification skip.

## 6. CEG-04 — Constitutional Supersession

### Constitutional purpose

Govern the lawful **replacement of published constitutional meaning by successor artefacts** — so successors inherit authority honestly while predecessors remain historically speakable.

### Constitutional scope

Governance of predecessor→successor relationships among identifiable published constitutional artefacts. Supersession is replacement of forward operation, not erasure of history. Does **not** silently merge unrelated authorities. Does **not** freeze a stack as the successor standard.

### Constitutional inputs

- Named predecessor artefact(s) and named successor artefact(s).
- Relationship justification (why successor lawfully replaces predecessor forward operation).
- Continuity map of preserved meaning vs intentionally changed meaning.
- Published CEGO-01 / CEGO-02 / CEGO-03 / CEGO-06 expectations.
- Supporting CT / historical identity inputs where lineage already exists.

### Constitutional outputs

- Supersession governance disposition: **permitted** / **refused** / **deferred** / **escalated**.
- Predecessor→successor provenance record.
- Continuity note distinguishing preserved meaning from changed meaning.
- Explicit non-erasure note for the predecessor’s historical identity.

### Permitted governance actions

- Bind predecessor and successor identities under a single supersession disposition.
- Require continuity explanation of what remains educationally / constitutionally true across the change.
- Compose with CEG-03 (deprecation of predecessor) and CEG-06 (archival of predecessor) when published law requires.
- Refuse supersession theatre that names a successor without a predecessor identity.
- Escalate conflicts where successor meaning would collapse integrity or authority maps.

### Prohibited governance actions

- Erase predecessor artefacts so only the successor appears to have ever been law.
- Treat “main now says X” as automatic supersession without published predecessor/successor identities.
- Privilege a preferred implementation as the successor constitutional standard.
- Use supersession to bypass authorship rules of the owning Programme.
- Replace WS1–WS4 catalogues by superseding “whatever is inconvenient.”
- Narrate supersession as educational quality improvement or exam-readiness proof.

## 7. CEG-05 — Constitutional Consolidation

### Constitutional purpose

Govern the lawful **unification of related published constitutional artefacts** — so redundant or fragmented law may consolidate without silent meaning loss or authority collapse.

### Constitutional scope

Governance of consolidation maps that unify multiple identifiable published artefacts into a clearer published structure while preserving historical source identities. Does **not** authorise discarding unresolved conflicts by majority vibe. Does **not** merge Programme VI educational meaning into Programme VIII runtime law under a consolidation banner.

### Constitutional inputs

- Named source artefacts proposed for consolidation.
- Consolidation map (sources → unified artefact / structure).
- Conflict inventory and integrity constraints.
- Published CEGO-01 / CEGO-02 / CEGO-03 / CEGO-06 expectations.
- Continuity requirements for each source’s historical meaning.

### Constitutional outputs

- Consolidation governance disposition: **permitted** / **refused** / **deferred** / **escalated**.
- Source→unified provenance records for each material source.
- Conflict resolution honesty note (resolved under published law vs deferred / escalated).
- Boundary preservation note (no cross-Programme authority absorption).

### Permitted governance actions

- Unify related artefacts when the consolidation map preserves source provenance.
- Require explicit treatment of conflicts rather than silent majority rewrite.
- Compose with CEG-03 / CEG-06 for sources that become non-forward-operative after unification.
- Compose with CEG-01 / CEG-02 when consolidation includes true amendment or additive publication — both must be named.
- Refuse consolidation that would launder unresolved educational meaning conflicts.

### Prohibited governance actions

- Discard source meaning without provenance because “docs were messy.”
- Merge educational meaning, orchestration ownership, and runtime execution into one god artefact under consolidation colour.
- Use consolidation to retrospectively rewrite assessment findings.
- Privilege a directory layout or tooling preference as constitutional consolidation law.
- Directly rewrite source texts under the governance label without lawful authorship process.
- Treat consolidation as a waiver of explainability or continuity.

## 8. CEG-06 — Constitutional Archival

### Constitutional purpose

Govern the lawful **preservation of non-forward-operative constitutional artefacts as historical law** — so retired meaning remains auditable, explainable, and eligible for honest restoration.

### Constitutional scope

Governance of archival designation and preservation obligations for identifiable published artefacts that are no longer forward-operative (often after CEG-03 / CEG-04). Archival is preservation, not deletion. Does **not** hide inconvenient history. Does **not** require a particular archive vendor or storage technology.

### Constitutional inputs

- Named artefact(s) proposed for archival.
- Archival rationale and relationship to deprecation / supersession if any.
- Continuity / accessibility constraints for historical reconstruction.
- Published CEGO-01 / CEGO-03 / CEGO-04 / CEGO-05 expectations.
- Confirmation that archival will not alter historical content retrospectively.

### Constitutional outputs

- Archival governance disposition: **permitted** / **refused** / **deferred** / **escalated**.
- Archival provenance record (artefact identity, archival boundary, related successor if any).
- Accessibility / reconstructability note for auditors.
- Explicit non-alteration note (archive preserves content; it does not rewrite it).

### Permitted governance actions

- Designate non-forward-operative artefacts for historical preservation.
- Require that archived meaning remain reconstructable for continuity and audit.
- Compose with CEG-03 / CEG-04 when archival follows deprecation or supersession.
- Refuse archival theatre that is actually deletion or history rewrite.
- Preserve eligibility for later CEG-07 restoration by keeping identity intact.

### Prohibited governance actions

- Delete artefacts under an “archive” label.
- Alter archived content to match current product narrative.
- Make archives inaccessible so continuity cannot be reconstructed.
- Require a particular SIEM, object store, or wiki vendor as constitutional archival law.
- Use archival to hide not-certified / non-conformant / failed assessment history that remains educationally material.
- Narrate archival as proof that prior law was never binding.

## 9. CEG-07 — Constitutional Restoration

### Constitutional purpose

Govern the lawful **return of previously deprecated, archived, or superseded meaning to forward operation** — so restoration is deliberate, conflict-checked, and continuity-preserving rather than nostalgic improvisation.

### Constitutional scope

Governance of restoration of identifiable previously non-forward-operative artefacts (or clauses) to forward-operative status. Restoration must confront conflicts with current forward law. Does **not** silently dual-operate contradictory law. Does **not** restore by product nostalgia or stack preference.

### Constitutional inputs

- Named artefact(s) proposed for restoration (with prior deprecation / archival / supersession identity).
- Restoration rationale bound to constitutional integrity / continuity / authority care.
- Conflict inventory against current forward-operative corpora.
- Published CEGO-01 / CEGO-02 / CEGO-03 / CEGO-06 expectations.
- Provenance of the artefact’s non-forward-operative period.

### Constitutional outputs

- Restoration governance disposition: **permitted** / **refused** / **deferred** / **escalated**.
- Restoration provenance record (restored identity, conflict outcomes, new forward boundary).
- Continuity note for the non-forward-operative interval.
- Boundary preservation note (no dual contradictory forward law without published conflict disposition).

### Permitted governance actions

- Restore named artefacts when conflicts are resolved or lawfully deferred / escalated.
- Require honesty about what changed in neighbouring law during the non-forward-operative interval.
- Compose with CEG-01 / CEG-04 when restoration requires amendment or re-supersession maps.
- Refuse restoration that would create silent contradictory forward law.
- Preserve the historical record of the non-forward-operative period.

### Prohibited governance actions

- Restore by vibe, nostalgia, or “the old file was nicer.”
- Privilege a preferred stack or team as restoration authority.
- Erase the deprecation / archival / supersession interval to make restoration look uninterrupted.
- Use restoration to bypass current Programme authorship rules.
- Restore contradictory law into silent dual operation.
- Narrate restoration as educational quality judgement or WS1–WS4 waiver.

## 10. Catalogue Discipline

| Rule | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Closed set** | Only CEG-01…CEG-07 are constitutional evolution types |
| **Multi-bind allowed** | A single evolution may bind multiple CEG types when published law requires |
| **No silent skip** | Required types for a scope may not be omitted for convenience |
| **Subjects ≠ types** | CC / CT / CV / CCM / CRT / RC / EC / EV / Programme VI–VII IDs may appear as *inputs or subjects*; they are not CEG types |
| **Dispositions ≠ enactments** | Permitted results authorise lawful change processes; they do not silently rewrite corpus text |
| **Dispositions ≠ assessment waiver** | Permitted results never skip or replace CC / CV / CCM / CRT evaluation |
| **History preserved** | Deprecation, supersession, archival, and restoration leave reconstructable provenance |
| **Technology-neutral** | No CEG type privileges a language, framework, protocol, or CI vendor |
