## 1. Purpose

Kwalitec coaches students preparing for demanding professional examinations (especially IFoA syllabi such as CM1/CS1 and peers).

An expert IFoA tutor does not mark an examination by inventing new syllabus rules mid-script, replace interpretation principles because the answer key was inconvenient, declare a student “compliant” because the marking pen moved, or treat a completed mark sheet as a new constitution that freezes tomorrow’s law. After the Constitution and EIP have defined *educational law*, after Programmes VI–IX have published *meaning, orchestration, runtime, and governance law*, and after Workstream 1 has *prepared, resolved, and completed* the constitutional execution context, the platform still needs one execution answer:

> **“How is a prepared Constitutional Execution Context lawfully executed?”**

That answer must ensure the engine consumes **completed execution contexts**; executes **constitutional rules** as published; evaluates **constitutional constraints**; preserves **constitutional authority** and **implementation neutrality**; and produces **execution outcomes** for downstream decision architecture — while remaining fully consistent, deterministic, repeatable, explainable, and constitutionally faithful — without amending constitutional specifications, replacing EIP, determining compliance, certifying implementations, interpreting constitutional law independently, determining governance outcomes, or becoming constitutional authority.

This document records that posture so every future constitutional execution activity (and successor) has a single constitutional reference for *how prepared context may be executed without becoming law, a compliance engine, or a certification seal*.

> **The Constitutional Execution Engine Model describes how constitutional rules are lawfully executed against a completed execution context.  
> It does not redefine constitutional law, create constitutional authority, determine governance independently, amend specifications, replace EIP, determine compliance, or certify implementations.**

## 2. What the Constitutional Execution Engine Is

The **Constitutional Execution Engine** is the binding **constitutional mechanism** by which **published constitutional rules** may be **executed against a completed Constitutional Execution Context** — consuming that context, dispatching and executing rules, evaluating constraints, collecting outcomes, managing execution state, coordinating exceptions, and publishing execution outcomes — while preserving constitutional authority, implementation neutrality, determinism, repeatability, explainability, and constitutional fidelity — without becoming a source of constitutional law, a substitute for EIP, a compliance determination engine, a certification authority, or an independent governance producer.

| Concept | Definition | Primary question |
|---------|------------|------------------|
| **Completed execution context** | A resolved execution context whose WS1 / MS003 preparation has lawfully completed | What prepared context may the engine consume? |
| **Constitutional rule** | A published obligation / procedure / constraint from Constitution, EIP, or Programme VI–IX specifications identified in the completed context | Which published rules may be executed? |
| **Rule execution** | Application of a published rule against the completed context exactly as published | Was the rule applied as published? |
| **Constraint evaluation** | Assessment of hard constitutional limits preserved in the completed context | Which constraints were evaluated? |
| **Execution outcome** | Reconstructable result of lawful rule execution and constraint evaluation for downstream decision architecture | What did execution produce? |
| **Execution state** | Speakable progress of constitutional execution under CEE-05 (not educational state, not governance disposition) | Where is execution in its lawful course? |
| **Exception coordination** | Lawful handling of execution stops, refusals, and boundary violations without inventing new law | How were exceptions handled? |
| **Constitutional authority** | The exclusive right of published corpora to define educational and execution meaning | Who owns the standard? |
| **Implementation neutrality** | Engine law does not privilege a stack, vendor, or topology | Remains the stack replaceable? |
| **Determinism** | Same completed context + same published rules ⇒ same execution disposition | Is the result fixed by inputs? |
| **Repeatability** | Execution is reproducible from reconstructable inputs | Can the run be reproduced? |
| **Explainability** | Rules, constraints, outcomes, state transitions, and boundaries are speakable | Can the run be narrated honestly? |
| **Constitutional fidelity** | Execution applies rules exactly as published; it never soft-amends them | Did authority survive execution? |

The Constitutional Execution Engine is:

- **context-consuming** — it consumes completed execution contexts; it never invents, rewrites, or completes them itself;
- **rule-centred** — it executes published constitutional rules; it never authors them;
- **authority-preserving** — published corpora remain the sole source of constitutional meaning;
- **EIP-faithful** — Educational Interpretation Principles are applied as published references; they are never replaced;
- **catalogue-closed** — only CEE-01…CEE-07 may be applied as constitutional execution engine components;
- **constraint-bound** — hard constitutional boundaries remain intact and evaluable;
- **outcome-honest** — outcomes are execution outcomes for downstream decision architecture, not compliance seals or certifications;
- **state-honest** — execution state describes engine progress; it never invents educational state or governance dispositions;
- **exception-honest** — exceptions stop or escalate under published boundaries; they never mint amendment power;
- **implementation-neutral** — no particular language, framework, store, or CI tool is required or privileged by engine law;
- **deterministic** — identical completed contexts and published rules yield the same execution disposition;
- **repeatable** — execution is reproducible from reconstructable inputs;
- **explainable** — students, developers, and auditors can answer which rules, constraints, outcomes, transitions, and boundaries applied;
- **non-legislative** — execution never amends constitutional specifications;
- **non-governance** — execution never determines conformance, verification, compliance, certification, or evolution dispositions;
- **non-substitutive** — engine machinery never becomes the constitutional standard for the next judgement.

The Constitutional Execution Engine is **not**:

- a second Educational Constitution, EIP, or Programme VI / VII / VIII / IX amendment path;
- a licence to reinterpret coach meaning, ownership maps, tip warrants, runtime contracts, or Programme IX judgements in “execution” prose;
- a Runtime A workflow runner, scheduler, Python interpreter, Flask request cycle, or CI job;
- a substitute for WS1 context / resolution / completion — those corpora prepare context; CEE executes against completed context;
- a substitute for Programme VIII runtime execution — RC / CE / REC govern *software execution of published law*; CEE governs *constitutional rule execution against a completed constitutional execution context*;
- a substitute for Programme IX conformance, verification, compliance, certification, or evolution — those corpora produce judgements; CEE produces *execution outcomes* and never re-produces those judgements;
- a claim that “execution complete” guarantees learning, mastery, exam readiness, product success, conformity, verification, compliance, certification, or frozen stack authority;
- a testing framework, CI/CD pipeline, GitHub Action, linter policy, or OpenAPI checker.

## 3. Engine Posture (Constitutional, Not Technical)

Every material constitutional execution travels through constitutional phases. These are **normative execution obligations**, not an implementation architecture.

```
Confirm completed resolved execution context (WS1 / MS003)
        │
        ├── incomplete / refused (lawful stop — execution must not begin)
        │
        └── completed → Dispatch published constitutional rules (CEE-01)
                │
                ▼
        Execute rules exactly as published (CEE-02)
                │
                ├── evaluate constraints (CEE-03)
                ├── collect outcomes (CEE-04)
                ├── manage execution state (CEE-05)
                └── coordinate exceptions (CEE-06)
                │
                ▼
        Publish execution outcomes (CEE-07)
                │
                └── never amend law, replace EIP, determine compliance,
                    certify implementations, or replace constitutional authority
```

**Refuse is lawful.** Prefer honest stop over inventing unpublished rules, soft-amending specifications, or minting governance dispositions so a demo can “finish.”

## 4. Integrity Rules

1. **Completed context only.** The engine may begin only against a WS1 / MS003-completed resolved execution context.
2. **Published rules only.** Unpublished customs and “how main works” are hard stops as rule sources.
3. **Published components only.** Unpublished CEE customs are unlawful.
4. **Exactly as published.** Successful execution never rewrites specification meaning.
5. **Authority first.** Published corpora remain the sole constitutional standard; the engine never becomes one.
6. **EIP intact.** Educational Interpretation Principles are never replaced by engine prose.
7. **Implementation independence.** No stack, vendor, or reference implementation becomes law by proximity to execution.
8. **Outcomes ≠ governance.** Execution outcomes never grade learning, determine compliance, or certify implementations.
9. **No independent interpretation.** Applying published rules is not inventing new constitutional meaning.
10. **No governance authorship.** Collecting outcomes is not minting CC / CV / CCM / CRT / CEG dispositions.
11. **No authority substitution.** A published outcome never replaces the corpus for the next act.
12. **No bypass.** Product urgency never waives required engine components or completed-context prerequisites.
13. **Determinism and repeatability.** Race-dependent or operator-dependent execution is a defect.
14. **Explain the stop.** Students, developers, and auditors should hear when execution must refuse or escalate.
15. **No emergency exemption.** Deadlines and demos never mint constitutional amendment power via the engine.
16. **Constraints survive.** Hard limits may be evaluated; they may not be waived by engine prose.
17. **Downstream honesty.** Downstream decision architecture consumes outcomes; it does not inherit legislative power from the engine.
