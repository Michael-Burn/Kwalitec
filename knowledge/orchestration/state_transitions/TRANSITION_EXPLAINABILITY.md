## 1. Purpose

Students should never have to guess why Kwalitec shifted from today’s priority to recovery, returned from a sitting to ordinary day focus, began waiting while valid options were settled, or simply held continuity — or mistake that shift for “you have mastered this,” “you are done,” or “a different coach now owns everything.”

Developers should never have to reverse-engineer which CST type fired, what contextual evidence warranted it, which published constitutional rules applied, or whether continuity of prior context was preserved.

State-transition explainability exists so every material contextual succession answers — in the right language for the audience — **why the contextual transition occurred**, **what constitutional evidence supported it**, **which published constitutional rules were applied**, and **how contextual continuity was preserved**.

Without transition explainability:

- focus changes feel arbitrary;
- succession is confused with mastery or completion;
- audits cannot prove CST catalogue discipline;
- continuity appears as amnesia or product mode flicker.

With transition explainability:

- the student trusts why educational focus moved;
- developers can verify WS4 updated representation without inventing meaning or executing workflows;
- claim types stay honest;
- refuse / remain (CST-13) stays dignified and clear.

## 3. Two Audiences

| Audience | Language | Must include | Must exclude |
|----------|----------|--------------|--------------|
| **Student** | Plain educational speech | Why focus changed (or why it did not); honest limits; that this is focus, not mastery / success / “finished” | CST/EST/STB IDs, Twin facets, optimiser jargon, internal document paths, ownership-transfer rhetoric |
| **Developer / auditor** | Precise constitutional references | CST-xx, from/to EST-xx, condition families satisfied, published rules cited, continuity record, STB-xx pass | Student motivational fluff as a substitute for audit fields |

Student copy narrates educational reasons for focus change. Developer traces cite transition IDs, warrants, and rule checks.

## 4. Traceability Obligation (Architectural)

Every material contextual state transition must be traceable through:

| Trace link | Student-facing role | Developer-facing role |
|------------|---------------------|------------------------|
| **Why the transition occurred** | “We’re shifting focus because…” / “We’re holding steady because…” | CST-xx; initiating warrant / event / disposition / plan-class change |
| **Constitutional evidence that supported it** | “Given your Study Plan / recent disruption / sitting that just ended / …” | C-CONTEXT warrants; optional C-FLOW references (as facts, not executions) |
| **Published constitutional rules applied** | Optional plain: “under your recovery / daily / plan rules…” | MS001 entry/exit; CST catalogue; C-RULE citations (WS1/WS2/WS4 corpora) |
| **Contextual continuity preserved** | “We’re not forgetting where you were — earlier focus was…” | Prior EST recorded; EIP-005 pass; no history erasure |
| **Non-claim honesty** | Implicit: no mastery/success/completion/ownership change from the move alone | Explicit STB-02…STB-07 / prohibited-interpretation checks |

A succession with no why → evidence → rules → continuity chain is invalid — even if the UI animation looks smooth.

## 5. Four Transition Questions (Binding)

Every material educational state transition must answer these four questions.

### STQ-01 — Why did the contextual transition occur?

**Student examples:**

- “We’re shifting from today’s priority to recovery because there’s been a meaningful break in your study rhythm.”
- “Your sitting has ended, so the focus returns to today’s study priorities.”
- “Several valid next steps competed, so we’re pausing ordinary focus while we settle which one to act on first.”
- “Nothing new is interrupting your Study Plan, so we’re holding continuity rather than inventing a special focus.”
- “We’re not changing focus yet — the conditions for a new focus aren’t met.”

**Developer requirements:**

- Record CST-xx (including CST-13 refuse/remain when applicable).
- Record initiating warrant class: plan class change, Programme VI warrant, WS1 progression/completion fact, WS2 disposition, continuity hold, or condition failure.
- Record that the transition was representational only (STB-01…STB-06).

---

### STQ-02 — What constitutional evidence supported it?

**Student examples:**

- “Your active Study Plan and today’s coaching warrant still apply — we’re only changing which question leads.”
- “The disruption signal and recovery coaching rules support this focus change.”
- “We’re not claiming you’ve mastered anything — this is about focus, not mastery.”
- “Coordination noted that concurrent guidance needed settling before ordinary focus resumes.”

**Developer requirements:**

- List C-CONTEXT facts (origin EST exit, destination EST entry, plan class, Programme VI warrant IDs).
- List C-FLOW facts only as *referenced* published WS1 progression / completion records — never as State Engine execution.
- List C-AUTH facts (primary owner unchanged; no tip invented).
- Distinguish context warrants from EIP-002 Educational Evidence of understanding.
- Record STB-02 / STB-03 / STB-07 non-claim checks.

---

### STQ-03 — Which published constitutional rules were applied?

**Student examples:**

- “Under your recovery rules, restorative focus leads before ordinary daily tips.”
- “Under your Study Plan rules, day coaching waits until a plan is active.”
- “Under continuity care, we hold steady when no special question is live.”

**Developer requirements:**

- Cite CST type from `TRANSITION_TYPES.md`.
- Cite MS001 `STATE_TYPES.md` entry/exit rows for origin and destination.
- Cite permitting condition IDs / families from `TRANSITION_CONDITIONS.md` (G1–G10 and type-specific rows).
- Cite any WS1 transition/completion rule or WS2 disposition rule referenced as C-RULE / C-FLOW / C-AUTH.
- Record STB-10 catalogue closure (no undocumented types).

---

### STQ-04 — How was contextual continuity preserved?

**Student examples:**

- “We’re returning to today’s priorities — your recovery focus isn’t erased; it’s concluded for now.”
- “Your sitting still happened; reflection and day coaching build on it rather than pretending it didn’t.”
- “We’re holding your Study Plan continuity until the next real educational question appears.”

**Developer requirements:**

- Record prior primary EST-xx and destination EST-yy (or same for CST-13).
- Record that learner-owned educational history was not deleted (EIP-005 / C-CONT).
- Record that Article IV states were not mutated by succession (STB-08).
- When material, name parallel-read siblings that remained observational only.

## 6. Audience Patterns

### 6.1 Student pattern (focus change, not score)

> “We’re changing focus from **[plain prior]** to **[plain destination]**.  
> That’s because **[why]**.  
> This is about **where we are now**, not a judgement that you’ve mastered the topic or finished everything — and we’re not forgetting where you were.”

### 6.2 Developer pattern (audit)

```text
cst: CST-xx
from_est: EST-xx
to_est: EST-yy | same
why: <warrant / event / disposition / plan-class / refuse>
context_evidence: [C-CONTEXT ...]
flow_references: [C-FLOW published facts | none]  # referenced, not executed
authority_facts: [C-AUTH ...]
rules_applied: [MS001 entry/exit | CST catalogue | condition IDs | WS1/WS2 cites]
continuity: {prior_est_recorded: true, eip005_pass: true, article_iv_mutated: false}
boundary_pass: [STB-01…STB-10]
prohibited_interpretation_pass: true
```

## 7. Special Cases

| Situation | Explainability rule |
|-----------|---------------------|
| **CST-04 Absent-plan enter** | Say plainly that day coaching waits on a Study Plan; do not invent tips; do not narrate failure |
| **CST-07 Recovery → Day** | Name return to ordinary daily focus; never “you succeeded at recovery” from CST alone |
| **CST-08 / CST-09 Conflict await** | Explain waiting / settling among valid options; do not narrate State Engine as tip author |
| **CST-10 / CST-11 Escalation await** | Explain waiting for plan / ownership resolution; prefer no-recommendation |
| **CST-12 Continuity hold** | Explain holding as lawful care, not product emptiness or student failure |
| **CST-13 Refuse / remain** | Explain why focus did *not* change; treat refusal as first-class honesty |
| **C-FLOW citation present** | Student: coordination fact in plain speech; Developer: WT/completion IDs as *references*; never claim State Engine executed the workflow |
| **Article IV mentioned in speech** | Keep “Study Progress / evidence / estimates” clearly distinct from EST focus change |

## 8. Relationship to Other Explainability Corpora

| Corpus | Explains | State-transition explainability adds |
|--------|----------|--------------------------------------|
| MS001 State explainability | What current context is | Why context *moved* and how continuity held |
| Programme VI coach / planner explainability | Why the educational answer means what it means | Frames which focus change made that question live |
| WS1 workflow / transition explainability | Why flow started / moved / concluded | May be cited as C-FLOW evidence — not replaced |
| WS2 authority / conflict explainability | Why owner permitted / how concurrency dispositioned | May cite CST-08/09 and owner-aligned EST changes as situation |
| WS3 recommendation / assembly / set explainability | Why tip / set exists | May cite post-transition EST as context reference — never as tip source |

State-transition explainability **does not replace** Programme VI educational explainability or WS1 workflow-transition explainability. It **narrates contextual succession**.

---

## 9. Non-Responsibility of Explanation

Explanation must **not**:

- invent CST or EST types absent from the catalogues;
- claim success, mastery, or workflow completion from succession (STB-02 / STB-03 / STB-07);
- create recommendations in speech that lack Programme VI / WS3 warrant (STB-05);
- transfer ownership in narration (STB-04);
- claim the State Engine executed a workflow (STB-06);
- redefine Educational Evidence (STB-09);
- treat Version 2 operational state machines as this Framework’s constitutional vocabulary.
