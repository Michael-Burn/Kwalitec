## 1. Purpose

Resolution without a closed stage catalogue invents law by proximity: whichever filter, ticket habit, or ambient product state happens to shrink the desk becomes the tutor’s “proof the system knew which rules applied.”

This catalogue names the only lawful constitutional execution context resolution stages — and binds each stage to purpose, inputs, outputs, and permitted / prohibited activities.

---

## 2. Stage Principles

1. **Ordered and skip-disciplined.** Stages may be brief under thin scope; mandatory initiation, scope determination, selection, dependency assembly, consistency validation, and publication obligations may not be silently omitted when a resolved execution context is claimed.
2. **Context before selection.** A consumable MS001 Execution Context is required before material artefact selection claims completeness.
3. **Explainability accumulates.** Each stage leaves traceable artefacts for `RESOLUTION_EXPLAINABILITY.md`.
4. **Refusal is a stage outcome.** Incomplete, inconsistent, and refuse-publish are first-class conclusions.
5. **Deterministic transitions.** Same published inputs ⇒ same next stage outcomes.
6. **Stages select and assemble; they do not legislate.** No stage creates, modifies, or redefines constitutional law.
7. **Stages do not execute.** Selecting artefacts is never running constitutional rules.
8. **Stages do not interpret independently.** Naming EIP principles is never producing interpretation outcomes.
9. **Stages do not decide.** Selecting governance artefacts is never minting constitutional decisions, compliance determinations, or certifications.

## 3. Catalogue Overview

| ID | Stage | Constitutional purpose (short) | Primary inputs | Primary outputs |
|----|-------|--------------------------------|----------------|-----------------|
| **CECR-01** | Resolution Initiation | Open resolution against a named execution identity and consumable Execution Context | Consumable MS001 context + execution identity | Initiated resolution record (or refuse-open) |
| **CECR-02** | Scope Determination | Bound what this resolution may lawfully address from the available context | Initiated record + CECX-06 / published scope rules | Explicit resolution scope (+ assumptions where explicit) |
| **CECR-03** | Artefact Selection | Select relevant constitutional artefacts; record exclusions | Resolution scope + available CECX artefact sets | Selected set + exclusion set (or refuse-select) |
| **CECR-04** | Dependency Assembly | Assemble published dependency relationships among selected artefacts | Selected set + published dependency relationships | Assembled dependency bundle (or refuse-assemble) |
| **CECR-05** | Consistency Validation | Validate that the candidate resolved view is internally consistent under published law | Selected set + dependencies + constraints | Consistency outcome (consistent / inconsistent / refuse) |
| **CECR-06** | Resolved Context Publication | Publish the resolved execution context (or refuse-publish) under honesty rules | Consistent candidate + boundary confirmations | Published resolved execution context (or refuse-publish) |

Material claims that a constitutionally valid execution view exists must map to this sequence as published law requires. Stages may be brief; none may invent a stage outside this catalogue.

**Relation to CECX / Programme VIII / IX catalogues:** Those catalogues remain defined solely by their owners. CECR stages *organise when* selection and assembly occur; they do not replace, reclassify, re-execute, re-judge, invent, or amend those catalogues.

## 4. Canonical Stage Sequence

```text
CECR-01  Resolution Initiation
           ↓
CECR-02  Scope Determination
           ↓
CECR-03  Artefact Selection
           ↓
CECR-04  Dependency Assembly
           ↓
CECR-05  Consistency Validation
           ↓
CECR-06  Resolved Context Publication
```

Lawful early stop dispositions (refuse-open / refuse-select / refuse-assemble / inconsistent / refuse-publish) may exhaust remaining *resolution* duties while still requiring honest recording of what occurred — never silent disappearance of the concern, and never elevation of an incomplete view into a published complete resolved context.

## 5. Stage Definitions

### CECR-01 — Resolution Initiation

**Constitutional purpose**  
Open a material resolution concern by naming the consumable Execution Context and the execution identity for which a resolved view is sought — without inventing law, opening against an absent context, or treating initiation as execution.

**Constitutional inputs**

- Identifiable consumable MS001 Execution Context (CECX-01…CECX-07 assembled or honestly incomplete as published)
- Named execution identity (CECX-05 or equivalent reconstructable identity under published naming discipline)
- Optional published indication of intended resolution scope candidates
- Published CECRO-01 / CECRO-05 expectations

**Constitutional outputs**

- An initiated resolution record naming the consumed Execution Context identity, execution identity, and initiation disposition — **or**
- Lawful refuse-open when the Execution Context is absent / unpublished / theatrical, identity cannot be established, or initiation would invent law

**Permitted activities**

- Cite the consumable Execution Context as the sole information horizon for selection
- Bind initiation to a stable execution identity
- Record initiation audit identity for later stages
- Refuse initiation when constitutional preconditions fail

**Prohibited activities**

- Treat “how main works,” tribal knowledge, or stack preference as the Execution Context
- Create or modify constitutional specifications by opening resolution
- Execute constitutional logic at initiation
- Produce constitutional decisions at initiation
- Skip initiation when a material resolved context will later be claimed
- Invent missing CECX components so initiation can succeed

**Authority preserved**  
Published corpora and MS001 remain the sole standards. Initiation organises; it does not legislate or execute.

---

### CECR-02 — Scope Determination

**Constitutional purpose**  
Determine **what this resolution may lawfully address** from the available Execution Context — and what it must not — including explicit non-legislative assumptions where required — so artefact selection does not inherit an unbounded or silently assumed world.

**Constitutional inputs**

- Initiated resolution record (CECR-01)
- Execution scope and assumptions from CECX-06 (and related published scope / boundary statements)
- Execution constraints from CECX-07 (for bound-checking)
- Published CECRO-01 / CECRO-05 expectations

**Constitutional outputs**

- Explicit resolution-scope bounds (in-scope / out-of-scope for *this* resolved view)
- Explicit assumption list where assumptions are non-legislative premises of the resolved view
- Honest incomplete / refused disposition when scope cannot be bounded under published law

**Permitted activities**

- Bound resolution scope under published law and the consumed Execution Context
- Carry explicit non-legislative assumptions from CECX-06 into the resolution scope
- Distinguish resolution scope from educational / orchestration context meaning
- Refuse scope determination when published law cannot bound the concern

**Prohibited activities**

- Unbounded “select whatever seems useful” scope
- Assumptions that soft-amend constitutional specifications or EIP claim classes
- Scope that absorbs Programme IX judgement production or Programme VIII execution into “resolution”
- Hidden assumptions not listed in the resolution record
- Treating scope determination as execution or decision

**Authority preserved**  
Scope bounds the resolved view; it never amends published law or replaces MS001 scope meaning.

---

### CECR-03 — Artefact Selection

**Constitutional purpose**  
Select the **relevant constitutional artefacts** from the available Execution Context for the determined scope — and record **exclusions** reconstructably — so the candidate resolved view contains only lawful, published information required for the specific execution.

**Constitutional inputs**

- Resolution scope (CECR-02)
- Available artefact sets from CECX-01…CECX-04 (authority, interpretation principles, specifications, governance artefacts) and related published identities in the consumed Execution Context
- Published CECRO-01 / CECRO-04 / CECRO-05 expectations

**Constitutional outputs**

- Named selected artefact set (paths / identities)
- Named exclusion set with reconstructable exclusion reasons bound to scope
- Honest refuse-select when required artefacts are missing and cannot be invented, or when selection would invent unpublished customs

**Permitted activities**

- Select published constitutional artefacts within resolution scope
- Exclude available artefacts that are out of scope, with speakable reasons
- Prefer refuse-select over inventing missing artefacts
- Preserve selection / exclusion audit trails for explainability

**Prohibited activities**

- Invent unpublished artefacts to fill gaps
- Silent exclusion of available artefacts without reconstructable reason
- Soft-rewrite selected artefact meaning as “selection clarification”
- Select artefacts as a disguised constitutional decision, compliance determination, or certification
- Execute constitutional rules under the label “selection”
- Independently interpret selected EIP principles into new outcomes

**Authority preserved**  
Selection chooses published identities; it never authors meaning.

---

### CECR-04 — Dependency Assembly

**Constitutional purpose**  
Assemble **published dependency relationships** among selected artefacts into the candidate resolved view — so constitutional couplings required for the specific execution are present as information — without inventing new legal dependencies or executing dependent rules.

**Constitutional inputs**

- Selected artefact set (CECR-03)
- Published dependency / subordination / stack-position / lineage relationships already present in Constitution / EIP / Programme VI–IX / MS001 corpora (and established CT relationships where available as information)
- Published CECRO-02 / CECRO-05 expectations

**Constitutional outputs**

- Assembled dependency bundle binding selected artefacts under published relationships
- Honest refuse-assemble when required published dependencies cannot be identified without invention
- Explicit non-claim: assembling a dependency is not executing either end of the dependency

**Permitted activities**

- Cite published subordination, stack-order, and lineage relationships among selected artefacts
- Carry established traceability relationships as information into the resolved view
- Record missing published dependencies as gaps (refuse-assemble or incomplete)
- Preserve dependency assembly trails for explainability

**Prohibited activities**

- Invent new constitutional dependencies not published upstream
- Mutate artefact relationships mid-assembly to manufacture completeness
- Treat dependency assembly as execution of dependent constitutional logic
- Treat dependency assembly as independent interpretation or decision
- Collapse neighbour catalogues (for example CT / CC / CV) into CECR stage identities

**Authority preserved**  
Dependencies remain as published; assembly never legislates new couplings.

---

### CECR-05 — Consistency Validation

**Constitutional purpose**  
Validate that the candidate resolved view is **internally consistent under published law** — selected artefacts, exclusions, dependencies, scope, assumptions, and constraints do not contradict one another — so execution does not inherit an incoherent constitutional picture.

**Constitutional inputs**

- Selected artefact set + exclusion set (CECR-03)
- Assembled dependency bundle (CECR-04)
- Resolution scope and assumptions (CECR-02)
- Execution constraints from the consumed Execution Context (CECX-07) and published boundary corpora
- Published CECRO-01 / CECRO-02 / CECRO-05 expectations

**Constitutional outputs**

- Consistency outcome: consistent / inconsistent / refuse-validate
- Named consistency checks performed (reconstructable)
- Honest refuse when consistency would require soft-amending law or inventing artefacts

**Permitted activities**

- Check alignment among authority references, specifications, interpretation principles, governance artefact identities, scope, assumptions, constraints, and dependencies
- Record material inconsistencies as defects requiring refuse / incomplete disposition
- Confirm that exclusions do not silently cancel required constraints
- Preserve consistency-check trails for explainability

**Prohibited activities**

- Silent “best-effort” merge of contradictions into a false consistent view
- Soft-amend specifications or EIP principles to manufacture consistency
- Treat consistency validation as verification, compliance determination, or certification
- Execute constitutional logic under the label “consistency check”
- Waive constraints because they inconvenience the resolved view

**Authority preserved**  
Consistency validation protects fidelity; it never rewrites law to pass itself.

---

### CECR-06 — Resolved Context Publication

**Constitutional purpose**  
Publish the **resolved execution context** — the constitutionally valid execution view — under published honesty rules, or refuse publication when integrity fails — so successor constitutional execution activities may consume a speakable, bounded information view without mistaking publication for execution, interpretation, or decision.

**Constitutional inputs**

- Consistency outcome and candidate resolved view (CECR-05)
- Boundary confirmations (no execution / independent interpretation / decision / amendment / substitution occurred)
- Published CECRO-03 / CECRO-04 / CECRO-05 expectations
- Explainability obligations from [`RESOLUTION_EXPLAINABILITY.md`](RESOLUTION_EXPLAINABILITY.md)

**Constitutional outputs**

- Published resolved execution context naming selected artefacts, exclusions, dependencies, consistency outcomes, identity, scope, assumptions, constraints, and intact boundaries — **or**
- Lawful refuse-publish when the candidate is inconsistent, incomplete, unexplained, or would present resolution as execution / decision / law

**Permitted activities**

- Publish the resolved execution context as information for successor execution activities
- Refuse publication when integrity or explainability fails
- Preserve publication artefacts for audit and continuity (EIP-005)
- State explicitly that publication is not execution, independent interpretation, or decision

**Prohibited activities**

- Publish an incomplete or inconsistent view as if complete
- Present publication as constitutional execution success
- Present publication as compliance determination, verification, or certification
- Amend corpora under publication pretext
- Freeze a stack, vendor, or reference implementation as law via the published view
- Skip publication honesty rules for demo urgency

**Authority preserved**  
Publication makes the resolved view available; it never becomes constitutional authority for the next act.

## 6. Composition Rules

1. **Closed catalogue.** Only CECR-01…CECR-06 may organise constitutional execution context resolution.
2. **Context before completeness.** Material resolved contexts without a consumable MS001 Execution Context are defective.
3. **Identity before publication.** Execution identity must be establishable before a resolved view may be published as complete.
4. **Selection requires exclusions.** Material selections must record exclusions (or explicitly record that no available in-horizon artefacts were excluded) — silence is a defect.
5. **Dependencies are published.** CECR-04 assembles published relationships; it never invents them.
6. **Consistency before publication.** CECR-06 may not publish a complete resolved view after CECR-05 inconsistent / refuse outcomes.
7. **No execution inside stages.** No CECR stage may execute constitutional logic or emit constitutional decisions.
8. **Cross-cutting binding.** Multiple stages bind together in sequence; none may invent an off-catalogue stage.
