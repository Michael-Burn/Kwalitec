## 1. Purpose

An expert IFoA tutor who cannot say *which papers were chosen for this sitting* — which artefacts were selected, which were left aside, which couplings were assembled, which consistency checks were run, and which limits remained intact — is not coaching; they are performing.

After Programmes VI–IX have made meaning, ownership, tips, context, contracts, evidence, runtime, conformance, verification, compliance, certification, and evolution speech speakable, and after MS001 has made pre-execution information-environment speech speakable, Programme X still needs one resolution-speech answer:

> **“Which constitutional artefacts were selected, which artefacts were excluded, which dependencies were assembled, which consistency checks were performed, and which constitutional boundaries remained intact?”**

That answer must cover every material resolution without inventing educational meaning, transferring authority, minting tips, inventing state, rewriting specifications, freezing a stack as law, inventing unpublished CECR stages, executing constitutional logic, interpreting law independently, or presenting resolution completeness as educational quality or governance outcome.

## 2. What Resolution Explainability Is

**Constitutional Execution Context Resolution Explainability** is the constitutional obligation to narrate **selection and assembly of named constitutional information from a consumable Execution Context through published CECR stages** — not to invent a second tutor voice from ambient product state, and not to grade the student or mint constitutional decisions.

| Concept | Definition | Primary question |
|---------|------------|------------------|
| **Artefacts selected** | Named published constitutional artefacts included in the resolved view under CECR-03 | Which constitutional artefacts were selected? |
| **Artefacts excluded** | Named available artefacts left out of the resolved view with reconstructable reasons | Which artefacts were excluded? |
| **Dependencies assembled** | Named published dependency relationships carried under CECR-04 | Which dependencies were assembled? |
| **Consistency checks performed** | Named CECR-05 checks and their outcomes | Which consistency checks were performed? |
| **Boundaries remaining intact** | Explicit confirmation that forbidden execution / independent interpretation / decision / amendment / substitution did not occur | Which constitutional boundaries remained intact? |

Resolution Explainability is:

- **selection-centred** — it narrates what was chosen from what was available, not what execution decided;
- **exclusion-honest** — every material resolution names exclusions (or explicitly records that no available in-horizon artefacts were excluded);
- **catalogue-bound** — every justification cites published CECR identities;
- **corpus-bound** — every justification cites published specifications and artefacts;
- **context-honest** — every material justification cites the consumable MS001 Execution Context (or names the hard stop when none exists);
- **non-executive** — explanation never pretends that selecting artefacts equals running rules;
- **non-interpretive** — explanation never smuggles independent interpretation outcomes into “selection notes”;
- **non-decisional** — explanation never presents resolution completeness as conformance, verification, compliance, or certification;
- **non-legislative** — explanation never becomes a back door to amend corpora;
- **quality-honest** — resolution completeness is never narrated as mastery, readiness, or educational quality;
- **technology-honest** — explanation never presents a stack preference as constitutional law;
- **audience-aware** — student speech and developer/auditor traces differ in vocabulary, not in truth;
- **layer-honest** — it does not replace MS001 context explainability, Programme VI tip warrant speech, Programme VII ownership / workflow / state speech, Programme VIII contract / evidence speech, or Programme IX judgement speech;
- **repeatability-honest** — explanations must not paper over race-dependent or anonymous ambient resolution.

Resolution Explainability is **not**:

- a rewrite of Constitution / EIP / Programme VI–IX / MS001 meaning;
- a quality report on learning, coaching, or exam readiness;
- a substitute for MS001 CECXEQ speech, Programme VII educational-context explainability, Programme VIII runtime explainability, or Programme IX judgement explainability corpora;
- a rendering system, UI template, CI badge, log shipper, or analytics dashboard;
- a claim that clear resolution speech guarantees learning, a pass, or lawful execution success;
- a licence to present filled forms, environment variables, or pipeline greens as constitutional proof of a resolved view.

## 3. Mandatory Explanation Questions

Every material constitutional execution context resolution should answer all five questions:

### CECREQ-01 — Which constitutional artefacts were selected?

Name the primary published Constitution / EIP / Programme VI / VII / VIII / IX specifications, authority references, interpretation principles, and governance artefact identities selected under CECR-03 from the consumable Execution Context (paths and identities). When a clause or catalogue family is material, name it (for example EIP-002; RC-02 as *selected subject*; CCM determination identity as *selected subject*). Unpublished tribal norms and “how main works” are not constitutional artefacts.

### CECREQ-02 — Which artefacts were excluded?

Name the primary available artefacts from the consumable Execution Context that were left out of the resolved view, with reconstructable exclusion reasons bound to resolution scope (CECR-02). If no available in-horizon artefacts were excluded, say so explicitly. Silent omission is a defect. Exclusion never means “this artefact ceases to be law.”

### CECREQ-03 — Which dependencies were assembled?

Name the primary published dependency / subordination / stack-position / lineage relationships assembled under CECR-04 among selected artefacts. Distinguish *dependency assembled as information* from *dependency executed* or *new dependency invented*. Missing required published dependencies must be speakable (refuse-assemble / incomplete).

### CECREQ-04 — Which consistency checks were performed?

Name the primary CECR-05 consistency checks applied to the candidate resolved view and their outcomes (consistent / inconsistent / refuse). Material checks typically cover alignment among authority references, specifications, interpretation principles, governance identities, scope, assumptions, constraints, and assembled dependencies. “Looks fine” without named checks is a defect.

### CECREQ-05 — Which constitutional boundaries remained intact?

Confirm explicitly that the resolution did **not**:

- execute constitutional logic;
- interpret constitutional law independently into new outcomes;
- produce constitutional decisions;
- determine compliance or perform verification;
- certify implementations;
- amend or modify constitutional specifications;
- replace constitutional authority;
- redefine MS001 CECX components;
- freeze a stack as law.

If any boundary was at risk, the explanation must narrate the refuse / incomplete / inconsistent stop — not a false completeness claim.

## 4. Audience Postures

| Audience | Vocabulary emphasis | Forbidden drift |
|----------|---------------------|-----------------|
| **Student-facing** | Plain speech about which rules and limits were chosen for *this* activity | Presenting resolution completeness as “you are ready” or “the plan is certified” |
| **Developer-facing** | Corpus paths, CECR IDs, selected / excluded artefact identities, consistency outcomes | Treating resolution as Runtime A execution success |
| **Auditor-facing** | Reconstructable identity, stage coverage, selection / exclusion / dependency / check records, boundary preservation | Accepting ambient env/CI state as resolved-context identity |

Truth is shared across audiences. Vocabulary may differ; claims may not.

---

## 5. Explanation Integrity Rules

1. **Answer all five CECREQ questions** for every material resolution.
2. **Cite published identities** — paths, corpus IDs, CECR stage IDs, CECX component IDs as consumed — not only tickets or commits.
3. **Separate selection from outcome** — selected artefacts ≠ executed rules ≠ produced decisions.
4. **Name gaps and exclusions** — missing artefacts and deliberate exclusions are part of the explanation.
5. **Preserve continuity** — do not erase prior lawful resolution history to simplify the next narration (EIP-005).
6. **Remain implementation-neutral** — do not narrate vendor preference as constitutional necessity.
7. **Refuse false completeness** — if boundaries were not intact, say so.
8. **Do not replace neighbour speech** — MS001 availability speech, tip warrants, ownership, runtime contracts, and Programme IX judgements keep their own explainability corpora.

---

## 6. Defective Explanations (Non-Exhaustive)

The following are constitutionally defective as resolution explanations:

- “Context was resolved” with no named selected artefacts, exclusions, dependencies, checks, or boundaries.
- “We selected what mattered” without artefact identities.
- “Nothing was excluded” when available in-horizon artefacts were silently omitted.
- “Dependencies were implied.”
- “Consistency was fine” with no named checks.
- “Compliance is fine” as a resolution note that re-determines CCM.
- “Certified because resolved.”
- “We used the usual stack, so fidelity is satisfied.”
- “Constraints were relaxed for the demo.”
- Student speech that converts resolution completeness into mastery or exam readiness.
- Developer speech that converts resolution completeness into Runtime A success or constitutional amendment.
- Treating MS001 “artefacts available” speech as if it answered selection / exclusion without further resolution narration.
