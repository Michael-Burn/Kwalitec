## 1. Purpose

An expert IFoA tutor does not optimise “everything selected,” “pipeline green,” or “resolved blob filled.” The tutor optimises **lawful resolution honesty**: only when ambiguity is minimised and determinism, repeatability, explainability, and constitutional fidelity are preserved may a specific constitutional execution be said to have a lawful resolved execution view.

These objectives bind every constitutional execution context resolution and every successor machinery that claims to resolve constitutional execution context.

---

## 2. Primary Objective

### CECRO-01 — Minimise ambiguity

**Definition.** Ensure that every material resolution establishes — for a named execution identity — which available constitutional artefacts are selected, which are excluded, which dependencies are assembled, and which consistency outcomes apply, so the resolved execution context presents an unambiguous constitutional execution view.

**Includes:**

- Binding every material resolution to an identifiable consumable MS001 Execution Context and named execution identity.
- Applying the closed CECR-01…CECR-06 catalogue required by published law for the concern.
- Making selection and exclusion reconstructable — never silent omission of available artefacts.
- Preferring lawful incomplete / refused resolution over inventing unpublished artefacts or soft-merging contradictions so execution can “start anyway.”
- Keeping ambiguity minimisation distinct from execution, independent interpretation, decision, educational quality, mastery, readiness, or constitutional amendment.

**Excludes:**

- Treating filled JSON, ticket fields, CI greens, Twin estimates, Adaptive scores, or UI ticks as an unambiguous resolved view.
- Soft-amending specifications, inventing unpublished customs, or privileging a preferred stack to force “ambiguity cleared.”
- Optimising for throughput, latency, or demo continuity as a substitute for constitutional clarity.
- Declaring resolution “close enough” without documenting unresolved gaps.

**Tutor rationale.** Professional exam preparation fails when software pretends an execution may begin against an unclear desk. Ambiguity honesty is care; resolution theatre is harm.

---

## 3. Supporting Objectives

### CECRO-02 — Preserve determinism

**Definition.** Ensure that the same consumable Execution Context, the same execution identity and resolution scope, and the same published selection / dependency / consistency rules yield the same resolved execution context (or the same honest refuse disposition) — so resolution is not operator-dependent, race-dependent, or silently mutable mid-run.

**Tutor rationale.** A coach who selects different syllabus papers for the same sitting depending on which corridor they walked through first cannot be trusted.

**Manifestations:**

- Same published inputs ⇒ same CECR stage outcomes and same resolved view (or same refuse).
- Selection rules cite published identities, not private reviewer habit.
- Ad hoc stage insertion, silent re-selection, or private “fast path” resolution are defects.
- Determinism never authorises inventing unpublished stages or artefacts to force agreement.

---

### CECRO-03 — Preserve repeatability

**Definition.** Ensure that every material resolved execution context is reproducible from reconstructable inputs — Execution Context identity, selected and excluded artefact identities, assembled dependencies, consistency-check outcomes, and preserved boundaries — so resolution history can be reconstructed without ambient theatre.

**Tutor rationale.** A coach who cannot reconstruct yesterday’s selected desk cannot stand behind today’s work. Non-repeatable resolution is constitutional amnesia.

**Manifestations:**

- Resolution cites corpus paths and artefact identities, not only ticket IDs or commit SHAs.
- Continuity (EIP-005) forbids erasing prior lawful resolution history to simplify the next start.
- Opaque “ambient” resolution without reconstructable inputs is a defect.
- Same published inputs → same assembled resolved view (or same honest incomplete / refused disposition).

---

### CECRO-04 — Preserve explainability

**Definition.** Ensure that every material resolution can answer which constitutional artefacts were selected, which were excluded, which dependencies were assembled, which consistency checks were performed, and which constitutional boundaries remained intact — without rewriting constitutional meaning.

**Tutor rationale.** A coach who cannot say *which papers* were chosen for *this* sitting — and which were left aside — is performing, not teaching.

**Manifestations:**

- Resolution narration obeys [`RESOLUTION_EXPLAINABILITY.md`](RESOLUTION_EXPLAINABILITY.md) (CECREQ-01…CECREQ-05).
- Students, developers, and auditors receive truthful speech appropriate to audience vocabulary — not different truths.
- Missing explanation for a material selection, exclusion, dependency, check, or boundary is a defect.
- Explanation never pretends that selecting artefacts equals executing rules or producing decisions.

---

### CECRO-05 — Preserve constitutional fidelity

**Definition.** Ensure that resolution selects and assembles published constitutional information *exactly as published* — preserving authority, meaning, constraints, and MS001 component integrity — so the resolved execution context never soft-amends law, invents meaning, or replaces constitutional authority.

**Tutor rationale.** A coach who “clarifies” the syllabus by rewriting it in the selection step has abandoned educational law.

**Manifestations:**

- Selected artefacts retain published identities and meaning; clarifying notes do not rewrite them.
- Exclusions never erase inconvenient published obligations from existence — they only bound *this* execution view.
- Assembled dependencies cite published relationships; they never invent new legal couplings.
- Consistency validation refuses contradictions; it never rewrites law to manufacture consistency.
- MS001 CECX components remain defined solely by MS001; resolution consumes them and never redefines them.
- No stack, vendor, or reference implementation becomes constitutional by proximity to a resolved view.

---

## 4. Objective Composition

Material constitutional execution context resolution must serve **all** of CECRO-01…CECRO-05. No objective may be traded away for delivery urgency.

| If this is sacrificed… | …the failure mode is |
|------------------------|----------------------|
| CECRO-01 Ambiguity | Execution inherits an unclear or silently curated constitutional picture |
| CECRO-02 Determinism | Equivalent inputs yield operator-dependent resolved views |
| CECRO-03 Repeatability | Resolution becomes theatre — unreproducible and unaccountable |
| CECRO-04 Explainability | Students / auditors cannot reconstruct what was selected or excluded |
| CECRO-05 Fidelity | Soft amendment, invented meaning, or authority substitution via resolution |

> **Objectives optimise the integrity of selection and assembly.  
> They never authorise constitutional execution, independent interpretation, or decision inside resolution.**

## 6. Relationship to MS001 Context Objectives

| MS001 objective | Resolution relationship |
|-----------------|-------------------------|
| CECXO-01 Scope | Resolution *consumes* established context scope and *bounds* the resolved view for a specific execution (CECR-02) |
| CECXO-02 Consistency | Resolution *validates* consistency of the resolved view (CECR-05); it does not replace context-level consistency |
| CECXO-03 Neutrality | Resolution inherits and preserves implementation neutrality (CECRO-05 / boundaries) |
| CECXO-04 Explainability | Resolution adds selection / exclusion / dependency / check speech (CECREQ) without replacing CECXEQ |
| CECXO-05 Repeatability | Resolution extends repeatability to the resolved view (CECRO-03) |

Compose; do not conflate. MS001 objectives protect the *available* environment. CECRO objectives protect *resolution* of that environment.
