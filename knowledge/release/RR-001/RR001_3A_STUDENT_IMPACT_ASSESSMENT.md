## 1. Student problem

Students could not answer *who is speaking* on the educational journey: onboarding taught **Kwalitec** as the tutor; later memory surfaces spoke as **Study Sensei**; Home often left the mentor unnamed; explanations said “tip”; Runtime C said “the system.” Mission, Session, and tip competed as names for today’s focus.

**Evidence:** EGC-001 NCR-001/014/015/018/020; RP-001 ED-01/ED-02; Authority Transition Map AC-01/T04.

---

## 2. Student benefit

| Design question | Helped? | How |
|-----------------|---------|-----|
| What should I do now? | Yes | Mission vs Session clarified; same authorised focus, clearer nouns |
| How am I progressing? | Indirect | Continuity copy uses Mission, not tip slang |
| What is stopping me? | N/A | Identity only |
| What happens next? | Yes | Handoff sets expectation that Sensei owns daily guidance after onboarding |

**Student benefit summary:** One mentor relationship begins on purpose; educational speech stops pretending the product brand or “the system” is the tutor.

**Final Test:** Does this help students become better professionals? **Yes (trust foundation)** — professionals need a clear guide relationship; this does not by itself deepen mastery evidence.
