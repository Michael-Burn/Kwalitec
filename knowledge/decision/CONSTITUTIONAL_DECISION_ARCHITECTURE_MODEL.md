## 1. Purpose

Kwalitec coaches students preparing for demanding professional examinations (especially IFoA syllabi such as CM1/CS1 and peers).

An expert IFoA tutor does not mark a script and then invent a new syllabus rule to justify the grade, reinterpret interpretation principles because the outcome was inconvenient, treat the mark sheet as a new constitution, or claim that finishing the mark is the same as owning the syllabus. After the Constitution and EIP have defined *educational law*, after Programmes VI–IX have published *meaning, orchestration, runtime, and governance law*, after Workstream 1 has *prepared* the constitutional execution context, and after Workstream 2 has *executed* published rules into *completed execution outcomes*, the platform still needs one decision answer:

> **“How are completed constitutional execution outcomes transformed into constitutional decisions?”**

That answer must ensure the Decision Architecture consumes **completed execution outcomes**; applies **published constitutional decision rules**; preserves **constitutional authority** and **implementation neutrality**; and produces **constitutional decisions** for downstream audit and explainability — while remaining fully deterministic, repeatable, explainable, and constitutionally faithful — without executing constitutional rules, reinterpreting EIP, amending constitutional specifications, replacing constitutional authority, determining governance outside constitutional authority, or becoming constitutional authority.

This document records that posture so every future constitutional decision activity (and successor) has a single constitutional reference for *how completed execution outcomes may become decisions without becoming law, a second execution engine, or an independent governance producer*.

> **The Constitutional Decision Architecture Model describes how completed execution outcomes are transformed into constitutional decisions.  
> It does not execute constitutional rules, reinterpret Educational Interpretation Principles, amend constitutional specifications, or replace constitutional authority.**

## 2. What the Constitutional Decision Architecture Is

The **Constitutional Decision Architecture** is the binding **constitutional mechanism** by which **completed execution outcomes** may be **transformed into constitutional decisions** — consuming those outcomes, evaluating published constitutional decision rules, constructing and validating decisions, managing decision state, and publishing decisions for audit and explainability — while preserving constitutional authority, implementation neutrality, determinism, repeatability, explainability, and constitutional fidelity — without becoming a source of constitutional law, a substitute for EIP, a re-execution engine, or an independent governance producer outside constitutional authority.

| Concept | Definition | Primary question |
|---------|------------|------------------|
| **Completed execution outcome** | A reconstructable WS2 execution result whose required execution lifecycle has lawfully completed and whose outcomes have been published | Which completed outcomes may be consumed? |
| **Constitutional decision rule** | A published rule that maps completed execution outcomes (and named authority) to lawful decision dispositions — never a licence to re-execute constitutional rules | Which decision rules may be applied? |
| **Constitutional decision** | Reconstructable disposition produced under published decision rules for downstream audit and explainability — never new law, never EIP replacement, never stack freeze | What decision was produced? |
| **Decision validation** | Confirmation that a constructed decision cites completed outcomes, published decision rules, and constitutional authority without boundary violation | Is the decision constitutionally valid? |
| **Decision state** | Speakable progress of decision production under CDA-06 (not educational state, not independent governance disposition) | Where is decision production in its lawful course? |
| **Constitutional authority** | The exclusive right of published corpora to define educational and execution meaning | Who owns the standard? |
| **Implementation neutrality** | Decision law does not privilege a stack, vendor, or topology | Remains the stack replaceable? |
| **Determinism** | Same completed outcomes + same published decision rules ⇒ same decision disposition | Is the result fixed by inputs? |
| **Repeatability** | Decision production is reproducible from reconstructable inputs | Can the decision be reproduced? |
| **Explainability** | Outcomes, decision rules, decisions, authority, and boundaries are speakable | Can the decision be narrated honestly? |
| **Constitutional fidelity** | Decision production applies decision rules exactly as published; it never soft-amends them | Did authority survive decision production? |

The Constitutional Decision Architecture is:

- **outcome-consuming** — it consumes completed execution outcomes; it never invents, rewrites, or re-executes them;
- **decision-rule-centred** — it applies published constitutional *decision* rules; it never authors them and never re-executes WS2 *execution* rules;
- **authority-preserving** — published corpora remain the sole source of constitutional meaning;
- **EIP-faithful** — Educational Interpretation Principles are referenced as published; they are never reinterpreted or replaced;
- **catalogue-closed** — only CDA-01…CDA-06 may be applied as constitutional decision architecture components;
- **validation-bound** — constructed decisions must survive constitutional validation before publication;
- **decision-honest** — outputs are constitutional decisions for audit / explainability, not constitutional amendments or stack freezes;
- **state-honest** — decision state describes Decision Architecture progress; it never invents educational state or independent governance outside authority;
- **implementation-neutral** — no particular language, framework, store, or CI tool is required or privileged by decision law;
- **deterministic** — identical completed outcomes and published decision rules yield the same decision disposition;
- **repeatable** — decision production is reproducible from reconstructable inputs;
- **explainable** — students, developers, and auditors can answer which outcomes, decision rules, decisions, authority, and boundaries applied;
- **non-executive** — decision production never performs constitutional rule execution;
- **non-legislative** — decision production never amends constitutional specifications;
- **non-substitutive** — decision machinery never becomes the constitutional standard for the next act;
- **non-independent-governance** — decision production never determines governance outside published constitutional authority.

The Constitutional Decision Architecture is **not**:

- a second Educational Constitution, EIP, or Programme VI / VII / VIII / IX amendment path;
- a licence to reinterpret coach meaning, ownership maps, tip warrants, runtime contracts, or Programme IX judgements in “decision” prose;
- a Runtime A workflow runner, scheduler, Python interpreter, Flask request cycle, or CI job;
- a substitute for WS1 context / resolution / completion — those corpora prepare context; CDA never prepares context;
- a substitute for WS2 execution engine / lifecycle / completion — those corpora execute rules and complete execution; CDA consumes *completed outcomes* and never re-executes rules;
- a substitute for Programme VII educational recommendation / ownership decisions — those corpora define educational decision ownership; CDA defines constitutional decision production from execution outcomes;
- a substitute for Programme IX conformance, verification, compliance, certification, or evolution — those corpora produce governance judgements under their own catalogues; CDA may apply published decision rules that *reference* that authority and never becomes an independent producer outside it;
- a substitute for Programme II Decision Simulation — that framework measures advisory divergence; it is not constitutional Decision Architecture;
- a claim that “decision published” guarantees learning, mastery, exam readiness, product success, conformity, verification, compliance, certification, or frozen stack authority;
- a testing framework, CI/CD pipeline, GitHub Action, linter policy, or OpenAPI checker.

## 3. Decision Posture (Constitutional, Not Technical)

Every material constitutional decision travels through constitutional phases. These are **normative decision obligations**, not an implementation architecture.

```
Confirm completed execution outcomes (WS2 / MS003)
        │
        ├── incomplete / refused (lawful stop — decision production must not begin)
        │
        └── completed → Intake completed execution outcomes (CDA-01)
                │
                ▼
        Evaluate published constitutional decision rules (CDA-02)
                │
                ▼
        Construct constitutional decision (CDA-03)
                │
                ▼
        Validate constitutional decision (CDA-04)
                │
                ├── invalid / refused (lawful stop — do not publish as valid)
                │
                └── valid → Publish constitutional decision (CDA-05)
                        │
                        ├── manage decision state (CDA-06)
                        │
                        └── never execute constitutional rules, reinterpret EIP,
                            amend specifications, or replace constitutional authority
```

**Refuse is lawful.** Prefer honest stop over inventing unpublished decision customs, re-executing rules under a decision label, or minting independent governance so a demo can “decide anyway.”

## 4. Integrity Rules

1. **Completed outcomes only.** Decision production may begin only against WS2-completed published execution outcomes.
2. **Published decision rules only.** Unpublished customs and “how main works” are hard stops as decision-rule sources.
3. **Published components only.** Unpublished CDA customs are unlawful.
4. **Exactly as published.** Successful decision production never rewrites specification meaning.
5. **Authority first.** Published corpora remain the sole constitutional standard; the Decision Architecture never becomes one.
6. **EIP intact.** Educational Interpretation Principles are never reinterpreted or replaced by decision prose.
7. **No rule re-execution.** Applying decision rules is not performing WS2 constitutional rule execution.
8. **Implementation independence.** No stack, vendor, or reference implementation becomes law by proximity to a decision.
9. **Decisions ≠ legislation.** Constitutional decisions never amend constitutional specifications.
10. **Decisions ≠ independent governance.** Decision production never determines governance outside published constitutional authority.
11. **No authority substitution.** A published decision never replaces the corpus for the next act.
12. **No bypass.** Product urgency never waives required decision components or completed-outcome prerequisites.
13. **Determinism and repeatability.** Race-dependent or operator-dependent decision production is a defect.
14. **Explain the stop.** Students, developers, and auditors should hear when decision production must refuse.
15. **No emergency exemption.** Deadlines and demos never mint constitutional amendment power via the Decision Architecture.
16. **Validation before publication.** Invalid constructed decisions must not be published as constitutionally valid.
17. **Upstream honesty.** Upstream execution outcomes remain execution outcomes; decision production never rewrites them into new law.

## 6. Relationship to Upstream Execution and Downstream Consumers

Completed execution outcomes produced under WS2 (CEE-04 / CEE-07 and CEEC completion) are **inputs** to this Decision Architecture. The Decision Architecture may:

- read reconstructable completed execution outcomes;
- bind decisions to published constitutional authority already named in the completed execution context / outcomes;
- refuse to decide when outcomes are incomplete or boundary-violating.

The Decision Architecture must never:

- re-execute constitutional rules under a decision label;
- treat decision rules as a licence to reinterpret EIP;
- treat published decisions as constitutional amendments;
- elevate a preferred stack because a decision was published on it;
- invent independent governance dispositions outside published constitutional authority.

Downstream audit, explainability, and product surfaces may consume published constitutional decisions. They inherit no legislative, executive, or independent-governance power from proximity to a published decision.
