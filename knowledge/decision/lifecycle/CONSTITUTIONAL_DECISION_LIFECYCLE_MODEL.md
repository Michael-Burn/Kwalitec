An expert IFoA tutor does not finish marking a script and then invent a new syllabus rule to justify the grade, reinterpret interpretation principles because the outcome was inconvenient, treat the mark sheet as a new constitution, re-run the marking under a “decision” badge, or claim that finishing a decision pipeline is the same as owning the syllabus. After the Constitution and EIP have defined *educational law*, after Programmes VI–IX have published *meaning, orchestration, runtime, and governance law*, after Workstream 1 has *prepared* the constitutional execution context, after Workstream 2 has *executed* published rules into *completed execution outcomes*, and after WS3 / MS001 has defined *which decision components lawfully transform outcomes into decisions*, the platform still needs one lifecycle answer:

That answer must ensure the lifecycle consumes **completed execution outcomes**; coordinates **constitutional decision activities** under recognised CDAL-01…CDAL-07 stages; evaluates **published decision rules**; constructs, validates, and publishes **constitutional decisions**; and remains fully deterministic, repeatable, consistent, explainable, and constitutionally faithful — without executing constitutional rules, reinterpreting EIP, amending constitutional specifications, replacing constitutional authority, determining governance outside constitutional authority, or becoming constitutional authority.

This document records that posture so every future constitutional decision activity (and successor) has a single constitutional reference for *how decision work may be sequenced without becoming law, a second execution engine, or an independent governance producer*.

> **The Constitutional Decision Lifecycle Model describes how constitutional decision production is lawfully coordinated from initiation through publication.

## 2. What the Constitutional Decision Lifecycle Is

The **constitutional decision lifecycle** is the binding organisation by which **constitutional decision activities** may proceed through recognised stages — consuming completed execution outcomes, evaluating published constitutional decision rules, constructing and validating constitutional decisions under WS3 / MS001 component law, publishing those decisions for audit and explainability, and closing honestly — while preserving constitutional authority, implementation neutrality, determinism, repeatability, consistency, explainability, and constitutional fidelity — without becoming a source of constitutional law, a substitute for EIP, a re-execution engine, an independent governance producer outside constitutional authority, or a rewrite of decision components.

| Concept | Definition | Primary question |
|---------|------------|------------------|
| **Completed execution outcome** | A reconstructable WS2 execution result whose required execution lifecycle has lawfully completed and whose outcomes have been published | Which completed outcomes may the lifecycle consume? |
| **Constitutional decision rule** | A published rule that maps completed execution outcomes (and named authority) to lawful decision dispositions — never a licence to re-execute constitutional rules | Which decision rules may stages evaluate? |
| **Lifecycle stage** | A recognised CDAL-01…CDAL-07 organisational step | Which coordination step is active? |
| **Decision architecture component** | A recognised CDA-01…CDA-06 decision mechanism from WS3 / MS001 applied under lifecycle stages | Which decision machinery may stages invoke? |
| **Constitutional decision** | Reconstructable disposition produced under published decision rules for downstream audit and explainability — never new law, never EIP replacement, never stack freeze | What decision was produced? |
| **Decision validation** | Confirmation that a constructed decision cites completed outcomes, published decision rules, and constitutional authority without boundary violation | Which validations were performed? |
| **Constitutional authority** | The exclusive right of published corpora to define educational and execution meaning | Who owns the standard? |
| **Implementation neutrality** | Lifecycle coordination is judged against law and completed outcomes, not against a preferred stack, vendor, or topology | Remains the stack replaceable? |
| **Determinism** | Same completed outcomes + same published decision rules + same stage sequence ⇒ same decision disposition | Is the result fixed by inputs? |
| **Repeatability** | Decision organisation is reproducible from reconstructable inputs | Can the run be reproduced? |
| **Explainability** | Outcomes, decision rules, decisions, validations, stages, and boundaries are speakable | Can the run be narrated honestly? |
| **Constitutional fidelity** | Stages apply decision rules exactly as published; they never soft-amend them | Did authority survive coordination? |

The constitutional decision lifecycle is:

- **coordination-centred** — it organises constitutional decision activities; it never authors constitutional obligations;
- **outcome-consuming** — it consumes completed execution outcomes; it never invents, rewrites, or re-executes them;
- **architecture-faithful** — stages invoke only published CDA-01…CDA-06 components from WS3 / MS001; they never invent decision machinery;
- **catalogue-closed** — only CDAL-01…CDAL-07 may be applied as constitutional decision lifecycle stages;
- **decision-rule-centred** — it evaluates published constitutional *decision* rules; it never authors them and never re-executes WS2 *execution* rules;
- **validation-bound** — constructed decisions must survive constitutional validation before publication;
- **decision-honest** — outputs are constitutional decisions for audit / explainability, not constitutional amendments or stack freezes;
- **authority-preserving** — published corpora remain the sole source of constitutional meaning;
- **EIP-faithful** — Educational Interpretation Principles are referenced as published; they are never reinterpreted or replaced;
- **implementation-neutral** — no particular language, framework, store, or CI tool is required or privileged by lifecycle law;
- **deterministic** — identical completed outcomes, published decision rules, and stage sequences yield the same decision disposition;
- **repeatable** — organisation is reproducible from reconstructable inputs;
- **explainable** — students, developers, and auditors can answer which outcomes, decision rules, decisions, validations, and boundaries applied;
- **non-executive** — lifecycle progress never performs constitutional rule execution;
- **non-legislative** — lifecycle progress never amends constitutional specifications;
- **non-substitutive** — lifecycle machinery never becomes the constitutional standard for the next act;
- **non-independent-governance** — lifecycle progress never determines governance outside published constitutional authority;
- **non-mutating** — the lifecycle never modifies constitutional specifications or implementation artefacts under coordination pretext.

The constitutional decision lifecycle is **not**:

- a second Educational Constitution, EIP, or Programme VI / VII / VIII / IX amendment path;
- a licence to reinterpret coach meaning, ownership maps, tip warrants, runtime contracts, or Programme IX judgements in “lifecycle” prose;
- a Runtime A workflow runner, scheduler, Python interpreter, Flask request cycle, or CI job;
- a substitute for WS1 context / resolution / completion — those corpora prepare context; CDAL never prepares context;
- a substitute for WS2 execution engine / lifecycle / completion — those corpora execute rules and complete execution; CDAL consumes *completed outcomes* and never re-executes rules;
- a substitute for WS3 / MS001 decision architecture meaning — CDA defines components; CDAL sequences when those activities occur;
- a substitute for Programme VII educational recommendation / ownership decisions — those corpora define educational decision ownership; CDAL sequences constitutional decision production from execution outcomes;
- a substitute for Programme IX conformance, verification, compliance, certification, or evolution — those corpora produce governance judgements under their own catalogues; CDAL may apply published decision rules that *reference* that authority and never becomes an independent producer outside it;
- a substitute for Programme II Decision Simulation — that framework measures advisory divergence; it is not constitutional Decision Lifecycle;
- a claim that “decision published” or “lifecycle closed” guarantees learning, mastery, exam readiness, product success, conformity, verification, compliance, certification, or frozen stack authority;
- a testing framework, CI/CD pipeline, GitHub Action, linter policy, or OpenAPI checker.

## 3. Lifecycle Posture (Constitutional, Not Technical)

Every material constitutional decision lifecycle travels through seven constitutional stages. These are **normative coordination obligations**, not an implementation architecture and not a CI job graph.

```
CDAL-01 Decision Initiation
        │
        ▼
CDAL-02 Outcome Intake
        │
        ├── incomplete / refused (lawful stop — decision production must not proceed)
        │
        └── admitted →
                │
                ▼
CDAL-03 Decision Rule Evaluation
        │
        ▼
CDAL-04 Decision Construction
        │
        ▼
CDAL-05 Decision Validation
        │
        ├── invalid / refused (lawful stop — do not publish as valid)
        │
        └── valid →
                │
                ▼
CDAL-06 Decision Publication
        │
        ▼
CDAL-07 Decision Closure
        │
        ▼
Explain outcomes / decision rules / decisions / validations / boundaries
```

| Stage | Constitutional meaning | Hard stop if … |
|-------|------------------------|----------------|
| **CDAL-01 Initiation** | Open a decision concern against named completed-outcome identity and published scope | Scope is unpublished custom, tribal knowledge, or “how main works today” |
| **CDAL-02 Outcome intake** | Admit identifiable completed execution outcomes under CDA-01 | Outcomes are missing, invented, rewritten, incomplete, or would require re-execution |
| **CDAL-03 Decision rule evaluation** | Apply published decision rules under CDA-02 | Evaluation would invent law, rewrite specs, reinterpret EIP, re-execute rules, or privilege a stack as law |
| **CDAL-04 Decision construction** | Assemble a candidate constitutional decision under CDA-03 | Construction would become an amendment, EIP replacement, or independent governance outside authority |
| **CDAL-05 Decision validation** | Confirm the candidate is constitutionally valid under CDA-04 | Validation would be skipped, forged, or treated as new law |
| **CDAL-06 Decision publication** | Publish valid constitutional decisions under CDA-05 | Publication would invent validity, hide failed validation, rewrite boundaries, or freeze a stack as law |
| **CDAL-07 Closure** | Close the lifecycle honestly when duties are exhausted | Closure would execute rules, reinterpret EIP, replace constitutional authority, or pretend incomplete work is done |

> **The lifecycle never “best-effort invents” an eighth stage called law-making, reinterpretation, rule re-execution, authority substitution, stack canonisation, or artefact mutation.**

## 6. Educational Purpose

The Constitutional Decision Lifecycle Model exists so that:

1. **Law remains upstream of coordination** — stages organise decision production against published decision rules and completed outcomes; progress does not quietly become the specifications.
2. **Outcomes remain honest** — intake admits completed execution outcomes; it does not invent or re-execute them to force decisions.
3. **Decision rules remain published** — the lifecycle evaluates published decision rules; it does not invent unpublished customs so construction can proceed.
4. **Decision production remains sequenced** — CDA activities occur under CDAL stages after lawful initiation and outcome intake — not as random green theatre.
5. **Execution remains untouched** — lifecycle decisions never become a second execution engine.
6. **Validation remains required** — publication never skips constitutional validation.
7. **Authority remains honest** — no team may treat a closed lifecycle as the new constitutional source.
8. **EIP remains intact** — decision coordination never reinterprets or replaces Educational Interpretation Principles.
9. **Implementations remain replaceable** — lifecycle law does not freeze Flask, SQLAlchemy, a particular API shape, or a CI vendor as law.
10. **Determinism / repeatability remain honest** — the same published inputs and stage sequence yield the same disposition.
11. **Audit remains possible** — every material lifecycle can be reconstructed against named outcomes, decision rules, stages, validations, and decisions.
12. **Explainability remains intact** — lifecycle speech describes coordination of decision production; it does not redefine what learning means.
13. **Students remain protected** — product theatre cannot launder incomplete execution or unvalidated constructions as “lifecycle decided.”
